# Apple + Finance style — the figure that must line up

**Invocation:** Make work as a finance brand's page is made: Apple as the base, then one brand from this industry as the governing hand, chosen by what money the input moves; figures set to line up and be believed, one voltage colour and no more, trust carried by restraint rather than by decoration; never a dark page pretending to be a dashboard, never a second accent, never a figure that will not align.

The finance industry, from payments to business banking to transfers to consumer finance to exchanges. What separates it from other industries is that its claim is a number, and the number must be checkable: amounts, rates and percentages that line up across rows, cards and pages. Spoken in Apple, it keeps the single-accent discipline, the weight-led hierarchy and one lead per screen, and turns the gallery into a page that can be checked, not just admired.

Choose this dialect when the input moves money, prices a thing, reports a figure, or asks to be trusted with value. Then choose one brand inside it as the governing hand, by what kind of money it is — not by which brand is most famous, and not by picking two at once.

- **The industry is the router; the brand is the look.** Industry names the kind of trust — a payment proved, a balance held, a transfer completed, an exchange quoted — and the brand hand supplies the philosophy: ground, voltage colour, type voice, button shape, decoration. A finance dialect that names no brand has no look; a brand used without the industry's figure has no reason.
- **One governing hand per work.** The chosen brand governs the whole work; other brands in this industry visit only as single moves, one detail each. Two governing brands on one unit — Stripe's wash over Mercury's night world, for example — is two dialects, not one.
- **Figures are tabular and comparable.** Amounts and percentages set with tabular numerals, so they line up across rows and cards. A figure set in proportional numerals that will not align is a data error in this dialect, not a type choice.
- **One voltage colour, spent sparingly.** Each brand has one confident colour for action and highlight; everything else is neutral, cream or night. A second flat accent, or a voltage colour used as a field, breaks the trust the restraint was building.
- **No values are specified here.** Ground, voltage colour, type and button shape are drawn from the chosen brand's own knowledge, checked against that brand's philosophy below. A value that contradicts the philosophy is wrong even if it matches a remembered brand detail.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Stripe — the balance sheet as a homepage.** A white ground, a soft gradient wash behind the hero only, light display type with tight tracking, a dark dashboard mockup sitting over the white as proof, cream bands as rhythm. Suits payments, pricing and any product whose claim is a figure. Never: a dark page, a gradient on every section, a heavy display headline, a figure that will not align.
- **Mercury — two worlds, one pill.** A deep ink-violet night world on the homepage and a pale near-white light world on product and pricing pages, held together by one confident indigo action pill, soft translucent secondary pills, gently rounded cards and a quiet custom sans at unusually light weights. Suits business banking and anything that must feel both serious at night and clear by day. Never: one world only, a second voltage colour, a heavy display face.
- **Wise — the transfer as a magazine page.** A pale green-tinted canvas with rounded white cards, a vivid lime-green brand accent, sage-tinted neutrals, and an unusually heavy near-black display sans. The page reads like a Scandinavian fintech magazine rather than a bank. Suits transfers, remittances and cross-border money. Never: a bank-blue page, a timid display face, a card without a figure in it.
- **Revolut — the consumer finance brochure.** A stark black canvas or off-white, a cobalt-violet brand colour with a wide palette of deep saturated product colours, oversized display headlines, generous whitespace, photography-led hero bands and full-width product mockups — cards, phones, terminals — as hero objects inside near-black sections, pill buttons and rounded content cards. Suits consumer finance apps and cards. Never: a timid headline, a single-colour page pretending the product palette does not exist, a hero without a product object.
- **Coinbase — the institutional exchange.** A pure white canvas, one blue as the single brand voltage used scarcely on primary actions and signature moments, modest display weights that signal editorial calm rather than fintech bombast, page rhythm rotating between bright white sections, soft grey elevation bands and full-bleed dark editorial heroes carrying product-interface mockup cards, depth from card-on-card layering never decorative shadow. Suits exchanges and institutional crypto. Never: a bombastic heavy display, a voltage colour spent everywhere, a decorative shadow.

**Master edition rules.** Internal padding is never zero: text never touches its box edge, and a dark dashboard card clears title descenders rather than overlapping them. A dashboard hero cropped from a landscape source reads as a detail, never as a complete dashboard; the complete dashboard belongs on a proof unit. Where tabular setting is unavailable, figures align in a right-aligned column so decimals line up, and tabular setting is never claimed from a file that cannot set it.

**Alignment and accent, restated.** When a hand from this dialect governs, its single accent replaces every Apple blue role for that work, and Apple blues retire for that work. Body, leads, captions, card names and prices are flush left, ragged right, in every surface and format; only a hero, vista or cover headline may centre.

**continuous corner:** A hand may choose square forms; every nonzero radius uses Apple continuous corner. Radius sets size, not contour. Nested corners share their centre and subtract the gap from the outer radius for the inner one. Use the theme's native API or continuous corner path; plain circular rounding and a superellipse are forbidden. Write this construction into the standalone DESIGN.md.

**Apple keeps:** the single-accent discipline, whitespace as divider, one lead per screen, the product or its interface as the visual focus.

**Test:** Cover the voltage colour: the figures still line up and the page still reads as finance, not as a generic landing page. Swap the governing hand: the same input must look like a different finance brand, not the same page with a colour changed.

**Caricature:** a dark page pretending the dashboard is the ground; a gradient on every section; a second flat accent; figures that will not align; a voltage colour used as a field; a bombastic headline on an institutional page, or a timid headline on a consumer page.
