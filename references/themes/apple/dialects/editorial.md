# Apple + Editorial style — the long read and the vista

**Invocation:** Apple + Editorial — Make the long read as an environment story is made: light leads, a vista where a product tile would be, the argument carried by atmosphere and fact together; never a product tile stretched into an essay, or a vista as wallpaper.

The apple.com environment pages and long-form stories. The field opens: the tile becomes a vista, the body a lead, and the pace slows. A surface, not an industry: it governs structure, and the dialect's hand governs look.

- **The lead leads, at 300.** Leads 24 px / 300 / 1.5; reading text 17 / 400 / 1.47.
- **A vista, of three kinds.** A photograph; where the subject has none, the dialect's proof — an interface, a dashboard, a material — at full bleed; or a section or cutaway readable as a diagram. Each takes one headline and at most one action.
- **One column, generous air.** About 980 px, centred, its text flush left; 64 px above a headline, 48–64 below; only the footer goes dense.
- **Facts stay checkable.** A claim the vista makes is stated beside it, its source in a caption.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **The environment hand — the vista page.** Open on the vista, then let long leads carry the argument in scenes, one idea each. Suits stories, reports and commitments. Never: a vista behind a wall of body text.
- **The gallery hand — the product pages.** One product tile inside the story, grounding an abstract claim in an object. Suits stories about a product. Never: a tile that repeats the vista's claim.
- **The keynote hand — the stage.** One sentence per scene at display size, so the story can also be presented. Suits stories told aloud. Never: a scene read twice.

**Apple keeps:** the single accent for links and the vista's one action; the Display cut for headlines, the Text cut for leads and body; the parchment close; the centred column.

**Test:** Remove the vista: the leads still make the argument, in order. Remove the text: the vista alone still names one subject, not a mood.

**Caricature:** a stock vista under a slogan; body at 300, too light to read; three headlines on one vista; a footer as dense as the story.
