# Apple + Editorial style — the long read and the vista

**Invocation:** Make work as an environment story is made: light weights and long leads, a photographic vista where a product tile would be, the argument carried by atmosphere as much as by fact; never a product tile stretched into an essay, never a vista used as wallpaper.

The apple.com environment pages and long-form stories. Spoken in Apple, it keeps the single blue and the centred column, and lets the field open: the tile becomes a vista, the body becomes a lead, and the pace slows.

- **The lead leads, at weight 300.** Lead paragraphs at 24 px, weight 300, leading 1.5; reading text below them stays at 17 px, weight 400, leading 1.47. The light weight is an airy cue, used only at large sizes.
- **A vista, not a product shot.** A photographic field, full-bleed, with one headline and at most one action; the photograph's atmosphere is the argument, and the headline names it in one sentence.
- **One column, generous air.** About 980 px of centred column on the web; 64 px of air above a headline, 48–64 px below it; the footer alone goes dense, so the full information is visible at a glance.
- **Facts stay checkable.** A vista never replaces a figure: a claim the vista makes is stated in text beside it, with its source in a caption.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **The environment hand — the vista page.** Open with a photographic field, then let long leads carry the argument in scenes, each scene one idea. Suits stories, reports and commitments. Never: a vista behind a wall of body text.
- **The gallery hand — the product pages.** One product tile inside the story, to ground an abstract claim in an object. Suits stories about a product. Never: a product tile that repeats the vista's claim.
- **The keynote hand — the stage.** One sentence per scene, at display size, so the story can also be presented. Suits stories told aloud. Never: a scene the audience must read twice.

**Master edition rules.** Internal padding is never zero: text never touches its box edge. On the web an editorial unit is a vista opener plus a flush-left long-read column, with the column container centred and its text flush left — a centred container is never centred text. A vista is one of three kinds: photograph, proof field, or diagram; a diagram vista must be readable as a diagram, not a rendered explosion.

**Alignment and accent, restated.** A centred column is never centred text: leads and body stay flush left, ragged right, and only a vista headline may centre. When a hand governs, its single accent replaces every Apple blue role for that work. Body, leads, captions, card names and prices are flush left, ragged right, in every surface and format; only a hero, vista or cover headline may centre.

**continuous corner:** A hand may choose square forms; every nonzero radius uses Apple continuous corner. Radius sets size, not contour. Nested corners share their centre and subtract the gap from the outer radius for the inner one. Use the theme's native API or continuous corner path; plain circular rounding and a superellipse are forbidden. Write this construction into the standalone DESIGN.md.

**Apple keeps:** the single blue for links and the one action per vista, SF Pro Display for headlines and SF Pro Text for leads and body, the parchment close, the centred column.

**Test:** Remove the vista: the leads still make the argument, in order; remove the text: the vista alone still names one subject, not a mood.

**Caricature:** a stock vista under a slogan; weight 300 body text too light to read; a vista with three headlines; a footer as dense as the story; a green accent invented for the theme.
