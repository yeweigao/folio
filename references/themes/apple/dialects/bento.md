# Apple + Bento style — many features in one glance

**Invocation:** Apple + ‹brand› + Bento — Make the grid as Apple's feature grids are made: tiles sized by importance, one claim per tile, specifications as small posters; never a wall of equal cards, or a table in disguise.

The community's name for the grid Apple popularised after 2022: a layout method that visits any brand or surface for a unit with many features. It governs that unit's structure, the brand its look.

- **One tile, one claim, one picture, sized by importance.** Every tile carries one visual — an interface crop, a number at poster scale, a cropped object, a construction; a tile of text alone is a slide. The lead takes the wide tile and holds the hero itself, at the largest scale on the page; supporting features, small tiles; a specification, a number tile — display number, small muted label, one quiet line. A tile that needs a legend or a second claim is a slide.
- **Specifications are posters, not rows,** numbers at one size across tiles so the grid scans by number alone.
- **An index, not proof.** Each tile's claim is proved in a following unit, or the tile carries its checkable figure.
- **Locally dense, globally loose.** A tight grid in generous air; homepage promo tiles sharp in a white gutter, feature cards inside a page on the radius ladder.
- **Showroom takes it on range units only:** one object and one price per tile.

**Goes wrong as:** equal cards with equal claims; an icon, a title and two grey lines in every tile; a rainbow tile; a number with no label; a mosaic read like a spreadsheet.

**Test:** Cover the numbers: the tiles still read as features, in order of importance. Cover the photographs: claims and numbers still compare without a table.
