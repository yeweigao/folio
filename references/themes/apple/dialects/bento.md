# Apple + Bento style — the grid of many features on one page

**Invocation:** Make work as a grid is made: many features on one page, tiles of different sizes, one claim per tile, the wide tile carries the lead and the small tiles carry the numbers; never a wall of equal cards, never a table in disguise.

The apple.com homepage and the feature galleries. Spoken in Apple, it keeps the single blue and the type ramp, and lets density rise without breaking the gallery: the page becomes a grid, the tile becomes a card of a size that matches its claim, and the whole range is read in one glance.

- **One tile, one claim, sized by importance.** The lead feature takes the wide or full-width tile; a supporting feature takes a small tile; a specification takes a number tile — one huge number at display size, its label beneath in small muted type, one quiet line of context, no more. A tile that needs a legend, a footnote or a second claim is not a bento tile, it is a slide.
- **Specs are posters, not rows.** A specification never sits in a table row inside the grid. Each spec is its own small poster: number, label, and at most one small image, so the grid can be scanned by number alone.
- **The grid rhythm is the homepage rhythm.** Full-width heroes and two-column tiles, light and dark alternating across the page, separated by a white gutter; a product overview page, by contrast, may stay on one ground — black — through its whole sequence, and forcing it to alternate per section breaks it. Alternation belongs to the grid surface, not to every surface.
- **Colour is one per tile, and only as an echo.** A tile may carry one colour drawn from its own product or photograph, and a headline may colour only its core word, never the whole headline and never in rainbow. On the product heroes themselves headlines stay white and colour is carried by the photograph; the coloured headline is a homepage-tile exception, not the rule.
- **Locally dense, globally loose.** Tiles sit close and the page stays loose: the grid is tight, the air around the grid is generous. Density is judged by whether each tile still holds one claim at a glance, never by a percentage of white space — a percentage counted on one page is that page's count, not a system rule.
- **Rounding follows the surface, not the tile.** Homepage promo tiles are sharp and full-bleed, separated by gutter; gallery and feature cards inside a page are rounded on the system ladder, and images sit inside those rounded containers rather than as sharp full-bleed pictures.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **The homepage hand — the promo grid.** Stack full-width heroes over two-column tiles, alternate the ground, and let the grid itself be the argument that the range is one family. Suits landings, ranges and overviews. Never: a grid where every tile shouts at the same size.
- **The gallery hand — the feature gallery.** Equal cards in a scrolling row, with a wide card after the small ones for the feature that deserves more room. Suits feature walkthroughs and highlights. Never: a gallery that must be read in order to be understood.
- **The data hand — the number tile.** One number at display size, label beneath, context in one quiet line, so a specification can be compared across tiles without a table. Suits specs, comparisons and proof. Never: a number without a label, or a label without a number.

**Master edition rules.** Internal padding is never zero inside tiles: tile text never touches a tile edge. On the web a bento unit is a main image first, then a multi-tile index, with numbers at equal size across tiles. A bento unit is an index, never proof: each tile's claim is provable in a following unit, or the tile itself carries its checkable number, label or figure. An image reused at a new crop across tiles is declared as reuse, not hidden.

**Alignment and accent, restated.** Tile text stays flush left inside its tile; a centred container is never centred text. When a hand governs, its single accent replaces every Apple blue role for that work. Body, leads, captions, card names and prices are flush left, ragged right, in every surface and format; only a hero, vista or cover headline may centre.

**continuous corner:** A hand may choose square forms; every nonzero radius uses Apple continuous corner. Radius sets size, not contour. Nested corners share their centre and subtract the gap from the outer radius for the inner one. Use the theme's native API or continuous corner path; plain circular rounding and a superellipse are forbidden. Write this construction into the standalone DESIGN.md.

**Apple keeps:** the single blue for links and actions, the type ramp with weight doing the hierarchy, the parchment and near-black grounds, the single product shadow on the render inside a card, the radius ladder.

**Test:** Cover the numbers: the tiles still read as features, in order of importance. Cover the photographs: each tile's claim and number still let a reader compare without a table.

**Caricature:** equal cards with equal claims; a rainbow tile; a table disguised as a grid; a gradient wallpaper behind the tiles; a KPI tile with no lead and no label; a dense mosaic that must be read like a spreadsheet.
