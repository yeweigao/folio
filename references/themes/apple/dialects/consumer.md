# Apple + Consumer style — the object in a life

**Invocation:** Make work as a consumer brand's page is made: Apple as the base, then one brand from this industry as the governing hand, chosen by what life the input belongs to; the object or the place is shown in use, photography leads, colour comes from the life rather than from chrome, and the page stays friendly without becoming playful decoration; never a spec sheet pretending to be a life, never chrome competing with the photograph, never a colour with no life behind it.

The consumer industry, from wellness wearables to sport commerce to travel marketplaces. What separates it from other industries is that its proof is a life, not a tool or a figure: a ring worn daily, a shoe in motion, a home stayed in. Spoken in Apple, it keeps the single-accent discipline, the product as sculpture and one lead per screen, and turns the gallery into a page where the reader can picture the object already in their life.

Choose this dialect when the input is a wearable, a sport product, a marketplace, a home, or any subject whose claim is a life lived with it. Then choose one brand inside it as the governing hand, by what life it is — wellness, sport, or travel — not by which brand is most famous, and not by picking two at once.

- **The industry is the router; the brand is the look.** Industry names the kind of proof — a daily rhythm, a kinetic image, a place photographed generously — and the brand hand supplies the philosophy: ground, accent, type voice, button shape, chrome. A consumer dialect that names no brand has no look; a brand used without the industry's life has no reason.
- **One governing hand per work.** The chosen brand governs the whole work; other brands in this industry visit only as single moves, one detail each. Two governing brands on one unit — Oura's warm paper under Nike's towering uppercase, for example — is two dialects, not one.
- **Photography leads; chrome stays retail-quiet.** Photographs of the object in life carry the page; commerce chrome — search, tags, cards, purchase bars — stays neutral, quiet and consistent, so the life is not interrupted by the shop. A page where chrome shouts louder than the photograph has left the consumer dialect.
- **Colour comes from the life or the product, never from chrome.** Earth and clay from a wellness life, sale red and success green only as pricing signals, one marketplace voltage for actions and ratings. A chromatic moment reserved for editorial photography or a pricing signal is not decoration; a chromatic chrome element is.
- **No values are specified here.** Ground, accent, type and button shape are drawn from the chosen brand's own knowledge, checked against that brand's philosophy below. A value that contradicts the philosophy is wrong even if it matches a remembered brand detail.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Oura — the warm paper wellness.** A warm-paper system pairing a high-contrast editorial serif with a quiet grotesque sans, wrapped in a cream-and-earth palette drawn from mustard, clay and olive, photography-led layouts, conversion driven by a single decisive action blue against otherwise calm surfaces. Suits wearables, wellness and daily rhythms. Never: a cool blue page, a sans-only display, a spec sheet pretending to be a life, a second action colour.
- **Nike — the kinetic commerce.** A photography-first commerce system built on extreme typographic contrast — towering uppercase display lockups burned into editorial campaign imagery — above a dense, neutral, near-monochrome retail chrome of pill-shaped black calls to action, grey search and tag pills, and tight product cards. Pure black, pure white, one soft surface grey, and a deliberately small set of semantic accents for sale and success. Suits sport, commerce and kinetic campaigns. Never: a quiet headline, a chromatic chrome element, a product card without a photograph, a sale colour used as decoration.
- **Airbnb — the generous marketplace.** A warm, generous marketplace on a clean white canvas, one rose-red voltage carrying every primary action, search orb and rating dot, modest-weight display type that trusts photography and generous whitespace over typographic muscle, pill-shaped search bars, softly rounded property cards, no hard corner anywhere except the body grid. Suits marketplaces, travel and homes. Never: a heavy display face, a hard-cornered card system, a second voltage colour, a page that trusts type more than photography.

**Master edition rules.** Internal padding is never zero: text never touches its box edge. Cover text sits in a calm zone of the photograph with transparent fill, never over solid blocks that cover descenders. Title boxes are taller than their type. Serif display is a substitute, never embedded, so spacing is buffered against substitution shifting line breaks.

**Alignment and accent, restated.** When a hand from this dialect governs, its single accent replaces every Apple blue role for that work, and Apple blues retire for that work. Body, leads, captions, card names and prices are flush left, ragged right, in every surface and format; only a hero, vista or cover headline may centre.

**continuous corner:** A hand may choose square forms; every nonzero radius uses Apple continuous corner. Radius sets size, not contour. Nested corners share their centre and subtract the gap from the outer radius for the inner one. Use the theme's native API or continuous corner path; plain circular rounding and a superellipse are forbidden. Write this construction into the standalone DESIGN.md.

**Apple keeps:** the single-accent discipline, the product as sculpture, one lead per screen, whitespace as divider.

**Test:** Cover the brand name: the life — wellness rhythm, kinetic sport, or generous travel — still reads from photograph and type alone. Swap the governing hand: the same object must look like a different consumer brand, not the same page with a colour changed.

**Caricature:** a spec sheet pretending to be a life; chrome shouting louder than the photograph; a chromatic chrome element; a second voltage colour; a quiet headline on a Nike page, or a heavy display face on an Airbnb page.
