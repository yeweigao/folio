# Apple + Keynote style — the presented deck

**Invocation:** Apple + ‹brand› + Keynote — Make the deck as an Apple launch is presented: one thesis per slide, live text over a calm zone, one dominant image; never a web tile shrunk onto a slide, or text baked into a picture.

A surface, not a brand: it governs structure, the brand governs look. Read with the slides surface, projected.

- **One thesis, one slide.** The title states the claim; the body proves or shows it. A slide that needs two heroes is two slides. One number per slide, unless comparison is the point: then a figure set on one baseline, the decisive figure in the accent.
- **Text stays live.** The image keeps 25–35% of its frame calmer than the hero area for the text; nothing critical in the outer 8%.
- **A slide is read in a glance.** No tables: specifications become one figure set on a baseline, or the handout.
- **One dominant subject per slide:** a cover, divider, concept, comparison, data, workflow or closing poster.
- **Cuts between slides;** a change of ground at a turn; builds only order attention.
- **Read across a room:** on 1600 × 900, a cover title 120 px, a slide title 90, body 33, caption 23, fine print 20; a display number 150 or more, label beneath, the group mid-frame. Every size is a floor; another frame scales them by its short side.

**Goes wrong as:** a webpage screenshot on a slide; a 44 pt title that reads as a document heading; bullet walls; every cover in the same half-frame structure; a transition on every slide; a chart that needs a legend.

**Test:** Projected, every slide reads from the back of the room in one glance; without its image, each title still states one claim.
