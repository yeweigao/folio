# Apple + Showroom style — the object, the message, the price

**Invocation:** Make work as a showroom page is made: Apple as the base, then one brand from this industry as the governing hand, chosen by what object the input shows; the photograph proves the claim, one message per screen, and buying is part of looking rather than a step after it; never a card grid pretending to be a showroom, never chrome competing with the photograph, never a price hidden until the end.

The showroom industry, from cars to rockets to drones and cameras. What separates it from other industries is that its proof is a physical object at full scale: a car, a rocket, a drone, photographed or rendered so its silhouette and material can be judged. Spoken in Apple, it keeps the single-accent discipline, the product as sculpture and one lead per screen, and turns the gallery into a room where the visitor walks from object to object.

Choose this dialect when the input is a physical object, a range, a showroom, a configurator, or any subject whose claim is carried by a photograph and a price. Then choose one brand inside it as the governing hand, by what object it is — not by which brand is most famous, and not by picking two at once.

- **The industry is the router; the brand is the look.** Industry names the kind of proof — a full-viewport photograph, a video hero, a tiled studio render — and the brand hand supplies the philosophy: ground, accent, type voice, button shape, chrome. A showroom dialect that names no brand has no look; a brand used without the industry's object has no reason.
- **One governing hand per work.** The chosen brand governs the whole work; other brands in this industry visit only as single moves, one detail each. Two governing brands on one unit — Tesla's configurator over SpaceX's mission hero, for example — is two dialects, not one.
- **The photograph carries the page.** The object is shown at the largest scale the screen allows, its silhouette intact, its material readable. Chrome is quiet, frosted or ghosted, and never competes with the photograph. A page where cards, badges and headlines crowd the object has left the showroom.
- **Price is a permanent element, or a deliberate absence.** Where the brand sells, price and configurator stay visible while the photograph changes; where the brand inspires, the absence of price is itself the statement, and no checkout chrome sneaks in. A price revealed only at the end, on a selling page, borrows Apple's reveal and loses the showroom's honesty.
- **No values are specified here.** Ground, accent, type and button shape are drawn from the chosen brand's own knowledge, checked against that brand's philosophy below. A value that contradicts the philosophy is wrong even if it matches a remembered brand detail.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Tesla — the showroom with the price left in.** One full-viewport photograph and one message per screen, normal tracking as an explicit break with Apple, only two weights with the heavier one restrained, small square buttons never pill, one electric blue for the primary action only, price and configurator always visible, no gradient, no shadow, no border, chrome reduced to a frosted nav. Suits cars, ranges and configurators. Never: a pill button, tight tracking, a heavy headline, a gradient wash, a price hidden until checkout, three messages stacked on one screen.
- **SpaceX — the mission as austerity.** A pure black canvas, full-bleed photographic and video heroes of rockets and landscapes, uppercase display type in tight vertical leading, chrome reduced to a single ghost outlined pill per band, all-caps eyebrow microtext, a fixed nav over photography, black and white and the imagery itself. Suits space, missions and anything whose claim is scale and austerity. Never: a coloured accent, a filled pill, a card grid, a headline that competes with the photograph.
- **DJI — the studio showroom in tiles.** Full-bleed studio renders in square, gutter-separated tiles on a white canvas, every action a rounded pill in a single engineering blue, tightly tracked type in near-black ink at stepped opacities, small tier labels in a product colour marking rank, store layers of option cards, a sticky purchase bar and pale panels on the same restrained system. Suits drones, cameras and any range sold by tile. Never: a full-viewport single hero pretending the range is one object, a second action colour, a tile without a tier or a price near it.

**Master edition rules.** Internal padding is never zero: text never touches its box edge. A cover claim sits in a calm light zone of the photograph and is shortened until it reads over light ground, never over a dark bumper or windshield. Price stays visible at every step. Cover structures vary across a set: a set that repeats one cover structure, right-half hero after right-half hero, is a weakness, not a system; full-bleed covers and right-half covers both belong in a set.

**Alignment and accent, restated.** When a hand from this dialect governs, its single accent replaces every Apple blue role for that work, and Apple blues retire for that work. Body, leads, captions, card names and prices are flush left, ragged right, in every surface and format; only a hero, vista or cover headline may centre.

**continuous corner:** A hand may choose square forms; every nonzero radius uses Apple continuous corner. Radius sets size, not contour. Nested corners share their centre and subtract the gap from the outer radius for the inner one. Use the theme's native API or continuous corner path; plain circular rounding and a superellipse are forbidden. Write this construction into the standalone DESIGN.md.

**Apple keeps:** the single-accent discipline, the product as sculpture, one lead per screen, empty space — here, empty photograph — as divider.

**Test:** Cover the accent: the photograph, the message and the price (or its deliberate absence) still read, in that order. Swap the governing hand: the same object must look like a different showroom, not the same page with a colour changed.

**Caricature:** a card grid pretending to be a showroom; chrome competing with the photograph; a pill button in a Tesla work, or a filled pill in a SpaceX work; a gradient wash; a shadowed card; a price hidden until the end on a selling page; three messages stacked on one screen.
