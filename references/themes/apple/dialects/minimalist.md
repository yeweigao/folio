# Apple + Minimalist style — nothing unnecessary

**Invocation:** Make work as a minimalist product's page is made: Apple as the base, then one brand from this school as the governing hand, chosen by what kind of subtraction the input claims; remove everything that does not change how the object is used, let material and function carry the meaning, and let quiet be the brand; never decoration, never a logo shouting, never a feature list pretending to be a product.

The minimalist school, from industrial design to no-brand retail to minimal audio. What separates it from other schools is that its claim is subtraction itself: the product is good because nothing unnecessary remains. Spoken in Apple, it keeps the single-accent discipline, the product as sculpture and one lead per screen, and turns the gallery even quieter: Apple already subtracts, minimalist design subtracts the brand as well.

Choose this dialect when the input is an everyday product, a home object, stationery, audio, or any subject whose claim is that nothing unnecessary remains. Then choose one brand inside it as the governing hand, by what kind of subtraction it is — functional system, no-brand, or material audio — not by which brand is most famous, and not by picking two at once. Where the input's claim is a mechanism or a performance figure, that is Engineering, not Minimalist; where it is craft or heritage, that is Luxury, not Minimalist; where it is a tool's workflow, that is Developer, not Minimalist.

- **The school is the router; the brand is the look.** Minimalism names the kind of proof — an object reduced to function and material — and the brand hand supplies the philosophy: ground, neutrality, type voice, button shape, chrome. A minimalist dialect that names no brand has no look; a brand used without the school's subtraction has no reason.
- **One governing hand per work.** The chosen brand governs the whole work; other brands in this school visit only as single moves, one detail each. Two governing brands on one unit — Braun's system under MUJI's no-brand, for example — is two dialects, not one.
- **Subtraction is judged by use, not by emptiness.** Remove everything that does not change how the object is held, seen, heard or used, and stop there. An empty page that is hard to use has mistaken emptiness for minimalism; a page that keeps one necessary control has understood it.
- **Material is the colour.** Neutral grounds, natural materials, aluminium, wood, paper and plain packaging carry the page; colour, where it appears at all, comes from the material itself, never from chrome. A decorative colour has already broken the school.
- **The brand mark is quiet or absent.** A mark, where it appears, is small, quiet or absent altogether; the object, not the logo, is the identity. A loud logo on a minimalist page contradicts the claim the page is making.
- **Type is a quiet grotesque, and nothing else.** One neutral sans, regular weights, generous leading, no display face competing with the object. A display serif or a condensed headline imports another school's voice.
- **No values are specified here.** Ground, neutrality, type and button shape are drawn from the chosen brand's own knowledge, checked against that brand's philosophy below. A value that contradicts the philosophy is wrong even if it matches a remembered brand detail.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Braun — less, but better.** Dieter Rams's ten principles made into products: functional, honest, unobtrusive, long-lasting, thorough to the last detail, environmentally friendly, and as little design as possible. Neutral systems, modular parts that combine without fitting, radios and record players as systems rather than objects. Suits systems, appliances and any product whose claim is function. Never: a decorative detail, a feature that does not change use, a system whose parts do not combine.
- **MUJI — no-brand quality products.** Only what is necessary, nothing more and nothing less: plain packaging, natural materials, no logo shouting, no decoration, products that fit any life because they claim no life of their own. Suits everyday products, home, stationery and anything whose claim is quiet usefulness. Never: a loud mark, a decorative colour, a product that demands attention rather than serving quietly.
- **Bang & Olufsen — material minimal audio.** Minimal audio products where craftsmanship and material carry the design: aluminium, wood and textile as the palette, the product as sculpture in a quiet room, sound as the only performance claim. Suits audio, home tech and any product whose claim is material and sound. Never: a plastic chrome detail, a feature list pretending to be sound, a loud visual competing with listening.

**Visitors, single moves only.**
- **Aesop — apothecary minimal.** Typography and store as gallery, amber glass and plain labels, the shop itself as a quiet room. Never: a decorative colour or a loud mark.
- **Braun as visitor to Engineering.** The same Rams system, used for one move only: a modular part or a functional detail, never the whole governing look twice.

**Master edition rules.** Internal padding is never zero on any text shape: text never touches its box edge; a shape carrying no text, such as a hairline rule, needs no inset, and that exception is not a padding failure. A hero source is scaled uniformly, never stretched in one axis, and re-cropped if it clips the product; the full silhouette stays intact on cover and proof. An action bar contains its text with no empty space left below it.

**Alignment and accent, restated.** Where this dialect carries no chromatic accent, no accent is introduced and Apple blue does not appear as a decorative accent; where a hand does carry an accent, that accent replaces every Apple blue role for that work. Body, leads, captions, card names and prices are flush left, ragged right, in every surface and format; only a hero, vista or cover headline may centre.

**continuous corner:** A hand may choose square forms; every nonzero radius uses Apple continuous corner. Radius sets size, not contour. Nested corners share their centre and subtract the gap from the outer radius for the inner one. Use the theme's native API or continuous corner path; plain circular rounding and a superellipse are forbidden. Write this construction into the standalone DESIGN.md.

**Apple keeps:** the single-accent discipline, the product as sculpture, one lead per screen, whitespace as divider, reduction as method.

**Test:** Cover the brand name: the object still reads as functional and material, with nothing unnecessary left on the page. Add one element back: if the page does not get worse without it, that element should never have been there.

**Caricature:** an empty page that is hard to use; a loud logo on a quiet product; a decorative colour; a feature list pretending to be minimalism; a display face competing with the object; a product that demands attention rather than serving quietly.
