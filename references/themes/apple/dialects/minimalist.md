# Apple + Minimalist style — nothing unnecessary

**Invocation:** Apple + ‹brand› style — Make the page as ‹brand›'s is made: remove whatever does not change how the object is used, let material and function carry it, let quiet be the brand; never decoration, a loud mark, or a feature list posing as a product.

Industrial design, no-brand retail, minimal audio. The claim is subtraction: Apple already subtracts; this school subtracts the brand as well.

**What the industry overturns in Apple**
- **Subtraction is judged by use, not emptiness.** Stop at the last element that changes use; an empty page that is hard to use is not minimal.
- **Material is the colour.** Neutral grounds, aluminium, wood, paper; colour only from the material or one functional control. A brand with no accent gets none, and Apple blue does not decorate.
- **The mark is quiet or absent;** one quiet grotesque at regular weights.

**Brands.** One governs; the name carries its look, the signature anchors it.

- **Braun — less, but better.** Signature: Dieter Rams's ten principles; grey and white with one coloured control; modular parts that combine; the product photographed straight on, as a system. Goes wrong as: retro nostalgia; a feature that does not change use.
- **MUJI — no-brand quality.** Signature: unbleached kraft and white; plain sans labels; natural materials; one small dark-red mark. Goes wrong as: Japanese decoration; a loud mark; a product that demands attention.
- **Bang & Olufsen — material minimal audio.** Signature: aluminium, wood and textile; the product as sculpture in a quiet room; sound the only performance claim. Goes wrong as: plastic chrome; a feature list posing as sound.
- **Visitor:** Aesop, the shop as a quiet gallery of amber glass and plain labels.

**Apple keeps:** one lead per screen, the product as sculpture, reduction as method.

**Test:** Cover the brand name: the object still reads as functional and material. Add one element back: if the page is no worse without it, it never belonged.

**Caricature:** grey minimal skin — empty white, a thin sans, nothing to hold; a decorative colour; a loud logo on a quiet product.
