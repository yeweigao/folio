# Brief — Fern Reading Night: the line we cut

One launch slide for a reading evening about the sentences writers delete. The
event, the room and the passage are fictional and must say so.

## Facts

Every string below must appear in the deliverable exactly as written.

- `Fern Reading Night`
- `We read the line we cut.`
- `我们读那句被删掉的话`
- `A listening evening for the lines that did not survive the edit.`
- `Friday 4 December 2026`
- `19:30–21:00`
- `The Paper Room, 14 Willow Lane`
- `纸屋 · 柳巷 14 号`
- `Free, 30 seats`
- `免费入场，30 个座位`
- `Reserve a seat`
- `Illustrative example`
- `Illustrative render, not a photograph`

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1600 × 900 px. Google Fonts are
allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset. No Apple
product, photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

An audience at the back of a small hall, four to eight metres from the screen,
a reader at the microphone. In one glance: what the evening is, when, where.
Next: they take one of the seats.

## The idea

On this surface, *editing understood as removal* becomes *a programme made of
absences*: the audience has come to hear what a writing assistant helped take
out, so the only document shown is one with a single line struck through, and
the evening is argued by the space that line leaves rather than by any claim
about how well Fern writes.

## Sequence

1. Cover / statement — lead: the one-line claim, register: dark-surface turn
   (one navy product surface inside the cream field, with a complete foreground
   palette on that card).
2. The document surface stands in the calm field as the exhibit; event details
   and one action queue beneath the title on a single left axis.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The document on the right is an illustrative Fern interface, declared in the
  caption; it is a stand-in, never a photograph or a real product screen.
- The struck-through line is the checkable thing: real readable text, not an
  abstract placeholder bar.
- The event, the room, the passage and the seat count are fictional;
  `Illustrative example` states this on the slide.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
