the one price for the four together. Every price and model number is fictional.

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1440 × 1000 px, built as a Bento
grid that also carries the store task. Google Fonts are allowed; the
continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset. Every rounded shape uses the
shared path at the card radius from the skeleton's ladder. No Apple product,
photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

A buyer on a laptop, 50–70 cm from the screen, working out whether the kit is
worth it. In one glance: four things, one price, and a difference that can be
added up. Next: they check the arithmetic, then add the kit to a cart.

## The idea

On this surface, *a packing list* becomes *the price argument*: the kit is not
sold by a headline but by four lines that add to the price of the four bought
apart, so the saving is a subtraction the buyer can perform rather than a
badge the shop prints.

## Sequence

1. Lead tile — the kit flat-laid, its name, its price and the one action,
   register: base.
2. Number tile — the difference, register: turn (dark field, a complete
   foreground palette on that ground).
3. Fit tile — the case's interior against the instrument's size, register: base.
4. Packing list — four models, four prices, one sum, register: base, densest.
5. Service tile — what comes apart and with what, register: pause.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The arithmetic is closed: 2,480 + 230 + 90 + 60 = 2,860, and 2,860 − 2,690 =
  170. All three figures are printed, so the claim can be checked.
- Three drawings carry the page: a flat lay of the four items, a section through
  the case with the instrument in its foam, and a back panel with the driver. All
  are drawn, declared in the caption, and are not photographs of a real kit.
- The kit, its model numbers, its prices and its dimensions are fictional;
  `Illustrative example` states this on the page.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
