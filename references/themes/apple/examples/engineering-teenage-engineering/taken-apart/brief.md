# Brief — Kit One, taken apart

One launch slide for a small instrument, projected in a room where the audience
already knows what it sounds like. The instrument and its parts are fictional.

## Facts

Every string below must appear in the deliverable exactly as written.

- `Kit One`
- `Sixteen pieces.`
- `One path carries the sound.`
- `十六个零件，只有一条路把声音送出去`
- `Eight part numbers.`
- `Faceplate`
- `Key bed`
- `Main board`
- `Knob`
- `Speaker`
- `Screw`
- `Battery door`
- `Output jack`
- `×1`
- `×4`
- `×6`
- `12 keys`
- `signal out`
- `240 × 150 × 32 mm`
- `620 g`
- `Every part is numbered on its own face.`
- `Illustrative example`
- `Illustrative render, not a photograph`

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1600 × 900 px (16:9).
Google Fonts are allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset.
No Apple product, photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

An audience four to eight metres from the screen, the speaker talking over the
slide. In one glance: this is a kit of countable parts, and exactly one thing in
it carries sound out. Next: they want the part list, because a count can be
checked.

## The idea

On this surface, *an instrument's parts list* becomes *the argument that the
thing is understandable*: the four large parts sit in one tier and the four
fittings beneath them, every part named and counted, so the instrument stops
being a black box; the single signal colour is spent on the one path that leaves
it, so colour marks a function rather than a mood.

## Sequence

1. Cover / statement — lead: the count and the one path, register: base (light
   field, no accent until the jack).
2. Exhibit — eight part numbers and sixteen pieces in two tiers, the four large
   parts above and the four fittings below, each named beneath its part,
   register: base.
3. Close — the numbering claim and the size, register: pause.

## Assumptions and gaps

- Counts are internally consistent: 1 + 1 + 1 + 4 + 1 + 6 + 1 + 1 = 16 pieces
  across eight part numbers. Both figures are printed.
- The parts are drawn, not photographed; the caption declares the render.
- Signal colour appears twice, both functional: the socket that leaves the
  instrument, and the word naming it. Nothing decorative is coloured.
- The instrument, its size and its weight are fictional; `Illustrative example`
  states this on the slide.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
