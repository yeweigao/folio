# Brief — the states on Kit One's panel

One editorial figure for a small instrument: the control panel read as a state
legend, followed by the same four states printed without colour.

## Facts

Every string below must appear in the deliverable exactly as written.

- `Kit One`
- `One colour carries four states.`
- `Nothing else on the panel is coloured.`
- `一种颜色承担四种状态，面板上别的地方不上色`
- `States`
- `Live`
- `Armed`
- `Waiting`
- `Off`
- `sounding now`
- `queued for the next bar`
- `in the pattern, not reached`
- `not in the pattern`
- `12 keys`
- `4 knobs`
- `No state is carried by colour alone.`
- `The panel at one moment. 12 keys, 4 knobs, four states, one colour.`
- `Four keys, one grey ink. The states still separate, because they were never separated by colour.`
- `With the colour removed, the four states still read.`
- `The colour marks four states. Everything else on the panel is an edge.`
- `Illustrative example`
- `Illustrative render, not a photograph`

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1200 × 1600 px. Google Fonts are
allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset. The page is a
long read: a figure with its legend, a lead, body copy, then a second figure
that repeats the first without colour. No Apple product, photograph, logo or
font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

A reader at 40–50 cm, in the manual or the long-form story, deciding whether
they understand the instrument before they play it. In one glance: the panel,
four states, and which is which. Next: they can read a key's state on the
instrument and explain why the light is drawn that way.

## The idea

On this surface, *the panel's legend* becomes *an argument about colour*: one
signal colour is asked to carry four different states, so the state has to be
built into the shape — filled, outlined, dashed, empty — and the figure can be
reprinted in grey, which is the proof that colour was never doing the work.

## Sequence

1. Cover / opener — lead: the claim that one colour carries four states,
   register: base.
2. Exhibit — the panel at one moment, with the four states marked on the keys
   and named in a legend beside it, register: base.
3. Argument — the lead and body: the colour is spent on state, and the forms
   do the carrying, register: base, lighter.
4. Proof — the same four states printed in grey, register: pause.
5. Close — the claim the figures proved, register: base.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The panel is one moment of a twelve-key pattern, not a specification of the
  instrument's behaviour; the distribution of the four states is illustrative.
- `12 keys` and `4 knobs` match the drawing, and the exploded view of the same
  instrument elsewhere.
- The grey strip is drawn from the same geometry as the colour figure at a
  smaller size; it is a second figure, not a reused image.
- The instrument is fictional; the caption and the footer declare the render
  and the example.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
