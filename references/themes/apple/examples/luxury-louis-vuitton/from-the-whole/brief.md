# Brief — From the whole bag to the grain

One Editorial opener and proof page for a fictional leather house: the same bag
read at three distances, each with its own scale bar and one dimension. The
house, the bag, the measurements and the price are fictional and must say so.

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1200 × 1600 px. Google Fonts are
allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset and the three
plates use its 18 px path as a clip. Serif display, sans reading, the world's
five grounds only; no chromatic accent, no Apple blue, no house mark or borrowed
photograph. Reading text, captions and the price stay flush left.

## Reader and outcome

A reader who has already met the bag, on a phone or a laptop, scrolling one page
in a quiet minute. In one glance: that the page will not show more of the bag
but less of it, in three measured steps. Next: they read the numbers on the
plates and trust the seam they cannot touch.

## The idea

On this surface, *the distance a photograph is taken from* becomes *the argument
for the price*: a bag can be admired at arm's length and still be unexplained,
so the page descends from the object to the pitch and the paint, and each step
carries a scale bar so the reader can check that nothing was swapped for
something prettier between the three.

## Sequence

1. Vista — the bag standing on the bench with the spool beside it, one headline
   and one action over the calm wall, register: base (the only cinematic field).
2. Lead — `A bag is three distances at once`, register: pause, reading pace.
3. Argument — one paragraph naming the three scales and the two thicknesses.
4. Exhibit — three plates in one row: the whole bag at 1:2, the seam at 8:1, the
   edge at 24:1, each with a scale bar, register: the page's densest moment.
5. Close — one judgement, one quiet price, the disclaimers, register: quiet.

## Assumptions and gaps

- All three plates are drawn stand-ins, declared once under the vista and once
  in the brief; the leather gradients and the stitches are drawn, never a
  photograph. `Illustrative render, not a photograph` sits directly under the
  vista, above the reading column, so no plate is mistaken for evidence.
- The scales are honest ratios: 10 cm reads 50 px, 10 mm reads 80 px, 1 mm reads
  24 px, and the pitch in the seam plate is 3.17 mm × 8 = 25.4 px, so eleven
  stitches fit the 300 px panel as the text says.
- The seam plate repeats the pitch already given in the seam note slide; it is a
  second use of one world constant, not a second proof of it.
- Ply 3.2 mm, paint 0.6 mm and pitch 3.17 mm are fictional but consistent across
  this world's three pieces.
- No second accent: the painted edge is a leather tone, the action is ink.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting, and the
  three plate captions sit in HTML below the images, never inside a drawing.
