## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1200 × 1600 px: a full-bleed vista
over a centred reading column, text flush left inside it. Google Fonts are
allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset. No Apple
product, photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

A reader alone at a laptop, 50–70 cm from the screen, arriving from the ring's
report. In three seconds: one night, one line, one subject. Next: they read the
column and understand that the two waking blocks, not the total, describe the
night.

## The idea

On this surface, *a night's sleep* becomes *a strip of time that can be read in
sequence*: the night is not summarised but laid end to end at a stated scale, so
the reader follows the same 478 minutes the ring recorded and sees where the
line left the sleep rows, instead of being told how the night felt.

## Sequence

1. Opener / vista — lead: one night as a continuous band, register: dark turn
   (a whole near-black field, one headline, no action, one callout).
2. Argument — the reading column, register: pause (light field, flush-left lead
   and body, one checkable table of the two waking blocks).
3. Close — source and the illustrative declaration, register: quiet.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The band is drawn at 1.86 px per minute across 478 minutes, so its length can
  be checked against the clock labels; the scale is stated in the body.
- The vista is a proof field, not a photograph; the caption declares it.
- The two waking blocks are counted in the foot of the table and repeated in
  the body, so the 46 minutes can be verified from the drawing alone.
- Nothing here is a medical reading: no score, no recommendation and no
  comparison with other nights appear.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
