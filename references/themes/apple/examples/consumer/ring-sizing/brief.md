## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1440 × 1000 px. Google Fonts are
allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset; every rounded
tile and chip uses it. No Apple product, photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

A buyer on a laptop, 50–70 cm from the screen, who knows their ring size on
paper but not in millimetres. In one glance: eight sizes with real measurements,
three finishes and one price. Next: they find their row, check the diameter
against the drawing, and add one ring to the bag.

## The idea

On this surface, *a ring size* becomes *a millimetre that can be checked against
a drawing*: a size is not a label but a diameter, so the page puts the eight
rows of the published chart beside a ring drawn at a stated diameter and lets
the buyer match their own measurement instead of guessing between neighbours.

## Sequence

1. Index / lead — lead: eight sizes and three finishes named at once, register:
   base (warm paper field, one claim per tile, tiles sized by importance).
2. Exhibit — the ring from above with its inner diameter dimension, register:
   base, quieter; the drawing is declared illustrative.
3. Reference — the size chart with the chosen size held in the chip row,
   register: base, densest unit on the page.
4. Collection — the three finishes, register: base, quiet.
5. Close — the running price and one action, register: pause.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The size table reproduces the published US ring chart in millimetres; a real
  shop would print its own measuring instructions beside it.
- The ring drawing is an illustrative stand-in at the size-9 diameter (18.9 mm)
  and is declared in the caption, never a photograph of a product.
- Finishes are three tints of this world's palette, not a manufacturer's range;
  the price is fictional and stated once.
- No rating, no stock badge and no discount appear; the page has one action.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
