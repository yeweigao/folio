# Brief — The night, in four stages

One sleep-report slide for a fictional wellness ring, read after a night rather
than at a launch. The night, its figures and the app export are invented.

- `The ring separates awake, light, deep and REM. This is one night, printed as it was recorded, with nothing smoothed.`

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1600 × 900 px. Google Fonts are
allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset. No Apple
product, photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

Someone who slept badly, at a laptop, 50–70 cm from the screen, opening the
report the ring wrote overnight. In one glance: how long they slept and which
stage was scarce. Next: they read the four stages and see that the total hides
a thin deep-sleep block, then decide whether to change the evening.

## The idea

On this surface, *a night's sleep* becomes *a quantity that has to be audited*:
the claim is not that the night was good or bad but that it divides into four
stages which must add back up to the time in bed, so the slide spends its width
on the division and lets the reader do the arithmetic against the same scale.

## Sequence

1. Statement — lead: the deep-sleep figure against the night's total, register:
   base (warm paper field, serif headline, one accent on the scarce stage).
2. Argument — the four stage bars on one shared 478-minute scale, with the total
   and the subtraction stated in one quiet line, register: base, quieter.
3. Close — source and the illustrative declaration, register: pause.

## Assumptions and gaps

- Every bar is drawn at 1 px ≈ 2.3 minutes on the same 478-minute scale; the
  scale is stated in the source line so the drawing can be checked.
- The night is a single illustrative record, not a series and not an average;
  no comparison with other nights is claimed.
- No price, no product render and no recommendation appear: the ring's own
  photograph would compete with the figure.
- `Illustrative example` and `Illustrative render, not a photograph` state the
  fictional status on the slide.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
