# Brief — Aero, the filter range

One index page: three filter grades for one housing, compared by what they hold
and how often they are replaced, with the price kept quiet. The product, the
grades and every price are fictional and must say so.

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1440 × 1000 px, laid out on the
Bento grid: one lead tile carrying the range, then three tiles carrying the
mechanism, the interval and the fit. Google Fonts are allowed; the
continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset. No Apple product,
photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

A buyer at 50–70 cm from a laptop, already owning the fan, deciding which grade
to reorder. In one glance: three grades fit the one housing, and the intervals
differ more than the prices do. Next: they pick a grade and check the interval.

## The idea

On this surface, *a filter grade* becomes *a schedule*: the three media are
drawn as the same frame at the same size, so the only visible difference is the
pleat, and the interval — not the price — is the number the reader is asked to
carry away.

## Sequence

1. Lead — one wide tile, three frames at one scale, each with its interval;
   register: base (light field).
2. Mechanism — the tile that shows the filter as the only obstruction in the
   path; register: base, quieter.
3. Turn — one dark tile carrying the interval as a number; register: dark turn.
4. Close — the fit tile and the declaration, register: quiet.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The Bento grid carries the comparison task: a Store page's job (grade,
  interval, price) is done by tiles rather than cards, because the subject is a
  range of consumables, not one product with options.
- All three drawings are illustrative and share one frame size and one scale;
  the caption declares them.
- Prices are fictional and deliberately set at body size, never larger than the
  filter name; `Illustrative example` states this on the page.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
