# Brief — three Mono models

One bento page for a shop: three sizes of the same radio at one scale, their
dimensions and prices in a row, and two posters for what does not change.

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1440 × 1000 px, built as a Bento
grid that carries the store's comparison task. Google Fonts are allowed; the
continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset. Every rounded shape uses the
shared path at the card radius from the skeleton's ladder. No Apple product,
photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

A buyer on a laptop, 50–70 cm from the screen, choosing between three sizes.
In one glance: which is which, how big each one is against the others, and what
it costs. Next: they pick a model and add it, knowing that the only difference
is the cabinet.

## The idea

On this surface, *a range page* becomes *a shared-scale measurement*: the three
radios are drawn at 1:1 on one baseline so the size difference is a fact of the
drawing rather than a caption, and the two posters beside them spend their
space on what does not change, so the comparison is honest about how little
there is to compare.

## Sequence

1. Lead tile — three radios on one baseline at one scale, register: base.
2. Number poster — the shared amplifier, register: turn (dark field).
3. Number poster — the shared dial at its real size, register: base.
4. Three model tiles — one per model, name, size, weight, price, one action,
   register: base, densest.
5. Close — the price note and the caption, register: pause.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The widths drawn are 180, 240 and 300 px for 180, 240 and 300 mm, so the
  drawing is 1:1 and can be measured on screen; the page says so.
- The dial is drawn at 60 px in every model because it is the same 60 mm dial;
  the poster repeats it at that size as a second use, not a second proof.
- The three thumbnails inside the order cards are the same elevations as the
  lead drawing at about 1:2.2; the reuse is declared here, not hidden.
- All three drawings are the same elevation at different sizes, declared in the
  caption.
- Models, dimensions, prices and the 4 W figure are fictional;
  `Illustrative example` states this on the page.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
