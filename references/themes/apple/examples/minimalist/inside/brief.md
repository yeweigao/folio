named inside the drawing, and the argument that the tuning is mechanical.

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1200 × 1600 px. Google Fonts are
allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset. The plate is a
cutaway with nine labels placed inside the cabinet, then a lead and body column
beneath it. No Apple product, photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

A reader at 40–50 cm, in a repair sheet or a long-form story, asking how the
radio can work with one control. In one glance: the parts, and the cord that
runs between them. Next: they can name what is inside and say why a second
control was never needed.

## The idea

On this surface, *a cutaway* becomes *the argument for subtraction*: the plate
keeps only the parts a function needs, and the one mechanism it shows — a cord
from the knob, around a pulley, to a pointer — is the reason the interface can
stop at a single control.

## Sequence

1. Cover / opener — lead: nine parts, one control, register: base.
2. Exhibit — the cutaway with nine labels inside it, register: base.
3. Argument — the lead and body under the plate, register: base, lighter.
4. Close — what the cutaway proves, register: pause.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The drawing is a back view with the panel removed at roughly 3.3 px to the
  millimetre; it is not a 1:1 plate, so no size is measured from it. The knob is
  dashed because it is on the far side, and the caption says so.
- The cord loop is drawn as two equal pulleys so both runs are horizontal; the
  real ratio is not shown, and `one ratio` is the only claim made about it.
- Part sizes are stated in the text, not measured from the drawing.
- The radio and its parts are fictional; `Illustrative example` states this on
  the page.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
