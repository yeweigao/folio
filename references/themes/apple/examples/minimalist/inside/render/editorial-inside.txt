The tuning is a length of cord, and a cord needs no second control.

Nine parts, and one of them is a control. The tuning is not a second circuit: a cord leaves the knob, turns a pulley, and clamps a pointer to a scale on the front.

That is why there is nothing else to set. A radio with a motorised tuner needs a memory, a display and a way to set them; a radio tuned by a cord needs a knob, and the hand supplies the memory.

What remains is what a function needs: a cabinet, a driver, a rod, a board, a pulley, a cord, a pointer, a knob and a socket. Nothing is in the case to fill an interval.

Take the cord away and the radio needs a display. Keep it, and the interface stops at one control.
