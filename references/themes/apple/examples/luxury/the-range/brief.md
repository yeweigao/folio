# Brief — The range

One range page for a fictional leather house, read in one glance as a Bento
grid of unequal tiles. The house, the objects, the prices and the figure are
fictional and must say so.

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1440 × 1000 px: a Bento range
grid. Google Fonts are allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local
sibling asset. The world's Tokens govern colour and face: white, cream and
neutral greys with one orange as the only accent, Cormorant Garamond for
display and a Chinese serif pair, Inter for labels. Every nonzero radius uses
the shared continuous-corner path (18 for tiles, 22 for the pill). No house
mark, monogram, pattern, product photograph or font file is borrowed.

## Reader and outcome

A buyer on a laptop, 50–70 cm from the screen, surveying a whole family of
objects. In one glance: that the series is one house's work, and what each
piece costs. Next: they enquire, or follow one object to its own page.

## The idea

On this surface, *a family of bags* becomes *one claim about a single hand*: a
house is judged by whether the same care survives from one object to the next,
so the range is read as evidence that consistency, not rarity, is what the
house sells — a single hero would prove one object, and the grid proves the
hand behind all of them.

## Sequence

1. Collection opener — lead: `The leather series`, register: base (a clean
   white field, one serif heading, one orange action).
2. Collection / Bento index — five tiles sized by importance, register:
   utility turn (a tight grid of softly rounded cream tiles on white gutters);
   one number poster, four object tiles, each carrying its own craft detail
   and a quiet price.
3. Close — the caption and source line, register: quiet.

## Assumptions and gaps

- Bento is an index, never proof: every object tile carries its checkable name
  and quiet price, and the number poster carries its own figure, label and
  context — no tile asks to be believed without them.
- Tile sizes differ by importance: one lead tile and four supporting tiles,
  not a wall of equal cards with equal claims.
- One orange action on the page; no second accent, no orange field or wash.
- Prices are quiet and never larger than the object names; no per-tile buy
  chrome competes with the objects.
- Every craft detail is a drawn stand-in, declared in the caption; the caption
  covers the whole grid rather than one tile.
- The house, objects, prices and the `9` figure are fictional; `Illustrative
  example` states this on the page.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting. Every
  tile carries real internal padding so no line touches a tile edge.
