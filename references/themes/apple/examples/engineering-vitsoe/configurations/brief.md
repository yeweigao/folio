# Brief — Shelf, three lengths

One index page: three configurations of the same system side by side, drawn to
one scale, each with its dimensions and its price. The system, its dimensions
and every price are fictional and must say so.

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1440 × 1000 px, on the Bento grid:
a lead tile for the longest wall, a second configuration, a third, a number
tile for the module and a tile for what the module buys. Google Fonts are
allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset. No Apple
product, photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

A buyer at 50–70 cm, comparing three walls for one room. In one glance: the
three are the same system at three lengths, drawn at one scale, and the price
difference is the count. Next: they check the dimensions against their wall and
pick one.

## The idea

On this surface, *a configuration* becomes *a count of one part*: all three are
drawn at the same scale on the same module, so the reader measures the
difference between the walls as extra bays rather than as a different product.

## Sequence

1. Lead — the longest wall, its drawing and its price; register: base.
2. Second — the middle configuration at the same scale; register: base,
   quieter.
3. Third — the single bay, with the module tile beside it; register: dark turn
   at the number.
4. Close — the slot-pitch tile and the declaration; register: quiet.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The Bento grid carries the comparison task; three tiles of different size
  rather than three equal cards, because the walls are the same system at
  different counts.
- Drawings share one scale (1:8), declared on the page; a tile too small for the
  longest wall would break the comparison, so the lead tile is the widest.
- Prices are fictional and set at body size, smaller than the configuration
  name; `Illustrative example` states this on the page.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
