# Brief — One storm, one figure

One Keynote data slide for the Highland weatherproofing record: a single
measured storm, the figure the cloth held, and the source a reader can check.
The house, the event, the place and every figure are fictional and must say so.

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1600 × 900 px (16:9).
Google Fonts are allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling
asset. Every rounded shape uses the shared continuous-corner path. No Apple
product, brand product, photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

An audience at the back of a small hall, four to eight metres from the screen,
with a speaker talking. In one glance: the one judgement, the one figure, and
that it was measured in weather. Next: they read the field log rather than
accept a claim about the cloth.

## The idea

On this surface, *one measured hour of rain over the Highland test field*
becomes *the coat's only argument*: the house sells a promise about weather,
and a promise about weather is worth nothing until a real storm produces a
figure with a unit and a source, so what the slide hands the reader is the
measurement, not the marketing of the garment.

## Sequence

1. Cover / data — lead: the judgement and the decisive figure, register: base
   (white canvas, one number in the house blue, everything else muted).
2. Exhibit / argument — the cloth section and the labelled field log, register:
   pause; the drawing is declared, the figures carry units and a source.
3. Close — the source line and one quiet action, register: quiet.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The decisive figure is `8,000 mm`, a fictional hydrostatic head for an
  illustrative study; the page says so with `Illustrative example`.
- The cloth cross-section and the small coat are illustrative stand-ins,
  declared in the caption, never evidence of a real material or object.
- The storm, the date, the test field and the field log are invented; no real
  weather record, place or house archive is quoted.
- No price, no second figure set and no comparison chart appear: one storm
  and one figure keep the slide to a single thesis.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
