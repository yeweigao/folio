## Reader and outcome

A reader choosing a finish, not a feature. The outcome: they see that the material is the whole decision — the cabinet and the price barely move — and they can name which of three surfaces they want without scrolling.

## The idea

On this surface, *three finish options* become *three crops of the same cabinet at the same scale*: each card shows the identical aluminium frame and the identical inset front, so the only thing that changes across the row is the material filling it, and the price sits at body size where it cannot compete with the surface.

## Sequence

1. Wordmark, two quiet links, one hairline. No banner, no promotion.
2. The claim in two lines, the Chinese line under it as the same claim.
3. One spec sentence at the right margin, so the dimensions are present before the reader compares anything.
4. Three cards, equal peers: a detail crop on top, at one scale across all three, then name, finish, price and one buy action.
5. The proof line under the row: the driver, the height and the weight are identical; only the surface differs.
6. Footer: the example declaration, and the render declaration for the crops.

## Assumptions and gaps

- Sound Column and the house are fictional; `¥12,800` for two surfaces and `¥14,600` for smoked oak are invented prices, set to show that the material, not the electronics, moves the number.
- The crops are drawn stand-ins, not photographs; each is a detail of the same drawing at the same scale, which is why the frame lines up across the row.
- Stock, delivery and finish availability are outside the page; the page makes one decision possible.
