## Reader and outcome

A reader who has looked at a hundred speaker photographs and learned nothing from them. The outcome: they understand one decision — that the front is one punched sheet rather than two joined panels — and they can see the evidence at a magnification where it can be checked.

## The idea

On this surface, *a manufacturing decision* becomes *a distance the reader can measure*: the story is told once in a vista where the column stands at one millimetre to one pixel, then again at ten to one, where the holes, the pitch and the metal between them are large enough to be counted rather than admired.

## Sequence

1. The vista: the column in an empty room, full-bleed, with the claim over the calm wall and one action. The product is drawn at 1:1 — 480 mm is 480 px — so the room is the reader's own scale.
2. The lead, in the reading column: one sheet, punched, nothing joined.
3. What a seam costs, in the body: the line where sound stops being one surface.
4. The proof, breaking the column to full width: a 10:1 macro of the perforated sheet with a 10 mm scale bar and the 3.2 mm pitch dimension drawn on it, then a caption that states the opening.
5. The closing body: what the sheet not moving means, and the one performance claim the house makes.
6. Footer: the example declaration and the note number.

## Assumptions and gaps

- Sound Column, the house and the figures are fictional: `4,500` holes, `3.2 mm` pitch, `1.1 mm` holes, `0.5 mm` sheet and `Nine per cent` open area are invented but internally consistent (a 96 × 480 mm sheet at a 3.2 mm pitch gives 4,500 positions; the pitch and hole size give the stated open area).
- The 10:1 macro is a drawn stand-in, not a photograph of a real grille; the scale bar is accurate to the drawing, not to a manufactured part.
- The vista is drawn at 1:1 for the dimension named; other dimensions in the drawing (the room, the table) are not to scale.
- `See the section ›` is a link with no destination in this static page.
