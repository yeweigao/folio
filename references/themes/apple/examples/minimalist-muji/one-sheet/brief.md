# Brief — The package is a measurement

One Keynote proof slide for a fictional everyday-goods house: the plain carton
of one everyday object, drawn unfolded at actual size, with its sheet and its
board stated. The house, the carton and every figure are fictional and must say
so.

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1600 × 900 px (16:9). Google Fonts
are allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset. Colour and
face come from the world's Tokens: warm canvas, white plate, ink and muted, one
grotesque at 400. Every corner stays square — this world's radius ladder is
zero, so no rounded shape and no accent appear anywhere. No logo, no borrowed
photograph, no decorative colour.

## Reader and outcome

An audience at the back of a small hall, four to eight metres from the screen,
being shown how a plain box is bought, folded and labelled. In one glance: that
the packaging is a measured sheet, and that nothing on it is printed. Next: they
read the six lines of specification, or they look at the net and see the box in
the sheet.

## The idea

On this surface, *the carton net* becomes *the only page the box has*: a plain
package carries no message, so the slide puts its whole argument in the
measurement — the cut and the creases at 1:1, three dimensions where they can be
checked with a ruler, and one small section of the board itself, because the
material is the other half of the specification.

## Sequence

1. Statement — `The package is a measurement.`, register: base (warm canvas,
   one grotesque, one left axis).
2. Exhibit — the net at 1:1 on the white plate, cut edges solid and folds
   dashed, three dimensions around it, register: the field turns from canvas to
   plate.
3. Material — one 24 px section of the board at 40:1 with its thickness, register:
   pause; the only tone in the piece is the board's own.
4. Reference — six lines of specification, one quiet closing line, register:
   quiet.

## Assumptions and gaps

- `1:1` is literal: 1 mm of the carton is 1 px of the drawing, so the 240, 160
  and 60 mm dimensions can be measured on screen at 100%. The board section is
  the one place the scale changes, and it is labelled 40:1.
- The dimensions are consistent: the net is 360 × 280 mm (240 + 2 × 60 by
  160 + 2 × 60) and fits the 420 × 297 mm sheet with a 30 mm margin, which is why
  the sheet size is stated.
- The net, the board section and the folds are drawn stand-ins, declared in the
  caption; they read as a technical drawing, never as a photograph.
- No price, no product and no second object appear: the packaging is the subject,
  and a price would make the slide a shop page.
- The house and the carton are fictional; `Illustrative example` states this.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting, and the
  plate note and caption sit in HTML, never inside the drawing.
