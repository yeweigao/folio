# Brief — Four materials, and no fifth

One Editorial page for a fictional everyday-goods house: a dustpan cut open at
the pan and the handle socket, with its four materials named and measured. The
house, the object, the section and the price are fictional and must say so.

## Facts

Every string below must appear in the deliverable exactly as written.

- `Plain`
- `Four materials, and no fifth.`
- `四种材料，没有第五种`
- `Read the material note`
- `Illustrative render, not a photograph`
- `A dustpan can be taken apart into four materials, and none of them is there to look like something else.`
- `一个撮箕可以拆成四种材料，没有一种是用来装成别的东西的`
- `One wall of polypropylene, 2 mm, undyed: no liner behind it. One beech handle, 22 mm, held in a socket by one screw. One cotton cord, 4 mm, through a drilled hole. One paper label, 40 × 20 mm, the only printed thing on the object.`

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1200 × 1600 px. Google Fonts are
allowed; the continuous-corner helper is a local sibling asset but no shape uses
it, because this world's ladder is zero and every edge here is square. One
grotesque at 400, warm canvas, a white plate, four material tones and no accent
colour. Reading text, the caption and the price stay flush left.

## Reader and outcome

A reader who already owns the object, on a phone or a laptop, in a quiet minute.
In one glance: that the object has four materials and no hidden fifth, and that
the page has cut it open to prove it. Next: they read the four dimensions on the
section and trust that nothing is glued, coated or laminated.

## The idea

On this surface, *a cheap object* becomes *a list of four honest decisions*: a
dustpan is the last thing anyone photographs, so the page cuts it open at the
one place where all four materials meet and lets their thicknesses — 2, 22, 4
and a sheet of paper — carry the argument that nothing is pretending to be
something else.

## Sequence

1. Vista — the dustpan hanging by its cord on a plain wall, one headline and one
   action over the empty wall, register: base.
2. Lead — `A dustpan can be taken apart into four materials`, register: pause.
3. Argument — one paragraph naming the four materials, their dimensions and how
   each is fixed.
4. Exhibit — the section at 4:1, four labels with leaders, two broken lines
   saying the drawing continues, register: the page's densest moment.
5. Close — one judgement and one quiet price, register: quiet.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The section is drawn at 4:1 and the ratios are honest: the 2 mm wall reads
  8 px, the 22 mm handle 88 px, the 4 mm cord 16 px, the 40 × 20 mm label
  160 × 80 px. The caption says it is a detail, and broken lines mark where the
  object continues.
- The object, the section and the label are drawn stand-ins, declared under the
  vista; `Illustrative render, not a photograph` sits above the reading column,
  so the plate is never mistaken for a photograph.
- Only four labels appear, one per material: the plate is a legend for the
  section, not a specification of the whole product.
- The price is fantasy and quiet, smaller than the closing sentence and never
  near the headline.
- No accent: the four materials are the only colour, and the plate is white on
  the warm canvas.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting, and every
  plate label is live text inside the SVG, never baked into a picture.
