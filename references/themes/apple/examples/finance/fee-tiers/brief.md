sales page. The shop, the rates and every figure are invented.

## Deliverable

One self-contained HTML file, one `.unit` of 1440 × 1000 px: a Bento grid of
unequal tiles on a hairline gutter. Google Fonts are allowed; the continuous
corner helper is a local sibling asset and every tile and the action use it. No
Apple product, photograph, logo or font file is used.

## Reader and outcome

A shop owner on a laptop, 50–70 cm from the screen, comparing what each rate
would have cost on the quarter just closed. In one glance: three rates, what
each one takes from the same ¥490,000, and the gap between them. Next: they read
the arithmetic of the rate their statement actually used, then add a shop.

## The idea

On this surface, *a fee schedule* becomes *one quarter priced three times*: the
tiers are not described but charged against the same 1,250 sales, so the buyer
compares outcomes on a shared scale instead of comparing percentages in the
abstract.

## Sequence

1. Index / lead — lead: three rates named at once with the cost of the spread,
   register: base (white field, one claim per tile, tiles sized by importance).
2. Comparison — the three tiers in one tile, names, rates, worked totals and
   bars on one scale, register: base, densest unit on the page.
3. Proof — the statement's own fee rebuilt from its two parts, register: base,
   quieter.
4. Close — one line and one action, register: pause.

## Assumptions and gaps

- The arithmetic is stated: 1.9%, 1.45% and 1.1% of ¥490,000 plus 1,250 charges
  at ¥0.30 or ¥0.20 give 9,685, 7,480 and 5,640; the spread is 4,045.
- Bars share one linear scale and every bar carries its amount in text, so
  no legend is needed.
- The tiles are unequal by importance, not a wall of equal cards: a wide lead, a
  single-number tile, a full-width comparison, a proof and one action.
- Rates, the shop and the quarter are fictional; `Illustrative example` states
  this on the page.
- Fonts load from Google Fonts; offline fallbacks change the setting.
