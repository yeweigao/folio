# Methods — Swiss

The masters' moves inside the [mother tongue](mother-tongue.md): each cell is a named way to make the reader do one [task](../../forms.md). Its parts are Gestalt's laws and Bertin's visual variables (position, size, value, color, shape, orientation, texture); its arrangement is Gerstner's morphological box. Craft files say how to do each right; breaking the grammar on purpose, or pushing a method until it breaks, is a [variation](variations.md).

- **Cells combine, not compete.** A strong unit often joins two or three cells from different columns into one image.
- **A move the tools cannot make well is swapped within its row, not faked.**
- **Methods take this grammar's form, not a generic one.** A leading line is a straight rule on the grid, never a wave; color blocking is solid, never translucent; a calligram keeps whole lines on the baseline grid; an oversized glyph is geometric, one per deliverable; a cut-out keeps a clean edge and the text stays legible.
- **Group by space, rules, or one field, never by a box.** A box is the default this table replaces.
- **A dialect redraws the cells it names.**

## Across units

- **A series is generated, not decorated one by one.** Declare one rule, a grid, a cycle, a permutation, and derive every unit from it, after Gerstner's *Designing Programmes* and Lohse's serial fields; the reader can almost reconstruct the rule.
