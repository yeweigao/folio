# Methods — Swiss

The masters' moves inside the [mother tongue](mother-tongue.md), read whole with it: this is how the theme draws each element. Each cell offers ways to make the reader do one [task](../../forms.md). Semicolons separate independent options, not a checklist; names are precedents, not required research. Its parts are Gestalt's laws and Bertin's visual variables (position, size, value, color, shape, orientation, texture); its arrangement is Gerstner's morphological box. Craft files say how to do each right; breaking the grammar on purpose, or pushing a method until it breaks, is a [variation](variations.md).

- **Cells combine, not compete.** Choose methods for each unit's job; a strong unit may join two or three cells from different columns, while reference units stay quiet.
- **A move the tools cannot make well is swapped within its row, not faked.**
- **Methods take this grammar's form, not a generic one.** A leading line uses straight segments on the grid, never a wave; color blocking is solid, never translucent; a calligram keeps whole lines on the baseline grid; an oversized glyph carries meaning; a cut-out keeps a clean edge and the text stays legible.
- **Group by space, rules, or one field, never by a box.** A box is the default this table replaces.
- **A dialect redraws the cells it names.**

## Across units

- **A systematic series is generated, not decorated one by one.** Where repetition serves the content, derive its units from a grid, cycle, or permutation, after Gerstner's *Designing Programmes* and Lohse's serial fields; a narrative may change method from unit to unit.
