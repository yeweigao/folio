# Variations

A variation breaks the active grammar, the mother tongue as the dialect has changed it, on purpose, so a change in the content is felt. A dialect's own moves are its grammar, never variations. Tactics inside the grammar are [methods](methods.md); a method pushed until it breaks the grammar becomes a variation.

- **A break answers the content, not a quota.** A turn, an emphasis, or a mood the subject asks for, on any unit; continuity is right while the reader's task stays the same.
- **One break per unit, not a pile.** Everything else on that unit keeps the active grammar.
- **A guest lends one move, not its system.** A variation may borrow a single signature from another dialect for one unit.
- **A variation repairs nothing.** Fix a weak composition first.
- **Reading text is never the experiment.** No rotation, overprint, or contrast below the floor for body text.
- **Borrow the system behind the lineage, not its surface.** Each entry names the default it breaks and where it comes from.
- **A break is named, not hidden.** Delivery lists the variation units and what each breaks.

### Breaking the type

| Variation | What it does | Where | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thin-mode turn | Hairline weights at giant size | Chapter openers, quiet statements | Small text keeps enough stroke | Frutiger's light Univers cuts, iOS 7's UltraLight |
| Mixed-type line | One display line changes size and weight inside a word or phrase | Culture posters, manifestos | Display only; one line per unit | Schwitters and Dada, Piet Zwart |
| Type filled with photograph | Letters clipped to an image | Covers, culture posters | The real title stays live text | Moholy-Nagy's typophoto |
| Vertical CJK | Chinese set top to bottom, right to left | Openers, quotations, culture | Short texts only | East Asian book tradition, Ikko Tanaka |

### Breaking the orthogonal grid

| Variation | What it does | Where | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rotation | One display element at ±6° or 90° | Posters, culture | Origin on a grid intersection | Lissitzky, Weingart |
| Radial field | Arcs or rings radiate from one point and carry the type | Music, launches, a single poster | One center; text set on the grid | Müller-Brockmann's concert posters |
| Symmetric axis | The unit is centered on one vertical axis | Ceremonies, invitations, inscriptions | Only once; everything else flush left | Classical inscription, late Tschichold |
| Split field | One hard line divides the unit into two contrasting fields | Before and after, either-or statements | The split carries the content's opposition | Constructivist posters, Lissitzky's wedge |

### Breaking the single photograph

| Variation | What it does | Where | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Photomontage | Two or three photographs at different scales compose one scene | Openers, campaigns | Declared as montage, never posed as a real scene | Heartfield, Klutsis, Herbert Matter |
| Macro crop | A photograph cropped until its texture becomes the field | Openers, material stories | The subject stays identifiable in a caption | Herbert Matter, Sachfotografie close-ups |
| Sequence strip | A row of equal frames shows time or movement | Process, events, sport | Frames in time order, one shared crop | Muybridge, Brodovitch's *Ballet* |
| Duotone | Greyscale photograph multiplied with the accent or signal | Image sets that need one voice | One duotone color per deliverable | Swiss tourism and concert posters |
| Cut-paper shape | One hand-cut organic shape among the geometry | Children's and cultural work, warm moments | One shape, colored from the palette | Matisse cut-outs, Paul Rand |

### Breaking the quiet field

| Variation | What it does | Where | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accent field | The whole unit on the accent; paper becomes the type | Chapter openers, statements, the ground of a single poster | Complete foreground roles; no other hue | Müller-Brockmann's Zurich posters |
| Type band | A hard band of dark or accent behind a line of display type | Statements, section labels | One band per unit | Swiss poster typography |
| Overprint / translucent field | Two inks multiply where they overlap; a translucent field can cover a photograph | Culture, music, posters | Keep reading text and evidence clear | Weingart, Greiman; Pieter Brattinga's *P.T.T.* poster |
| Line field | Parallel lines vary in weight or spacing until the field vibrates | Openers, music, technology | Never behind reading text | Op art (Riley, Vasarely), Gerstner |
| Programme field | A bounded generative field with a declared cycle | Openers, back covers, dividers | Cells carry no data | Gerstner, Lohse |

### Breaking the density

| Variation | What it does | Where | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pause | A near-empty unit with one small line or mark | Before a climax, between chapters | One per sequence | Kenya Hara, the Japanese *ma* |
| Text wall | A full unit of small real text becomes texture | Credits, rosters, names, timelines | Every item readable up close | Karel Martens, Experimental Jetset |
| Rhythmic repetition | One word or shape repeats in stepped scale, position, or weight | Openers, music, movement | One element, one direction of change | Müller-Brockmann's *musica viva*, Weingart |
