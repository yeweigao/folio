# ma 間 — the charged interval

**Invocation:** 原研哉 Kenya Hara — Make work as Hara made the MUJI Horizon campaign: one real photograph of the world, emptied until the small subject inside it becomes precious; never a rendered object, never Zen props.

Kenya Hara and the MUJI lineage, Tokyo: Ikko Tanaka's founding art direction, Hara's Horizon campaign (2003), *Designing Design*, *White*. Emptiness is not simplicity: simplicity removes in order to clarify, emptiness removes so that the viewer brings the meaning. Spoken in Swiss, ma turns the Swiss white from placed space into an open vessel.

- **Emptiness is a vessel, not spacing.** Content gathers so that one large interval stays whole; the interval frames the content and never replaces it.
- **One thing leads by isolation, not by scale.** Small, quiet type in a large field. Position, horizon and interval set the hierarchy; centering is allowed.
- **The subject is real, not drawn.** A photograph of the actual object, material, light or place; with none, the interval and the type alone. A rendered object is a costume.
- **Mincho and paper, not a black grotesque.** A Song or Mincho serif with a quiet sans; white or unbleached stock, soft ink, one small mark at most.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Kenya Hara — MUJI Horizon (2003).** Photograph the real horizon or the real thing on location, and let the photograph be the emptiness. Set the name small inside it, on the horizon line. The tools are a camera and a place. Suits brands and openings whose value is plainness. Never: a horizon drawn in code.
- **Kenya Hara — *White* (2008) and the paper works.** Choose the stock as the medium: its whiteness, temperature and weight. Then place a few lines of Mincho where the paper is most felt. Suits print, craft and ceremony. Never: type below the floors, because quiet is not small.
- **Kenya Hara — *Re-Design* (2000), exformation.** Take the everyday object the subject offers, and photograph or re-make it until it is seen again. Suits everyday things gone invisible by habit. Never: novelty for its own sake.
- **Ikko Tanaka — the *Nihon Buyo* poster (1981).** Abstract a traditional motif into bold flat geometry on the grid. Suits cultural subjects whose motif survives abstraction. Never: a motif pasted on as heritage.

**Swiss keeps:** the grid, exact placement on it, and the type floors.

**Test:** The emptiness changes how the subject is felt, and the subject rewards the attention.

**Caricature:** Zen props, stones, bamboo, brush ink; a computer-rendered bowl on beige; an empty page with nothing worth seeing; type too small to read.
