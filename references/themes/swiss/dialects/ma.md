# ma 間 — the charged interval

**Invocation:** 原研哉 Kenya Hara — Make work as Hara made *White* and the MUJI posters: emptiness as a vessel, the subject precious by isolation, paper with a temperature; never Zen props, never type below the floors.

Kenya Hara and the MUJI lineage, Tokyo: Ikko Tanaka's MUJI, Hara's Horizon campaign (2003), *Designing Design*, *White*. Emptiness, not simplicity: simplicity removes to clarify; emptiness removes so the viewer brings the meaning. Spoken in Swiss, ma turns the Swiss white from placed space into an open vessel.

- **Emptiness is a vessel, not spacing.** Content gathers so one large interval stays whole; it frames the content, never replaces it.
- **One thing leads by isolation, not by scale.** Small, quiet type in a large field; position, horizon, and interval set the hierarchy, and centering is allowed; the subject stays identifiable at viewing size.
- **The image evokes, not proves.** Material, light, and the ordinary seen again; never posed as evidence.
- **Mincho and paper, not a black grotesque.** A Song or Mincho serif with a quiet sans; white or unbleached stock, soft ink, one small mark at most.

**Hands in this school** — the DESIGN chooses one to govern; the others guest as moves, never as co-hands:
- **Ikko Tanaka — the Nihon Buyo poster (1981), MUJI's founding art direction.** Abstract a traditional motif into bold geometry; Japan as construction, not ornament. Suits cultural subjects whose motif survives abstraction. Never: a motif pasted on as heritage.
- **Kenya Hara — MUJI and White (2002–08).** Emptiness as a vessel; the subject small, the paper warm, the viewer completing the meaning. Suits subjects that gain by withholding. Never: type below the floors — quiet is not small.
- **Kenya Hara — the exformation works (Re-Design, 2000).** Make the familiar strange until it is seen again. Suits everyday subjects gone invisible by habit. Never: novelty for its own sake.

**Swiss keeps:** the grid, exact placement on it, and the type floors.

**Test:** The emptiness changes how the subject is felt, and the subject rewards the attention.

**Caricature:** Zen props, stones, bamboo, brush ink; an empty page with nothing worth seeing; type too small to read.
