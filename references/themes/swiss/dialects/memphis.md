# Memphis — the party on the grid

**Invocation:** 孟菲斯 Memphis Group, Ettore Sottsass — Make work as Sottsass made the 1981 collection: surfaces clad in drawn laminate pattern, primitives colliding at jaunty angles, hierarchy by exuberance; never good taste, never a pattern that carries information.

Milan, 1981–88: Sottsass, Du Pasquier, De Lucchi, Shire, with the Carlton bookcase and the Bacterio laminate. Spoken in Swiss, Memphis keeps the grid of good form and refuses its good taste.

- **Color clashes on purpose, not one accent.** Several saturated hues with black and white, chosen as relations.
- **Pattern is material, not ornament.** A laminate-like pattern clads a surface; never behind reading text, never posing as data.
- **Form plays; it does not only serve.** Primitives at odd proportions and off axis; delight is part of the function.
- **Contrary voices coexist; they do not resolve.** Type joins the clash in color, weight and register.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Ettore Sottsass — Bacterio (1978), the Carlton bookcase (1981).** Draw the pattern first, as a tileable laminate. Then cut the primitives and clad them with it, so the form becomes a symbol. In code, the pattern is a tiled SVG with a declared repeat. Suits subjects that refuse good taste on purpose. Never: pattern carrying information.
- **Alessandro Mendini — the Proust armchair (1978).** Take a classic of the subject and re-skin it, so the decoration criticizes the canon. Suits homages and anniversaries. Never: a classic quoted unchanged.
- **Nathalie Du Pasquier — the Memphis textiles (1981–87).** Draw bold geometric pattern fields like architecture, at full scale. Suits bands and grounds that must sing without depicting. Never: pattern at timid scale.
- **Andrea Branzi — Domestic Animals (1985).** Marry raw nature to neon and laminate. Suits collisions of nature and technology. Never: rustic styling.

**Markers follow correspondence.** A marker that names a category keeps that category's color everywhere. A color cycle is for markers that only count. One fact may join the family and wear the pattern.

**Swiss keeps:** the grid under reading text, the facts flush left, and every contrast floor.

**Test:** The clash changes how the subject is felt; remove it, and the character goes.

**Caricature:** Squiggle confetti on everything, black outlines everywhere, an 80s pastel palette, Memphis as wallpaper behind a Swiss page.
