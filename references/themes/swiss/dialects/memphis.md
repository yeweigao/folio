# Memphis — graphic construction

**Invocation:** 孟菲斯 Memphis — Compose a graphic world after Sottsass and Du Pasquier's colour and pattern: clashing inks, a finite vocabulary of shapes or letterforms, and a deliberate way of joining them into a field. Choose its construction from the subject and identity, then preserve that construction through new content; never substitute interchangeable geometric decoration or pattern behind reading text.

Milan, 1981–88: Ettore Sottsass, Nathalie Du Pasquier, George Sowden, Peter Shire, with the Bacterio laminate and Memphis textiles; then the graphic revival that brings their colour and play into modular, typographic and projected compositions. These are related methods, not one compulsory page template.

- **Colour has several working parts.** Assign clashing hues to grounds, figures, type or faces before choosing their amounts. Preserve those functions across a world; the palette's count comes from its construction, not an accent quota. Resolve every reading ground's foreground separately.
- **The composition selects the module.** Choose a tiled area, unequal partitions, a letter-built field or an assembly of projected parts. Pattern may fill the frame, a band, a corner or a stepped edge around a continuous reading field. Derive the module and texture pitch for that construction; permit a new scale or field shape where the format calls for it.
- **The vocabulary carries an identity.** Construct the distinguishing contours, internal parts, relative scales and joins; record what a new piece may vary. A primitive can acquire its identity through proportion, combination and use, without becoming a literal icon of the subject. A name such as flower, arc or creature alone does not transmit that identity.
- **Type participates in the construction.** It can occupy a merged solid field, become the partition, or turn with a projected face. Choose the lineage and hierarchy for that method: light/heavy contrast is one option, dense letter masses or narrow face lettering are others. Give each reading role a resolved face and size relationship.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **The tile system — Memphis graphics now (2010s–).** A synthesized register. Construct the kit first, derive its square module, then join whole tiles into the field shape the piece needs: a full collage, band, column, corner or stepped landscape. Merge reading areas and preserve their clear ground. Keep motif construction and texture pitch while changing the arrangement. Suits identities, series, decks and campaigns. Never: a generic kit whose only continuity is its colours, or one miniature strip repeated on every format.
- **Ettore Sottsass and Nathalie Du Pasquier — Bacterio (1978), the Memphis textiles (1981–87).** Draw a repeat as a tileable laminate at full scale, then clad the chosen surface with it. Its repeat and scale are material decisions, with reading areas resolved as separate solids. Suits grounds, bands and surfaces whose pattern carries the voice. Never: timid pattern stretched or compressed to fill a leftover rectangle.
- **Camille Walala — the painted façades and pattern environments (2010s–).** Divide a surface into large connected blocks of contrasting stripes, checks, dots and zigzags, and give words a solid block. Let changes in block size and pattern direction make the route through the work. Suits environments, events and covers. Never: one fine texture spread uniformly over the whole composition.
- **Peter Shire — the Bel Air chair (1982).** Assemble clashing parts as a volume; when using its graphic projection, solve the shared vertices and faces before lettering. Set type in each face's local coordinates, then project the face and its type together. Suits launches, events and technology. Never: depth supplied by shadow, or detached lettering fitted after the faces have been drawn.

**Swiss keeps:** declared geometry and alignment, one readable lead, live facts, resolved reading grounds and every contrast floor. A square grid belongs to the method that uses it.

**Test:** With new content, the world's motif construction, colour functions, type hierarchy and operative field relationships remain recognizable while the arrangement can change. Inspect the image; shared colours or a shared title alone are insufficient. In grayscale the composition still organizes reading.

**Caricature:** generic flower-and-star confetti; the same pattern band on every format; outlines on everything; or an ordinary page made colourful without changing its construction.
