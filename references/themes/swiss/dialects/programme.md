# programme — design as a system of permutations

*A dialect of the Swiss theme: it inherits [the mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and changes only what it names.*

Karl Gerstner's *Designing Programmes* (1964) and the concrete art of Richard Paul Lohse and Max Bill: instead of designing one solution, design the rule that generates all of them. Color fields in measured progressions, modules permuted, a visible logic the reader can almost reconstruct.

Reach for it for identities that must flex across many pieces, series covers, dividers, campaigns, data-adjacent culture work, and anything where variety must still look like one family; 0.5–10 m. Not for long reading, documentary, or a single quiet page.

## Picture it

*Illustration, not a template.* Twelve covers for a lecture series share one 6 × 6 field of squares. Each cover is the same field with a different rule applied: cover 3 fills every third cell with ink, cover 7 turns the seventh cell into a quarter circle in the accent, cover 12 inverts the field. The title sits in the same column on every cover. Seen together, the series reads as a single system.

## What makes it programme

- **Signature move**: a declared rule (a grid of cells, a color progression, a permutation) generates the composition, and each unit is a different state of the same rule.
- **Motif**: the field itself, bounded and on the grid, with the rule stated in the delivery note.
- **Owner's test**: someone shown three units can guess the fourth.

## House values

- Colors: ink, accent, and signal as the field's three inks, cycling on a fixed period (every fifth cell, every seventh). Progressions in equal steps of tint or area, never random.
- Shapes: squares, quarter circles, and circles cut from the cell, no other forms.
- Type: the mother tongue's heavy mode, or thin mode for very large titles over a dense field; titles never placed inside the field's cells.
- Fields carry no data. When numbers matter, they sit in text beside the field.
- Voice in copy: precise, almost mathematical.

## The caricature

Asked for "generative" or "geometric", models produce random confetti: scattered circles in five colors, Memphis squiggles, gradient blobs, a different rule on every page. Refuse it. A field without a stated rule is decoration.

## Latitude

Free: cell count, period, which state each unit shows, which of the three inks leads. Say so when adding a fourth ink, a shape outside square, quarter circle, and circle, or a field that encodes data.
