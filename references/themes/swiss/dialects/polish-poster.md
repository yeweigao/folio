# polish-poster — the authored metaphor

The Polish Poster School, Warsaw, 1950s–70s: Henryk Tomaszewski, Jan Lenica, Roman Cieślewicz, Waldemar Świerzy, Franciszek Starowieyski, Jan Młodożeniec; film, theatre, opera, and circus posters. It inherits the [mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and replaces only these rules, across the whole piece:

- **The image interprets, not reports.** One visual metaphor states a view of the subject; it never poses as evidence.
- **The hand shows, not hides.** Choose brush, crayon, cut paper, or montage to make the metaphor. Anchor it in one named artist's verified work; if no work is named in the brief or references, research one before borrowing its technique. If no source is available, state the medium as an authored choice rather than inventing an attribution. Do not blend techniques by default.
- **Lettering belongs to the picture, not beside it.** Drawn titles join the image; every fact also stands as live type.
- **The idea holds the composition, not the grid.** Image and title arrange around the metaphor; supporting text still sits on the grid.

**Test:** The picture says something about this subject that a photograph of it could not.

**Caricature:** A brush font, distressed paper, and a surreal object unrelated to the subject.
