# Polish Poster School — the authored metaphor

**Invocation:** 波兰海报学派 Polish Poster School — Make work as Tomaszewski's school made the film posters: a painted metaphor first, letters manhandled, the facts demoted to a quiet block; never a ruled mechanical image.

Warsaw, 1950s–70s: Tomaszewski, Lenica, Cieślewicz, Świerzy, Starowieyski; film, theatre, opera, and circus posters. Spoken in Swiss, the Polish poster gives the image to the author and the facts to the grid.

- **The image interprets, not reports.** One visual metaphor states a view of the subject; it never poses as evidence.
- **The hand shows, not hides.** Brush, crayon, cut paper, or montage, in one named artist's line, not a blend of all.
- **Lettering belongs to the picture, not beside it.** Drawn titles join the image; every fact also stands as live type.
- **The idea holds the composition, not the grid.** Image and title arrange around the metaphor.

**Hands in this school** — the DESIGN chooses one to govern; the others guest as moves, never as co-hands:
- **Henryk Tomaszewski — the CYRK and film posters (1947–).** A painted metaphor, letters drawn and manhandled, color washed on a muted ground. Suits subjects sayable as one ironic image. Never: a mechanical diagram.
- **Tadeusz Trepkowski — Nie (1952).** One stark symbol, near-wordless; the thing's own silhouette, black on white. Suits protest, warning, grief. Never: explanation beside the symbol.
- **Jan Młodożeniec — the film posters (1950s–).** Expressive paint, folk energy, letters as brushwork. Suits culture and human subjects. Never: restraint mistaken for respect.
- **Roman Cieślewicz — the photomontages (1960s–).** Cut photographs in surreal collision over dense black; the image argues by montage. Suits subjects with evidence to weaponize. Never: montage as decoration.

**Swiss keeps:** the facts: date, place, and credits flush left on the grid, in live type.

**Test:** The picture says something about this subject that a photograph of it could not.

**Caricature:** A brush font, distressed paper, and a surreal object unrelated to the subject.
