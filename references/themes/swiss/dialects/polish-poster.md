# Polish Poster School — the authored metaphor

**Invocation:** 波兰海报学派 Polish Poster School — Make work as Tomaszewski's school made the film posters: one hand-made metaphor in one artist's medium, letters drawn into the picture, the facts demoted to a quiet block; never a ruled mechanical image.

Warsaw, 1950s–70s: Tomaszewski, Lenica, Cieślewicz, Świerzy, Starowieyski, with film, theatre, opera and circus posters. Spoken in Swiss, the Polish poster gives the image to the author and the facts to the grid.

- **The image interprets; it does not report.** One visual metaphor states a view of the subject and never poses as evidence.
- **The hand shows; it does not hide.** Brush, crayon, cut paper or montage, in one named artist's line, never a blend of all.
- **Lettering belongs to the picture, not beside it.** Drawn titles join the image, and every fact also stands as live type.
- **The idea holds the composition, not the grid.**

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Henryk Tomaszewski — the CYRK and film posters (1947–).** Find the one ironic image the subject can be said in. Paint it in gouache on a muted ground, and draw the letters in the same hand. Suits subjects sayable as one ironic image. Never: a mechanical diagram.
- **Jan Lenica — *Wozzeck* (1964) and the cut-paper posters.** Cut the figure from paper with scissors; let jagged edges be the drawing, and tear the letters rather than draw them. Suits subjects with violence or absurdity inside them. Never: torn edges as texture.
- **Tadeusz Trepkowski — *Nie* (1952).** Reduce the subject to its own silhouette, one stark symbol, black on white, near-wordless. Suits protest, warning and grief. Never: an explanation beside the symbol.
- **Roman Cieślewicz — the photomontages (1960s–).** Cut real photographs and collide them over dense black, so the image argues by montage. Suits subjects with evidence to weaponize. Never: montage as decoration.

**Hand-made material.** Use scans of real hand work where you have them. Without them, generate the image with the artist and the medium named in the prompt, declare it as generated, and keep the facts live. A brush font is not a hand.

**Swiss keeps:** the facts (date, place, credits) flush left on the grid, in live type.

**Test:** The picture says something about this subject that a photograph of it could not.

**Caricature:** A brush font, distressed paper, and a surreal object unrelated to the subject.
