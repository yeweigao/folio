# Bauhaus — construction from elements

**Invocation:** 包豪斯 Bauhaus — Make work as Kandinsky, Moholy-Nagy and Bayer made the Dessau work: circle, square and triangle in the primaries (yellow triangle, red square, blue circle) constructing the field, type as one shape among shapes; never decoration, never a fourth hue.

Weimar and Dessau, 1919–33: Bayer, Joost Schmidt, Moholy-Nagy, Albers; Kandinsky's yellow triangle, red square, and blue circle; Schmidt's 1923 exhibition poster, Bayer's universal alphabet. Spoken in Swiss, the Bauhaus constructs: the Vorkurs's elements, assembled on the Swiss module.

- **Primaries together, not one accent.** Red, yellow, and blue with black, on white or off-white paper; no other hue.
- **The image is constructed from elements, not photographed or drawn.** Point, line, and plane, after Kandinsky's *Point and Line to Plane*: circle, square, triangle, bar, and their halves and quarters assemble the subject, a face, a chair, a letter, a number.
- **Shapes overlap, not only abut.** Where two primaries cross, one overprints or cuts the other.
- **Type is a shape among shapes, not a caption.** A geometric sans or constructed capitals, set vertical, diagonal, or across the field; Latin may go lowercase.

**Hands in this school** — the DESIGN chooses one to govern; the others guest as moves, never as co-hands:
- **Wassily Kandinsky — the form–colour questionnaire (1923), Point and Line to Plane (1926).** Assign form and color by law: yellow triangle, red square, blue circle; construct the subject from them. Suits systems and three-part structures. Never: a primary paired against its law for decoration.
- **László Moholy-Nagy — the photograms, the Light-Space Modulator (1922–30).** Compose with overlap and diagonal dynamics; image and type fused in one construction. Suits motion, technology, layered evidence. Never: a static centered emblem.
- **Herbert Bayer — the universal alphabet (1926), the exhibition designs.** Latin lowercase throughout; one functional alphabet for every voice. Suits wayfinding and long functional text. Never: capitals for ceremony.
- **Josef Albers — the Vorkurs material studies, the Variant series.** Spend the least material for the most effect; nest one form inside itself and let relation work. Suits economy and series. Never: an element that does not change its neighbour.

**Swiss keeps:** the module, every shape snapped to one square grid at doubling sizes; reading text, the type ramp, and the facts.

**Test:** Remove the type, and the shapes still compose the subject on the grid.

**Caricature:** Shapes scattered off the grid with no relation; pastel or teal "Bauhaus"; gradients, shadows, rounded UI cards; geometry pasted beside an ordinary layout.
