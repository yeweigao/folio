# Bauhaus — the subject built from elements

**Invocation:** 包豪斯 Bauhaus — Make work as Joost Schmidt made the 1923 exhibition poster: build the subject's figure from circle, square and bar on one module, in red, yellow, blue and black; never shapes that build nothing.

Weimar and Dessau, 1919–33: Schmidt, Bayer, Moholy-Nagy, Albers, Kandinsky. Spoken in Swiss, the Bauhaus constructs: the elements of the Vorkurs, assembled on the Swiss module into a figure of the subject.

- **The image is constructed, not photographed or illustrated.** Point, line and plane (circle, square, triangle, bar, and their halves) assemble a figure that belongs to this subject: a face, a machine, a letter, a building, a number.
- **The primaries act together, not as one accent.** Red, yellow and blue with black, on white or off-white; no other hue.
- **Shapes overlap, not only abut.** Where two primaries cross, one overprints or cuts the other.
- **Type is a shape among shapes, not a caption.** A geometric sans or constructed capitals, set vertical, diagonal or across the field; Latin may go lowercase.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Joost Schmidt — the Bauhaus exhibition poster (1923).** Start from the subject's figure and draw it with compass and square: circles, squares and bars on one module until the figure reads. Then set the type into the same construction. Suits events and institutions where one figure can stand for the subject. Never: a composition of shapes that depicts nothing.
- **Herbert Bayer — the poster for Kandinsky's 60th-birthday exhibition (1926), the universal alphabet.** Make the typography itself the structure. Bars, rules and circles organize lowercase type into a reading order, and letters are drawn from circle and line. Suits programmes and information with a lot of type. Never: capitals for ceremony.
- **László Moholy-Nagy — typophoto, the Bauhausbücher (1925).** Use the camera as a tool. Set photograph, type and bars together in diagonal tension, as one construction. Suits technology, industry, and layered evidence. Never: a static centered emblem.
- **Wassily Kandinsky — *Point and Line to Plane* (1926).** Visits only, never governs. Assign form and color by law (yellow triangle, red square, blue circle) to three real parts of the subject. Never: the three forms recited, constructing nothing.

**A fourth part takes the line.** A fourth term (sound, time, a sequence) is drawn in black line, never in a fourth hue.

**Swiss keeps:** the module, with every shape snapped to one square grid at doubling sizes; reading text, the type ramp, and the facts.

**Test:** Remove the type, and the shapes still build a figure of this subject.

**Caricature:** The Kandinsky triad recited on any subject; shapes scattered off the grid; pastel or teal "Bauhaus"; gradients, shadows, rounded cards.
