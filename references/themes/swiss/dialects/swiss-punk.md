# swiss-punk — one gesture

*A dialect of the Swiss theme: it inherits [the mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and changes only what it names.*

Swiss Punk and the New Wave: Weingart in Basel, April Greiman, Dan Friedman, then *Emigre*. Swiss typography breaking its own rules on purpose (rotation, overprint, layering, crop, newsprint) while the grid stays and the text still reads. For festivals, music, independent publishing, experimental exhibitions, at 0.5–3 m; not for boards, teaching, long argument, or wayfinding.

## What makes it swiss-punk

- **One gesture per unit, not a pile.** A rotation, an overprint, or a crop, on the display word or the image only; it may change between units.
- **The body stays Swiss, not distressed.** Text and data return to the strict grid, upright.
- **Owner's test:** remove the gesture, and a correct Swiss page remains.

## House values

| Role | Relationship | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | White or grey newsprint | ≥ 50% |
| Accent | One hot saturated hue: one word, one bar, one date | ≤ 15% |
| Overprint ink | A second hue far from the accent, present only where they overlap | once per piece |

- Display Helvetica Black, Archivo Black, Druk, or a condensed grotesque; grotesque text, never rotated.
- Rotation 90° or a small tilt, once per unit, its origin on a column intersection; overprint covers at most a quarter of the format and never touches text.
- One image: high-contrast black and white or a real halftone.
- Voice: short and hard.

## Caricature

Grunge: filters, ransom-note letters, skulls and pins, glitch slices, photocopy noise, unreadable stacks, whole-page rotation, "punk" as an excuse not to typeset. Refuse it.

## Latitude

Free: which gesture, angle, crop, newsprint temperature, accent area. Say so for two gestures in a unit, the overprint ink, or a second image.
