# swiss-punk — one gesture

*A dialect of the Swiss theme: it inherits [the mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and changes only what it names.*

Weingart's and Greiman's experiments, when Swiss typography started to break its own rules on purpose: rotation, overprint, layering, cropping, newsprint. Rebellious, yet the grid is still there and the text still reads.

Reach for it for festivals, music, independent publishing, and experimental exhibitions, at 0.5–3 m. Not for boards, teaching, long argument, or wayfinding.

## Picture it

*Illustration, not a template.* Grey newsprint stock. The date "11.07" in Black at 160 px is rotated 90° and climbs the left edge. A halftone photograph of a drummer is cropped hard on the right. Everything else (band names, venue, ticket price) sits on a strict 12-column grid in 16 px Regular. One bar in the accent.

## What makes it swiss-punk

- **Signature move**: one gesture per unit (a rotation, an overprint, or a crop) applied to the display word or the image only. Body text returns to the strict grid.
- **Motif**: the gesture may change between units, but never two gestures in one unit.
- **Owner's test**: remove the gesture and a correct Swiss page remains underneath.

## House values

| Role | Relationship | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | White, or a grey newsprint stock | ≥ 50% |
| Accent | One hot, saturated hue: one word, one bar, one date | ≤ 15% |
| Overprint ink | A second hue far from the accent, multiplied with it, present only where they overlap | once per piece |

- Display: Helvetica Black, Archivo Black, Druk, or condensed Oswald / Archivo Narrow at 64–160. Text: grotesque 16–20 at leading 1.55, never rotated.
- Rotation is 90° or −8°, once per unit, and the rotated element stays readable; its origin sits on a column intersection.
- One element per unit may leave the grid; overprint covers at most a quarter of the format and never touches text.
- One image: high-contrast black and white or a real halftone scan. Data stays quiet: no rotation, overprint, or distress.
- Voice in copy: short and hard.

## The caricature

Asked for "punk design", models produce grunge filters, ransom-note lettering, safety pins and skulls, glitch slices, distressed photocopy noise, and unreadable stacked type. Refuse all of it, along with whole-page rotation and "punk" as an excuse not to typeset.

## Latitude

Free: which gesture, rotation angle, crop, newsprint temperature, accent area. Say so when using two gestures in one unit, the overprint ink, a second image, or rotated body text.
