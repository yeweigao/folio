# De Stijl — the grid as the picture

**Invocation:** 荷兰风格派 De Stijl — Make work as van Doesburg and Mondrian made the compositions: black bars, unequal planes, the three primaries, every word flush-left inside its plane; never a diagonal, never symmetry.

The Netherlands, 1917–31: Mondrian, van Doesburg, Rietveld, van der Leck; Mondrian's compositions with red, yellow, and blue, the Rietveld Schröder House. Spoken in Swiss, De Stijl draws the grid: the Swiss tracks become black bars, and the fields between them become planes of color.

- **The grid is the picture, not the scaffold.** Black bars on the tracks divide the field; text and images live inside its planes.
- **The lead is a balance, not an object.** The largest plane leads against unequal counterweights; never symmetry, never equal cells.
- **Primaries are planes, not an accent.** Red, yellow, and blue build the structure beside white, grey, and black.
- **Right angles, not rotation,** unless the brief names van Doesburg's Elementarism, which turns the whole field 45°.

**Hands in this school** — the DESIGN chooses one to govern; the others guest as moves, never as co-hands:
- **Piet Mondrian — the compositions (1921–30).** Balance unequal planes on black bars; nothing leads but the relation. Suits subjects that are themselves a system of weights. Never: a centered title block.
- **Piet Mondrian — New York (1942–43), Broadway Boogie Woogie.** The same hand, late: black bars dissolve into colored segments, blocks break into syncopated beats. Suits turn units, closers, anything rhythmic or urban. Never: the early bars quoted beside it as decoration.
- **Theo van Doesburg — the counter-compositions (1924–26).** Tilt the construction to the diagonal; energy over equilibrium. Suits rupture points and arguments in motion. Never: inside a Mondrian-governed deliverable — the two split the school over exactly this.
- **Bart van der Leck — the print work (Delftsche Slaolie poster, 1919).** Flatten content itself into color blocks: a fact, a figure, a product becomes a plane. Suits planes that must carry whole fact-blocks. Never: illustration drawn inside a plane.

**Swiss keeps:** asymmetry, the tracks the bars fall on, and flush-left type inside the planes.

**Test:** Resize or recolor one plane, and the balance of the whole shifts.

**Caricature:** A card grid filled red, yellow, and blue; a black lattice laid over an ordinary page; Mondrian as a border.
