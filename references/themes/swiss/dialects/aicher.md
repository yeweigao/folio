# aicher — legible at a distance

*A dialect of the Swiss theme: it inherits [the mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and changes only what it names.*

Otl Aicher's Munich 1972 system and the transit and airport signage it inspired: Univers, Gerhard Joksch's pictograms on a modular grid, the seven-color Munich palette, color that means something, information a stranger reads in a second in any language. For teaching, wayfinding, public events, schedules, multilingual readers, at 3–10 m or in handouts; not for board argument, fashion, or long documentary text.

## What makes it aicher

- **The same action always looks the same.** Pictograms, labels, and positions are consistent everywhere.
- **Progress is marked only where stages are real.** Its ticks match the real number of steps.
- **Owner's test:** someone at the back of the room, or a visitor who reads neither language well, knows what to do next.

## House values

- Paper, ink, and one accent, from the Munich palette (blue, green, silver, orange) when the brand gives none; function colors only for real wayfinding or status, at most three, mapped in a legend, never the only code, checked against regional meaning.
- One grotesque (Univers, Helvetica Now, Archivo; Noto Sans SC): regular to explain, bold to instruct, black for numbers.
- Primary language above, secondary below, smaller and muted; never two languages in one line.
- Pictograms are built, not drawn: a solid figure of a person doing the action, assembled from geometric parts on a modular grid at 0°, 45°, and 90°, one set; never UI line icons.
- Timetables, steps, and rules as tables or single-axis lists; progress as a ruler with a number.
- Voice: short, unambiguous instructions.

## Caricature

Kindergarten clip art: rainbow palettes, mascots, emoji, bubbly fonts, icon walls, three kinds of arrow; red and green outside safety meaning; pictograms off the baseline. Refuse it.

## Latitude

Free: stage count, route turns, pictogram scale, size steps, bilingual hierarchy. Say so for a function color, a second pictogram set, a new motif, or photography.
