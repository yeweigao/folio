# isotype — statistics as pictures

*A dialect of the Swiss theme: it inherits [the mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and changes only what it names.*

Otto Neurath and Gerd Arntz's International System of Typographic Picture Education (Vienna, 1920s–40s): quantities shown by repeating a symbol, so a reader who barely reads can still compare. "Words divide, pictures unite."

Reach for it when numbers must reach a broad or young audience: education, public reports, parent communications, annual reviews for non-specialists; 0.5–5 m. Not for precise analysis that needs exact values on axes, for fashion, or for manifestos.

## Picture it

*Illustration, not a template.* "Students per campus, 2026": four rows, one per campus; each figure-pictogram stands for 100 students; Campus A shows 12 figures, Campus B 7 and a half. Pictograms are flat, frontal, in ink; one row is in the accent because it is the campus being discussed. The exact numbers sit at each row's end in Inter.

## What makes it isotype

- **Signature move**: more means more symbols, not bigger symbols. Every symbol is the same size and stands for a stated quantity.
- **Motif**: one pictogram family, drawn flat and frontal on a square module, with no perspective and no outline-plus-fill mixtures.
- **Owner's test**: a ten-year-old can say which row is bigger and roughly by how much.

## House values

- Pictograms: solid silhouettes on the 8 px module, frontal or profile, one family for the whole piece; partial quantities shown by cutting a symbol, not shrinking it.
- Rows read left to right on a shared baseline; a key states what one symbol equals; exact values printed at row ends.
- Color: ink for all symbols, the accent for the row or category under discussion, at most three mapped colors for categories, always with a legend.
- Type: the mother tongue's heavy mode; argument titles state the finding, reference titles name the comparison.
- Voice in copy: plain, concrete, child-readable.

## The caricature

Asked for "infographic", models produce icon-salad: colorful 3D icons of different styles, a big icon scaled up to show a big number, donuts and gauges next to clip art, decorative arrows. Refuse it. Scaling both dimensions by the value exaggerates area; Isotype uses equal-sized repeated symbols.

## Latitude

Free: the unit each symbol represents, row order, which row takes the accent. Say so when adding a second pictogram family, perspective, or more than three category colors.
