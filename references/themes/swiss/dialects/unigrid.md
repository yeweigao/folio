# unigrid — unequal modules

*A dialect of the Swiss theme: it inherits [the mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and changes only what it names.*

Müller-Brockmann's modular grid and Vignelli's Unigrid for the US National Park Service (1977), carried into product overviews: unequal modules on the Swiss grid, one clearly leading; the bento grid without its rounded-card cliché. For product overviews, design systems, feature maps: many parallel parts of unequal weight, at 0.5–1.5 m; not for long reading, a single argument, or documentary.

## What makes it unigrid

- **Modules are ranked, not equal.** One spans six or twelve columns and is read first; the rest follow importance.
- **Modules are fields, not floating cards.** Separation by space, 1 px lines, or solid fields, one strength per screen.
- **Owner's test:** squint, and the dominant module still jumps out first.

## Caricature

The keynote grid: equal rounded cards, soft shadows, an icon per tile, gradients for depth, KPI tiles with donuts and status dots, cards in cards. Refuse it.

## Latitude

Free: module count, dominant span, row unit, separation strength. Say so for rounded corners, card treatment, or competing focal points.
