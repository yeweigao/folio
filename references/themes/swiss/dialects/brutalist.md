# brutalist — Swiss Brutal

Swiss Brutal: the Swiss International Style's grid and left axis, stripped of polish — no radius, no shadow, no apology — and given Brutalism's exposed structure. The line runs from the *béton brut* of Le Corbusier and Alison and Peter Smithson through Swiss typographic print — twelve columns, flush-left setting, a tight grotesque in the Archivo/Helvetica register, information as ornament — to the web's brutalist revival (Pascal Deville's brutalistwebsites.com, 2014; Craigslist and the Drudge Report as raw precedents). It inherits the [mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and replaces only these rules, across the whole piece:

- **The grid is visible, not implied.** Twelve columns on one left axis; 1px rules at one weight; zero radius, no shadows, no gradients. A box is allowed only when it is the real structure.
- **Information is the ornament.** Monolithic sans titles and huge numerals; dense ledger rows, specs, versions, fields. No decorative image, icon, or texture. A title may overflow the trim when the word is the structure, never to fill a gap.
- **Dense, not airy.** A working document, not a landing page; every band carries real content; whitespace is structural, not a placeholder for an image.
- **Industrial mode: paper, ink, one hazard.** Newsprint or grey-white ground, black ink, at most one hazard-red accent on a real alert or figure. ASCII rules (`┌─┘`) only when they are actual structure, never as texture.

**Test:** Every visible line is a real boundary, and every band is filled with working content; strip the polish and the page still reads as a ruled ledger, not a landing page.

**Caricature:** Terminal cosplay (green on black, scanlines, noise); neo-brutalist stickers (thick outlines, fluorescent blocks, hard shadows); ugliness on purpose; broken layouts called raw.
