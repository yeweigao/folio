# gridnik — type built from the grid

*A dialect of the Swiss theme: it inherits [the mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and changes only what it names.*

Wim Crouwel, "Mr. Gridnik", and Total Design: the Stedelijk posters and the New Alphabet (1967), letters constructed from the grid that holds the page. Systematic, cool, visibly engineered. For technology, architecture, music and exhibitions, research institutes, constructed identities, at 0.5–10 m; not for warm community materials, long reading, or documentary.

## What makes it gridnik

- **The grid is inside the letters, not behind them.** Display letters are built from cells: rectangles, one corner radius or none, one stroke; text aligns to the same lines.
- **The construction is visible, not implied.** The grid shows faintly on display units.
- **Owner's test:** overlay the grid, and every stroke and text edge lands on it.

## House values

- Constructed lettering as vector shapes with the real word also present as live text, or a geometric face with square terminals; text in Inter or the mother tongue's sans.
- Square cells on display units; stroke equals one cell or one gutter.
- Ink and paper with the accent on one element, or one strong field with ink type; the grid in the accent at low strength.
- Images rare: geometric or architectural photographs cropped to whole cells.
- Voice: terse, technical, numeric.

## Caricature

Retro-futurism: neon wireframes, dot-matrix on black, synthwave gradients, glitch. Refuse it; gridnik is printed, flat, and calm, futuristic through system, not glow.

## Latitude

Free: cell size, stroke, which letters are constructed, field color. Say so for mixing constructed and conventional display type or a second radius. Keep the live-text equivalent.
