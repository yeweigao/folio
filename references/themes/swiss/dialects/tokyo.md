# tokyo — the grid and the void

*A dialect of the Swiss theme: it inherits [the mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and changes only what it names.*

Japanese modernism: Yusaku Kamekura's Tokyo 1964 posters, Ikko Tanaka's geometric figures and Nihon Buyo poster, and later Kenya Hara's emptiness for MUJI. The Swiss grid meets *ma*, the charged interval: large calm fields, one mark, vertical and horizontal CJK set together.

Reach for it for culture, education with an East Asian identity, exhibitions, ceremonies, brands that need restraint with warmth, and Chinese or Japanese content that deserves its own typographic tradition; 0.5–10 m. Not for dense data argument or technical documentation.

## Picture it

*Illustration, not a template.* A poster almost entirely soft paper. One red disc, a third of the width, sits off-center on the upper grid line. Down the right edge, the event name runs vertically in heavy serif; the date and place sit small, horizontal, at the bottom left on the grid. Two-thirds of the sheet is empty, and the emptiness holds it together.

## What makes it tokyo

- **Signature move**: one mark (a disc, a square, a single brushless geometric figure) in a field of charged emptiness, with type that may run vertically.
- **Motif**: the interval itself. Empty area is measured and repeated, often half to two-thirds of the unit.
- **Owner's test**: remove the mark and the page feels unbalanced; add a second and it feels crowded.

## House values

- Paper: the soft field or white; ink; one accent, classically red, used as a single shape rather than as text color.
- Type: heavy serif display for vertical titles, Noto Sans SC for horizontal text, Inter for numbers. Vertical text only for short lines (titles, quotations, names), with Latin and numbers rotated or set upright as the content needs.
- Geometry built from circle, square, and their halves on the grid; no decorative illustration, no brush texture.
- Photographs, if any, are quiet and generous, with large margins.
- Voice in copy: few words, seasonal and specific.

## The caricature

Asked for "Japanese style", models produce tourist pastiche: cherry blossoms, waves after Hokusai, brush-stroke fonts, torii gates, red-and-gold patterns, rising-sun rays. Refuse it. The modernist tradition is geometric and empty, not ornamental.

## Latitude

Free: proportion of emptiness, the mark's shape and position, vertical or horizontal title, paper temperature. Say so when adding a second mark, a second accent, illustration, or vertical body text.
