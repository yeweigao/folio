# public-info — legible at a distance

*A dialect of the Swiss theme: it inherits [the mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and changes only what it names.*

Otl Aicher's system for the Munich 1972 Olympics, and the airport and transit signage it inspired: one grotesque, pictograms drawn on a strict grid, color that means something, information that a stranger can read in a second, in any language.

Reach for it for teaching materials, wayfinding, public events, schedules, and readers who do not share one language, read at 3–10 m (projection, poster, signage) or 0.5 m in handouts. Not for board argument, fashion, or long documentary text.

## Picture it

*Illustration, not a template.* A projected classroom slide: "Listen, then circle the answer" in Bold at the top left; a single pictogram of an ear, 2 px stroke, square ends; below, four answer lines with generous space. The Chinese instruction sits one size smaller beneath the English, in muted ink. A thin progress ruler marks step 2 of 4.

## What makes it public-info

- **Signature move**: consistent pictograms, labels, and positions express direction and state. The same action always looks the same.
- **Motif**: a route or progress marker, used only when the content really has stages; its ticks match the real number of steps.
- **Owner's test**: someone at the back of the room, or a visitor who reads neither language well, still knows what to do next.

## House values

- Color: the project palette, or the mother tongue's paper, ink, and one accent. When real wayfinding or status needs function colors, use at most three, each mapped in a legend and never the only code. Color meaning varies by region; check the setting.
- Type: one grotesque only (Univers, Helvetica Now, Archivo; CJK Noto Sans SC). Weights 400 explanation, 700 instruction, 900 numbers. Sizes come from the surface's viewing distance, not from this file.
- Bilingual: primary language above, secondary below, two steps smaller and muted. Never two languages squeezed into one line.
- Pictograms: geometric, uniform 2 px stroke, square corners, one set for the whole piece, depicting actions and objects rather than moods.
- Data: timetables, steps, and rules as tables or single-axis lists; progress as a ruler with a number; proportion as length.
- Voice in copy: short, unambiguous instructions.

## The caricature

Asked for "fun learning materials", models produce kindergarten clip art: rainbow palettes, cartoon mascots, emoji, rounded bubbly fonts, colored icon walls, three kinds of arrows on one page. Refuse it. Also refuse red and green used for anything other than safety meaning, gradients for "modern", and pictograms that sit off the text baseline.

## Latitude

Free: number of stages, route turns, pictogram scale, size steps, bilingual hierarchy. Say so when adding a function color, a second pictogram set, a changed motif, or photography.
