# brodovitch — type against image

Alexey Brodovitch's *Harper's Bazaar*, New York, 1934–58, with Martin Munkácsi, Richard Avedon, and Cassandre's covers; carried on in Fabien Baron's *Harper's Bazaar* (1992) and its Didot masthead. It inherits the [mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and replaces only these rules, across the whole piece:

- **Type and image collide, not sit side by side.** One display word in Didot or Bodoni covers, cuts, or yields to the photograph; one focal point.
- **The photograph is desire, not fact.** A daring crop, motion, the body, still one readable gesture; credits name model, garment, and photographer.
- **The issue moves like film, not a stack of pages.** Scale, crop, and white change from spread to spread.
- **Black and white, not color.** Or one duotone; one cool accent at most.

**Test:** On an image-led unit, remove the type and the picture loses its composition.

**Caricature:** Fake luxury: gold gradients, rose gold, glass, a centered wide-tracked serif logo; Bodoni as body text.
