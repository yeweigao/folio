# gerstner — design as permutation

*A dialect of the Swiss theme: it inherits [the mother tongue](../mother-tongue.md) and changes only what it names.*

Karl Gerstner's *Designing Programmes* (1964), the concrete art of Richard Paul Lohse and Max Bill: design the rule that generates every solution, not one solution. Measured color progressions, permuted modules, a logic the reader can almost reconstruct. For flexible identities, series covers, dividers, campaigns, data-adjacent culture work, at 0.5–10 m; not for long reading, documentary, or a single quiet page.

## What makes it gerstner

- **A declared rule generates the units, not taste per unit.** A grid of cells, a progression, or a permutation; each unit is another state of it.
- **The field is bounded and on the grid; the rule is stated in delivery.**
- **Owner's test:** shown three units, someone can guess the fourth.

## House values

- Ink, accent, and signal as the field's three inks, cycling on a fixed period; progressions in equal steps, never random.
- Squares, quarter circles, and circles cut from the cell; no other forms.
- Type in heavy mode, or thin mode over a dense field; titles never inside the cells.
- Fields carry no data; numbers sit in text beside them.
- Voice: precise, almost mathematical.

## Caricature

Random confetti: scattered five-color circles, Memphis squiggles, gradient blobs, a new rule per page. Refuse it; a field without a stated rule is decoration.

## Latitude

Free: cell count, period, each unit's state, which ink leads. Say so for a fourth ink, another shape, or a field that encodes data.
