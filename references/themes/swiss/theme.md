# Swiss — the International Typographic Style

Grid, asymmetry, and objectivity, set for Chinese and Latin: Müller-Brockmann's *Grid Systems*, Ruder's *Typographie*, Hofmann's Basel, *Neue Grafik*; Lohse, Vivarelli, Neuburg, and Gerstner's *Designing Programmes*; Akzidenz, Univers, Helvetica. It governs relationships, so it hosts other movements without becoming costume. Every dialect inherits this file and replaces only the rules it names.

## Grammar

- **The grid comes before placement.** Six shared tracks, twelve to subdivide; margins, gutters, module, and leading set before anything is placed.
- **Asymmetry is the default.** Flush left, ragged right, unequal spans; centering needs a reason.
- **Rules, not boxes.** An opening rule establishes a table, index, or figure; hairlines separate; a closing rule completes. Group by space, rules, or one field, never by floating rounded cards, cards in cards, decorative shadows, glow, or plates.
- **A field resets every foreground.** Paper, soft, dark, and accent each carry a complete palette, so any field nests in any other.
- **One module, few axes.** Half a module within a group, one between peers, two between sections; leading keeps its own rhythm.
- **Furniture earns its place by function.** *Kopfzeile* and *Fusszeile* locate, *Verzeichnis* serves lookup, *Bildlegende* ties evidence to source, *Kennzahl* makes a consequential quantity visible, *Zeitachse* needs a real sequence, *Bemassung* a real measure. What orients keeps its position and treatment.

## Type

Display has three lineages, none more refined: a heavy grotesque for mass, a large light sans for scale without a black block, a high-contrast serif or Song for editorial and cultural weight. The subject and the viewing distance choose. Sustained reading may take a text serif; apparatus, a sans.

## Color

| Role | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Paper · soft | The reading ground: white on screen, the stock in print; a quieter second field only when useful |
| Ink · muted | Main and secondary reading, contrasted on every field they touch |
| Line | Separation of groups already legible; never text |
| Dark | A change of register with its own complete foreground |
| Accent | The exception; an accent field is a deliberate turn |
| Signal | An optional second ink with a different job and a non-color cue |

- **One accent, not a palette.** Another hue enters only as a declared second ink, a print stock, a mapped function color (at most three, with a legend, never the only code), or an overprint; one accent never serves unrelated roles.
- **The accent is an exception, not a second paper.** Paper ≥ 50% of the area; accent ≤ 10%, ≤ 20% on a statement unit; a full accent field is a variation.
- **A light accent never inks the paper.** Below 3:1 on paper it is no text, line, or mark there; only a ground behind dark text, or ink on a dark field.
- **The accent returns rarely enough to matter.** White paper on screen; a warm screen ground is a brand decision. The habit is Folio vermilion `#C83024`, with Folio signal yellow `#F5C84B` as a second ink.

## Images

- **A photograph serves the subject, not a generic mood.** *Sachfotografie*, edge to edge or cut by a hard horizontal; no plate, shadow, radius, or scrim beyond legibility.
- **Drawing is native when it explains.** Geometry, diagrams, maps, pictograms; flat, one line weight, one style, on the grid, colored from the roles. Clip art, mascots, blob people, emoji, an icon per bullet, and icon walls stay out.

## Methods

Two prices are hidden in this table: most cells are baseline craft, and a few carry a master's hand. - **Cells combine, not compete.** A strong unit may join two or three cells from different columns; reference units stay quiet.
- **A move the tools cannot make well is swapped within its row, not faked.**
- **Methods take this grammar's form.** A leading line uses straight segments on the grid, never a wave; color blocking is solid, never translucent; a calligram keeps whole lines on the baseline grid; an oversized glyph carries meaning; a cut-out keeps a clean edge and legible text.
- **A systematic series is generated, not decorated one by one.** Where repetition serves the content, derive units from a grid, cycle, or permutation, after Gerstner and Lohse; a narrative may change method from unit to unit.

## Variations

A variation breaks the active grammar, the mother tongue as the dialect has changed it, on one unit, so a change in the content is felt. A dialect's own moves are its grammar, not variations; a method pushed until it breaks the grammar becomes one, cited as pushed plus the method's name.

- **Every turn gets a decision.** At an opener, a key number, a pause, or a change of mood, take a variation from the tables below or record in DESIGN.md why continuity serves — with the rhythm cost named (step 6 of SKILL.md). A variation named without its magnitude is a wish, not a decision: every use states its angle, area, count, or size in the Sequence, beside its Keep condition.
- **A break answers the content, not a quota;** continuity is right while the reader's task stays the same.
- **One break per unit;** everything else on it keeps the grammar.
- **A guest lends one move, not its system:** one signature from another dialect, for one unit.
- **A variation repairs nothing.** Fix a weak composition first.
- **Reading text is never the experiment.** No rotation, overprint, or contrast below the floor for body text.
- **Borrow the system behind the lineage, not its surface.**

**Breaking the orthogonal grid**

| Variation | What it does | Where | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rotation | One display element at ±6° or 90° | Posters, culture | Origin on a grid intersection | Lissitzky, Weingart |
| Radial field | Arcs or rings radiate from one point and carry the type | Music, launches, a single poster | One center; text set on the grid | Müller-Brockmann's concert posters |
| Symmetric axis | The unit is centered on one vertical axis | Ceremonies, invitations, inscriptions | Only once; everything else flush left | Classical inscription, late Tschichold |
| Split field | One hard line divides the unit into two contrasting fields | Before and after, either-or statements | The split carries the content's opposition | Constructivist posters, Lissitzky's wedge |

**Breaking the single photograph**

| Variation | What it does | Where | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Photomontage | Two or three photographs at different scales compose one scene | Openers, campaigns | Declared as montage, never posed as a real scene | Heartfield, Klutsis, Herbert Matter |

## Anti-patterns

The floors and craft's honesty rules are evidence: test them. Everything below is house preference that a brief, brand, or dialect may override; functional backing, shadow, or halo is judged by legibility, not style.

- **The caricature:** grey minimal skin at one volume; Helvetica, white, no scale extreme, a red square as ornament.
- **Habit posing as a decision:** white, black, one red, and hairlines when the brief never chose them; a cream ground with a high-contrast serif and terracotta; near-black with one acid accent; a broadsheet of hairlines, zero radius, and dense columns. Each needs a reason in DESIGN.md; otherwise let the content choose the furniture, type mode, and move. A plan that never names the subject is a default.
- Centered hero, three equal cards, gradient button, logo wall.
- KPI tiles: numbers floating without baseline, period, or meaning.
- Numbers on unordered content; watermark numerals; a giant word with no claim behind it.
- Two whole dialects on one unit.
- Gradients, glass, fake grain, fake 3D; greys invented per component.
- Fake luxury: gold gradients, glowing outlines on black, spaced serif capitals.
- Default fonts, extra families, or colored text as hierarchy; a colored eyebrow above every heading.
- Fake editorial: drop caps, quote walls, "Vol. 01".
- A chart on a card with a legend and a grey plot.
