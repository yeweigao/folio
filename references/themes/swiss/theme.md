# Swiss — the International Typographic Style

The International Typographic Style, as set for Chinese and Latin. Its sources are Müller-Brockmann's *Grid Systems*, Ruder's *Typographie*, Hofmann's Basel, *Neue Grafik*, the work of Lohse, Vivarelli and Neuburg, and Gerstner's *Designing Programmes*, set in the grotesques and neo-grotesques of the 1950s. It governs relationships, so it hosts other movements without becoming a costume. Every dialect inherits this file and replaces only the rules it names.

## Dialects

Alone, the mother tongue suits material that argues, reports, testifies, counts or lists. A dialect is a named movement that overturns a few of its rules across the whole work; one per work, or none. Open the one in play before drawing.

| Dialect | The material | Departs from Swiss |
|---|---|---|
| [De Stijl](dialects/de-stijl.md) | Relates: compositions, text-led reports, comparisons and sequences | A drawn field organizes the relationships; name the strict historical method or one reference-derived contemporary system |
| [Bauhaus](dialects/bauhaus.md) | Celebrates or explains, where point, line and plane can build the subject | Elements as forces built into a figure; direction as meaning; primaries mixing only where parts meet |
| [Brodovitch](dialects/brodovitch.md) | Seduces, where people or images are the subject | Black-and-white photographs under one colour of the subject's own; the figure cut against a serif word; paced like film |

| [Ulm](dialects/ulm.md) | Coordinates a system of related messages | Rules before cases, analysis before form; colour and pictograms as codes |
| [Swiss Punk](dialects/swiss-punk.md) | Provokes, still readable | The grid tested by rotation, layering and stepped type |
| [Memphis](dialects/memphis.md) | Delights: products, events, launches, youth culture | Clashing colour roles and a constructed vocabulary; tiles, partitions, letters or projected parts organize the field |

## Hands of the mother tongue

With no dialect, one governs and the others visit as single moves; with a dialect, its hands govern and these only visit. Each is a way of working, not a look.

- **Josef Müller-Brockmann — the Tonhalle *musica viva* posters, *Grid Systems* (1950s–81).** Compute the grid from the format before anything is placed. Find a structure inside the subject (a score's form, a building's plan, a schedule's intervals) and build the image from it in the grid's own geometry. Set type flush left, in few sizes. Suits programmes, institutions, music and public information. Never: a grid declared in the file and invisible in the work.
- **Emil Ruder — *Typographie* (1967), the Basel typography classes.** Compose with type alone. Counters, interletter rhythm, texture and the white of the page are the material, and contrasts of size, weight and direction are the procedure; proof the type as it will really be set. Suits text-led, cultural and bilingual work. Never: an image where type can carry the meaning.
- **Armin Hofmann — the Basel theatre posters, *Graphic Design Manual* (1965).** Reduce the subject to contrasts of form: point, line, letter, black against white. Work the letter and the photograph in tension at large scale, and test by cutting away until only the contrast remains. Suits theatre, culture, and posters with a single claim. Never: color used where a contrast of form would do.
- **Karl Gerstner — *Designing Programmes* (1964).** Write the rule before the cases. Set out the variables in a morphological box, choose the combination, and generate every unit from it in code. Suits series, identities, and anything that must extend. Never: a "system" whose rule nobody can state.

## Grammar

- **The grid comes before placement.** Margins, gutters, module and leading are set first: six tracks, with twelve to subdivide.
- **One grid system per work, chosen by name.** The column system chosen silently is a default, not a decision.
  - *Column*: content flows down shared tracks. For reading and mixed apparatus.
  - *Baseline-led*: sizes are multiples of the leading. For dense text where many sizes must agree.
  - *Modular*: the field is cut both ways into whole modules. For catalogues and peers. Derive the module so the count comes out whole.
  - *Hierarchical*: zones sized by the weight of their content. For one message. Every size must be arguable from the content.
  - *Diagonal*: one family of parallel angles organizes the field. For motion and rupture. Fact bands close inside the frame.
  - *Radial*: rings from one centre carry the type. For music, launches, one strong centre. Sequence maps to radius; bottom arcs read upright.
- **Asymmetry is the default.** Flush left, ragged right; centering needs a reason.
- **Rules, not boxes.** Group by space, selective horizontal or vertical rules, or one field; keep the unused construction grid invisible. No floating cards, shadows, glow or plates.
- **A field resets every foreground.** Each ground carries a complete palette.
- **One module, few axes.** Half a module within a group, one between peers, two between sections.
- **Furniture earns its place by function.** *Kopfzeile* locates, *Verzeichnis* serves lookup, *Bildlegende* ties evidence to source, *Kennzahl* shows a consequential quantity.

## Type

Display has three lineages: a heavy grotesque for mass, a large light sans for scale, a high-contrast serif or Song for editorial weight. The subject and the distance choose; the governing hand's lineage chooses the face.

## Color

- **Roles:** ground, ink, muted, line (never text), dark (a change of register), accent (the exception), signal (an optional second ink with a non-color cue).
- **One accent, not a palette.** A further hue enters only as a declared second ink, a print stock, or a mapped code.
- **The ground covers 50% or more of the area; the accent 10% or less** (20% on a statement unit). A full accent field is a variation.
- **The accent comes from the subject or the hand.** There is no house red.
- **A light accent never inks the ground.**

## Images

- **A photograph serves the subject, not a mood.** *Sachfotografie*: edge to edge or cut by a hard horizontal; no plate, shadow or radius.
- **Drawing is native when it explains:** flat, one line weight, on the grid.

## Variations

A variation breaks the grammar on one unit, where the content turns. Each is stated with its magnitude and its keep condition. One break per unit; reading text is never the experiment; a variation repairs nothing.

**Breaking the type**

| Variation | What it does | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thin-mode turn | Hairline weights at giant size | Small text keeps its stroke | Frutiger's light neo-grotesque cuts |
| Mixed-type line | One display line changes size and weight inside a phrase | Display only; one line per unit | Schwitters, Piet Zwart |
| Type filled with photograph | Letters clipped to an image | The title stays live text | Moholy-Nagy's typophoto |
| Vertical CJK | Chinese set top to bottom, right to left | Short texts only; Latin figures stand upright (tate-chū-yoko), never rotated | East Asian book tradition, Ikko Tanaka |

**Breaking the orthogonal grid**

| Variation | What it does | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rotation | One display element at ±6° or 90° | Origin on a grid intersection | Lissitzky, Weingart |
| Radial field | Arcs radiate from one point and carry the type | One centre | Müller-Brockmann's concert posters |
| Symmetric axis | The unit centered on one vertical axis | Only once | Classical inscription, late Tschichold |
| Split field | One hard line divides the unit into two contrasting fields | The split carries the content's opposition | Lissitzky's wedge |

**Breaking the single photograph**

| Variation | What it does | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photomontage | Two or three photographs at different scales compose one scene | Declared as montage | Heartfield, Herbert Matter |
| Macro crop | A crop so close its texture becomes the field | The subject stays identifiable in a caption | Matter, Sachfotografie |
| Sequence strip | A row of equal frames shows time or movement | Frames in time order, one crop | Muybridge, Brodovitch |
| Duotone | A greyscale photograph multiplied with one ink | One duotone per deliverable | Swiss tourism and concert posters |
| Cut-paper shape | One hand-cut organic shape among the geometry | One shape | Matisse, Paul Rand |

**Breaking the quiet field**

| Variation | What it does | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accent field | The whole unit on the accent | Complete foreground roles | Müller-Brockmann's Zürich posters |
| Type band | A hard band behind a line of display type | One band per unit | Swiss poster typography |
| Overprint | Two inks multiply where they overlap | Reading text stays clear | Weingart, Greiman, Brattinga |
| Line field | Parallel lines vary until the field vibrates | Never behind reading text | Riley, Gerstner |
| Programme field | A bounded generative field with a declared cycle | Cells carry no data | Gerstner, Lohse |

**Breaking the density**

| Variation | What it does | Keep | Lineage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pause | A near-empty unit with one small line or mark | One per sequence | Kenya Hara, *ma* |
| Text wall | A unit of small real text becomes texture | Every item readable up close | Karel Martens |
| Rhythmic repetition | One word or shape repeats in stepped scale or position | One direction of change | *musica viva*, Weingart |

## Anti-patterns

House preferences; a brief, a brand or a dialect may override them.

- **The caricature:** grey minimal skin at one volume; a neo-grotesque, white, a red square as ornament.
- A centered hero, three equal cards, a gradient button, a logo wall.
- KPI tiles; numbers on unordered content; a giant word with no claim.
- Two whole dialects on one unit.
- Gradients, glass, fake grain, fake 3D.
- Fake luxury: gold, glowing outlines, spaced serif capitals.
- Fake editorial: drop caps, quote walls, "Vol. 01".

**Test:** the grid can be redrawn from the render alone and argues every size and position; with color removed, the piece still reads.
