# Material and reading order · Swiss, Müller-Brockmann

A visual world for this tea identity: let rock be felt through material, and let the order of naming, sensing and preparing govern how its messages are read.

> Use alone: read Overview and Images before any value. All reference sizes use S = 1000, where S is the short side; multiply by S ÷ 1000. Recompute the grid from W × H. Content lives in the brief, never in this file. Build one leading form per unit, with supporting information kept quiet.

## Overview

Swiss, Josef Müller-Brockmann — Grid Systems and the musica viva method: build the grid from the format and the reading relationships from the subject; make a line reveal a real connection, boundary or change of state; never turn the construction grid into wallpaper.
World: one tea identity, its material stories, bilingual naming, packaging, preparation and visual standards, across a portfolio, package, poster or information sheet. Keep the shared axes, paired type, material palette and line roles; let each piece bring its own object and reading task. Translate unfamiliar content by asking what must be identified, understood and done; do not force the brewing sequence onto unrelated material.
Method: first write the reader's actions and distinguish peers, transitions and dependencies. Select the relationship that matters most. Place its subjects on the shared grid, then draw only the line that makes that relationship perceptible. Use a quiet rule for a boundary; use one connected editorial figure when linking regions, hanging entries, pointing to an object or tracing changing states is the composition. Proof the whole sequence at thumbnail size and each page at reading size.
Amplitude: full on the material image and the one relational figure; restrained on apparatus. Speak principally in the reader's language. Translations identify, they do not automatically outrank.
Anti-reference: a generic portfolio cover, repeated boxed cards, blank packaging labels, diagrams whose path expresses no relationship, and small grey prose used to preserve a spacious appearance.

## Images

Use the subject's material: stratified dark stone, uncoated paper, ceramic, clear amber liquid and natural shadow. Keep a consistent low-saturation photographic register, with amber confined to liquid. Obtain actual material photographs when supplied. Generated concept imagery is allowed for atmosphere and concept mockups only; identify it in the piece's caption and provenance record. It is never evidence of a manufactured object or a named place. Let a leading material image meet the trim: use full bleed or a trim-connected edge panel, full-height in landscape and full-width when stacked, with a running provenance band cut at the common footer axis when needed. Recompute the crop and move apparatus to a stable ground. Crop one decisive detail; restore its identity in the adjacent caption. Derive the cover scale from the source's actual pixel ratio, then crop the surplus at a declared focal point; horizontal and vertical scale remain identical. Check the exported PPTX's source crop and frame as well as the rendered image. Flat packaging labels and explanatory type remain live when they carry the design argument. No invented documentary attribution.

## Layout

Grid system: column — twelve shared tracks, used as a continuous field rather than twelve containers.
At S = 1000 use margin 100 and gutter 30. For twelve-track landscape, usable width U = W − 2m, column c = (U − 11g) ÷ 12; zero-based track k starts at m + k(c + g). Reserve the top 12% and bottom 10% for quiet orientation only when orientation is needed. In this landscape grid, begin a right-edge image at track six and use the five-track region on the left for reading; end a left-edge image two gutters before track five, where the seven-track reference region begins. Place only the apparatus belonging to each region within it. Type-only references may use two equal six-track regions. Portrait uses six tracks and square eight, with the same margins, gutters and column formula adjusted to n tracks; stack an edge image and reading region rather than reuse the landscape panel counts. Align text starts, image edges, rule endpoints and captions to the selected tracks.
Place unused space as one or two coherent intervals around the lead. A blank track is not a vacancy. For an ordered contents page, entries may descend in equal steps along consecutive columns; this encodes chapter progression, never importance.

## Typography

Use Inter for Latin and Noto Sans SC for Chinese, both OFL 1.1; pair their optical density and baseline rather than their nominal em. The large Latin editorial voice is a real Light cut at weight 300; use its static Inter Light face in PowerPoint and inspect the exported glyphs for fallback. Chinese identity marks and statement leads use Noto Sans SC 700; Latin identity names use Inter 500, at one sixth of the Chinese em in a stacked lockup or one fifth beside it, with the navigation role as their smallest reading size. Set reading text flush left and ragged right; specimen marks retain their own construction. Keep the variable sources, static instances and licenses in the delivery bundle; PDF is the portable viewing proof. These faces deliberately replace the reference's system-font pair.
At S = 1000 use identity 240/700 for the primary cover, chapter 200/300, page title 108/300, statement 80/700, subhead 35/600, body 28/400, bilingual subtitle 30/400, field label 24/400, specimen label 22/400, navigation 19/500 and micro 18/400. Keep the cover identity at its role size and recompose if its measure does not fit. In a label, application or font specimen the mark is an exhibit instead: derive its size from that object's assigned span, maintaining its proportions and reading floors; the cover role does not also constrain it. Body leading 1.5–1.6, labels 1.25, display 1.0–1.12. Chinese and Latin within one sentence use the same body role. Break Chinese at semantic boundaries. Read-alone deck: smallest live text is the micro role, never a smaller export footer. Hand-set long lines; do not compress characters or use fit-to-box shrinking.
Measure gaps from rendered ink, not adjacent text-box coordinates. Between a heavy Chinese identity and its Latin name leave at least one quarter of the Chinese em. Between a page title and its translated subtitle leave at least one third of the title em. Keep at least one body line of clear space between distinct reading groups, and more at a section change; a lockup is one group, not permission for its glyphs to touch. Inside a small label specimen, scale the lockup and its internal gaps together. Check these relationships in the actual PowerPoint render, not solely in HTML.

## Colors

Use primary charcoal for identity and reading, neutral white for the main ground, stock for uncoated-paper samples, stone for material swatches, muted for secondary text, line for nonessential quiet boundaries, and moss for the editorial figure or one selected action. White may reverse on charcoal or moss. Stock, stone and line never carry small white text. In material photographs preserve the amber of tea without treating it as a second graphic accent. Reverse the complete foreground palette on a uniformly dark photographic field. A mixed-light photograph may have two stable reading regions: use charcoal on the light region and white on the dark region, with one polarity per text block. Inspect every text region on the final composite; move text or recompose the crop if its ground is unstable. Put small provenance text on an opaque running band connected to the trim when a photograph cannot provide a stable ground: white under a light panel, charcoal under a full photographic field, aligned with the common footer. This is one edge band, never a floating plate.

## Shapes

Three layers: invisible construction grid; quiet typographic rules; at most one leading connected editorial figure when a real relationship calls for it. Keep rules at 1.1 reference units and figures at 2.0; ring stroke shares the figure weight. Use squared ends. Group reading text with space and open rules; keep material swatches and flat native label specimens as exhibits. No shadow, rounded plate or ornamental stroke.
A rule's span identifies its scope: a local text rule stops at that text region, a section rule spans its peers, a footer rule ends the content area. Start and end on shared axes. Draw only boundaries that separate named groups; do not close them into cells. Keep at least one label-height between a rule and unrelated reading text. Quiet rules use line; the leading figure uses moss on white or white on dark.
The editorial figure has one job per unit. Link joins two content regions with one continuous course, bending only at their actual boundary; hang the groups from its levels and keep type clear. Cut hangs ordered entries from one common strand with unequal connected drops ending at their entry anchors; never add a horizontal lattice. Point leads to one labelled target and may terminate in one ring; connect exactly to the ring edge and keep the ring clear of supporting copy. String connects every entry to real ordered states; a change in height encodes a named change of state, while equal horizontal spacing encodes order only. Put its bends on the state nodes themselves. Self-ordering entries require an additional named relationship carried by the strand; otherwise use a quiet typographic sequence. Use 0°, 45° and 90° segments. Do not mimic a physical fold on a screen or imply numerical growth with an unlabelled rising path.

## Forms

- Cover: identify the tea world with its name and scope; add one Link only when it joins a named relationship; refuse a larger generic category word.
- Statement: one consequential sentence isolated by space; refuse a second argument.
- Exhibit: one material object or application, large and identified; refuse a competing montage.
- Argument: a judgment title, its visible specimen and implication; refuse an explanation without an inspectable example.
- Sequence: use String when a named relationship beyond self-ordering labels needs a path, otherwise set a quiet ordered sequence; refuse a decorative flow curve.
- Comparison: peers share rows and scale, with direct labels; refuse differences encoded only by hue.
- Exercise: state the sensory task, then reserve an open writing area inside the same margins; derive its size from the answers requested, without filling it with apparatus.
- Collection: use shared columns and selective rules; refuse a complete lattice without a lookup need.
- Opener: one chapter title and one short promise, on the recurring title and index axes; refuse body paragraphs.
- Reference: use guessable order, typographic levels and quiet rules; refuse a leading figure that competes with lookup.
- Close: restate the claim already demonstrated, or let the last specimen finish it; refuse an unrelated slogan.

## Variations

Permit a full-bleed material image at the shift from a chapter promise to its exhibit; keep the orientation axes and apply the photographic region rules in Colors. Permit a macro crop where a material detail is the evidence; identify the whole in its caption. Permit one editorial figure at a relationship-bearing turn; the line's job changes with the reader task, not arbitrarily by page. Sparse openers are repeated base forms, not automatically the named Pause variation. A palette swatch and a reverse wordmark sample do not change the register of the whole unit.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Verify every fixed string against the brief. Mark concept samples as concepts; retain asset provenance and font licenses.
- Keep reading text live, static and within the page; use 4.5:1 for ordinary text and 3:1 for large text and essential graphical cues. Inspect text over photographs at final size.
- School test: redraw the common axes from the render; remove hue and the sequence must still read. Remove a quiet rule and a local boundary should become less clear; remove a leading figure and the relationship should lose its composition.
- Caricature: stone-and-tea mood pictures attached to a generic portfolio, a full grid of hairlines, or a wandering line with numbered text beside it.
- Do not infer manufacturing, geography, elapsed time or growth from a concept image or an unscaled diagram.
