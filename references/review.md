# Review — judged from outside

The context that made the work is a poor judge of it. Review comes from contexts that did not make it, and judges the picture, not the files that describe it. Finding faults is easier than making, so critics can run on a lighter model.

| Role | Sees | Never sees |
|---|---|---|
| Critics, three to five | Renders at viewing size, the brief, the rubric | The maker's reasoning, the skeleton, the intended hand |
| Judge | The critics' points, the renders, the rubric | The maker's reasoning |
| Process reader | The skeleton, the brief, the renders | The maker's reasoning |
| Maker | The judge's kept points | — |

Where helpers cannot be spawned, run each role as a clean pass that reads only what its row allows.

## Critic

> You did not make this work. Prove it falls short. For each fault: the rubric line it breaks, where it is visible, the one change that fixes it. Look at viewing size, then as thumbnails. Faults only.

Give each critic one angle: the reader at distance; the school and its master; the idea and its interchangeability; craft and floors; the set as a contact sheet.

## Judge

> Keep a point only if it names a rubric line, the fault is visible, and the fix breaks no other line. Merge, then rank, floors first. Then answer: would a master of the claimed school sign it? Is it interchangeable with a rival subject? Which line would the most valuable change serve?

The maker decides; a point can be right and still not worth acting on. Fix, render, and review again while a kept point is within reach. Report what remains.

## Process reader

> A right-looking result reached by habit fails. Check that the grid is derived from the format, the idea from the subject, the palette and faces from the hand and the world, and that the method in the file is visible in the render. Name every value that looks inherited.

## Rubric

Give every reviewer this, and nothing else from the skill:

1. The philosophy and the floors (SKILL.md).
2. The claimed school's test and caricature: the dialect file, or the theme's anti-patterns.
3. The surface's "Done means".
4. Where unexamined choices land, listed below.

### Where unexamined choices land

Measured on Claude Opus and Sonnet without a skill, November 2026. None is banned; each needs a reason from the subject or the hand. Re-measure on every model release.

- A warm cream or greige ground; black with one vermilion or rust accent.
- Newsreader, IBM Plex Sans and Mono, Archivo, Jost, Inter, Noto Serif SC.
- Tracked uppercase kickers, 01/02/03, monospace labels, hairlines over a split footer, middle-dot strings.
- Oversized numerals as hero; KPI trios; a navy closing slide carrying the ask.
- The subject's most literal pictogram: a clock for time, a plate for ceramics, a ledger for accounting.
- A named style's palette and primitives without its method.
- Objects drawn in CSS with soft shadows, standing in for material.

## Comparing versions

Label renders neutrally and shuffle them; keep the key out of reach. Per purpose, ask which the reviewer would sign, and score authorship from 1 to 10: made by a particular designer for this particular subject.
