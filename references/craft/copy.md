# Copy

Copy is design material: the title shapes the layout, the sentences set its rhythm. AI copy is as recognizable as AI layout and ruins an otherwise good page.

- **Words come before layout, not after.** An argument needs claim, evidence, implication, action; a claim without evidence becomes a question or goes. Covers identify; reference units label and order.
- **Facts are fixed; wording is a service.** Where editing is allowed, a vague title becomes a claim, a paragraph a line, a line a slogan; names, dates, numbers, places, quotations, and required text stay exact. Edit for the message, never only to rescue a layout; disclose substantive changes. Verbatim means verbatim.
- **An argument title states a judgment, not a topic.** One judgment in 12–24 Chinese or 40–70 Latin characters, at most two lines; read in order, the titles form the summary. Covers, tables, reference, and navigation keep descriptive titles.
- **Concrete, not inflated.** Replace each tell with the action, object, quantity, or date; if nothing concrete remains, the sentence had no information.
  - Words: elevate, seamless, unleash, empower, revolutionize, cutting-edge, game-changing, unlock; 赋能, 一站式, 打造, 引领, 闭环, 抓手, 颠覆.
  - Shapes: "not just X, it's Y"; three adjectives in a row; "Imagine a world where"; rhetorical questions; "In today's fast-paced world"; "We believe".
  - Marks: dramatic em dashes, exclamation marks, suspense ellipses, emoji. Buttons: "Learn more", "Get started", "Submit".
- **One idea per sentence, not stacked nouns.** Active voice; review sentences past 35 Chinese characters or 25 English words.
- **Overload is solved by grouping, not by shrinking type.** Regroup, reword, or paginate within scope.
- **One primary language, not decorative bilingualism.** A second language only for real bilingual readers, one line below and two sizes smaller, rewritten rather than translated word for word. Ornamental English labels on Chinese pages go.
- **Interface words name the action, not the mood.** Buttons are verb plus object; errors say what happened, how to fix it, and what was lost, without apology; empty states say what will appear; dangerous confirmations state the consequence.
- **Voice comes from sentence length, verbs, and density, not adjectives.** Each theme and dialect names its voice.
