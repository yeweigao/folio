# Grid

The grid fixes how things relate before deciding what they look like. Without one, AI layouts fall back to equal thirds and centered stacks.

- **The grid is solved from the format, not picked from a preset.** For equal tracks: content = n × column + (n − 1) × gutter; zero-based column k starts at margin + k × (column + gutter). The surface offers starting dimensions.
- **Tracks are construction lines, not columns to fill.** Equal spans relate peers; unequal spans express priority. Subdivide only for an alignment the content needs; an unused track is not a vacancy.
- **Asymmetry carries priority, not novelty.** Unequal spans for a lead; equal spans for real peers. Never invent priority to avoid symmetry.
- **Axes are shared, not accidental.** Image edges, title starts, captions, and sources repeat the alignments their relationships need.
- **Space comes from a system, not isolated guesses.** Declared steps distinguish within-group, between-group, and section spacing; the theme sets their ratios. Baselines follow leading.
- **Alignment is optical, not arithmetic.** Round and pointed forms overshoot; quotes and points hang; large titles compensate for side bearing.
- **Breakpoints are where content breaks, not device names.** Reflow when measure or relationships fail; the surface sets viewport and navigation requirements.

| Reading need | Composition | Invariant |
|---|---|---|
| Compare peers | Equal spans, criteria in shared rows | Row alignment, units, scales |
| Read with evidence | Unequal spans for reading and support | Text and evidence share an anchor |
| Annotate | Narrow note track beside the reading column | Each note beside its passage |
| Argue | Claim, evidence, implication | A shared reading axis, claim before evidence |
| Survey | Contact sheet | Comparable crops, aligned caption baselines |
| Carry a brand | A unit derived from the actual mark | Its proportions and clear space |
