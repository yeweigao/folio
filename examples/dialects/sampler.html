Nine dialects, one mother tongue

Generated from references/themes/swiss/dialects/. Render the same hardest unit in a dialect before choosing it; each card states that dialect's test and its caricature.

Bauhaus — construction from elements

**Primaries together, not one accent.** Red, yellow, and blue with black, on white or off-white paper; no other hue.

Test: Remove the type, and the shapes still compose the subject on the grid.

Caricature: Shapes scattered off the grid with no relation; pastel or teal "Bauhaus"; gradients, shadows, rounded UI cards; geometry pasted beside an ordinary layout.

Brodovitch — the fashion magazine

**Type and image collide, not sit side by side.** A Didot or Bodoni display word covers, cuts, or yields to the photograph; a text block may echo the figure's line.

Test: Remove the type, and the photograph loses its composition; remove the photograph, and the type loses its reason.

Caricature: Fake luxury: gold gradients, rose gold, glass, script fonts, a centered serif logo over a stock model; Bodoni as body text.

Swiss Brutal — the structure shown

**The grid is visible, not implied, and quieter than the text.** Hairlines at one weight in a mid grey, never full ink; a heavier ink rule only opens or closes a table; zero radius, no shadows, no gradients; a box only when it is the real structure.

Test: Every visible line is a real boundary and every band holds working content; squint, and the text reads before the rules.

Caricature: Terminal cosplay: green on black, scanlines, noise; neo-brutalist stickers with thick outlines, fluorescent blocks, and hard shadows; ugliness on purpose; broken layouts called raw.

De Stijl — the grid as the picture

**The grid is the picture, not the scaffold.** Black bars on the tracks divide the field; text and images live inside its planes.

Test: Resize or recolor one plane, and the balance of the whole shifts.

Caricature: A card grid filled red, yellow, and blue; a black lattice laid over an ordinary page; Mondrian as a border.

ma 間 — the charged interval

**Emptiness is a vessel, not spacing.** Content gathers so one large interval stays whole; it frames the content, never replaces it.

Test: The emptiness changes how the subject is felt, and the subject rewards the attention.

Caricature: Zen props, stones, bamboo, brush ink; an empty page with nothing worth seeing; type too small to read.

Memphis — the party on the grid

**Color clashes on purpose, not one accent.** Several saturated hues with black and white, chosen as relations.

Test: The clash changes how the subject is felt; remove it and the character goes.

Caricature: Squiggle confetti on everything, black outlines everywhere, an 80s pastel palette, Memphis as wallpaper behind a Swiss page.

Polish Poster School — the authored metaphor

**The image interprets, not reports.** One visual metaphor states a view of the subject; it never poses as evidence.

Test: The picture says something about this subject that a photograph of it could not.

Caricature: A brush font, distressed paper, and a surreal object unrelated to the subject.

Swiss Punk — the grid tested

**The grid is tested, not obeyed.** Rotation, stepped baselines, and displacement run through the piece as its grammar.

Test: The typographic relations themselves express the subject; remove one tilted word and the dialect survives.

Caricature: Glitch filters, random distress, illegible stacks; a word repeated and spread down the page like an echo; one rotated word on an otherwise Swiss page.

Ulm — the system decides

**The system is the design, not the page.** Rules for every case come first; each unit is one application another designer could extend.

Test: A stranger knows what to do next, and a designer could apply the rules to a new case.

Caricature: Sports pictograms and the Munich palette pasted onto an ordinary page; UI line icons; three kinds of arrow.
