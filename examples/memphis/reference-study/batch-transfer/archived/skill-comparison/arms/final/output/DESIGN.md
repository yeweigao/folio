# Graphic Memphis · stepped tile identity

## Overview

Build cultural and art-activity materials from two interdependent parts: a large, uninterrupted reading field and a colourful assembly of geometric tiles that grows from its edges. The tiles form a little constructed landscape, with a few recognizable flower and orbit signs. They are an identity vocabulary, not an illustration of each new subject.

Use the graphic Memphis tile-system method: construct the kit, join it into a field suited to the format, and fit the message into the solid ground that remains. Keep the kit's contours, internal proportions, flat colour relationships and blocky display voice while changing the assembly. Do not replace this construction with scattered generic Memphis shapes.

The source establishes a shared visual world across wide displays, ordinary upright pieces, very narrow strips, cards, bags and tags. It establishes no designer attribution, original font identity, production colour specification or universal rule for other Memphis work. The dimensions and colour values below are practical approximations for transferring the visible relationships, not recovered production measurements.

Every piece keeps the clear field, orthogonal tile joins, warm/cool colour collision and compact display hierarchy. Each piece chooses its ground, the location and extent of the tile assembly, the motifs it needs, its title breaks and its information alignment. A small piece need not contain the entire kit.

## Materials

Draw the graphics as crisp, opaque vector shapes. Use solid fills, circular arcs, straight edges and hard clipping. The source does not establish a photographic, textured, shaded or dimensional image treatment. Apparent overlapping colours inside a flower are separate flat colour regions; do not add gradients or glow.

Treat any organization mark as supplied content: use its approved artwork, small and monochrome, on a clear field. No reusable wordmark asset is supplied by this extraction. Omit it if none is provided. The presentation-board grey surround, product hardware, barcodes and social-platform marks are not graphic background materials or obligatory ornaments.

## Layout

### Construction and scale

Use a hierarchical reading field bordered by a locally modular mosaic. Let `W × H` be the finished face and `S = min(W,H)`. Begin with a reading inset of about `0.06–0.09S`; deeper insets are appropriate for a sparse information card. Pattern can meet the trim while text remains inside this inset.

Choose one base tile dimension `m` for each connected assembly. Most cells are squares; adjacent halves, doubles and low rectangular subdivisions provide the changes of rhythm. Do not stretch a finished motif to an arbitrary rectangle. Build another cell arrangement instead. Across a normal edge assembly, `m` is roughly `0.12–0.22S`; in a narrow motif-led strip it can approach the full width. A large panel can combine `m` and `m/2` cells, but each local run must reveal shared seams and alignments. The evidence supports a coherent tiled construction, not an exactly recoverable single grid extending through every item.

Join neighbouring cells without gutters or outlines. Let curved figures be clipped by their rectangular cells and let neighbouring solid colours create the boundaries. Rectangular colour fields may span several cells. Use half-module steps for ledges and changes in height. Some end forms may protrude above the mosaic: a quarter-round cap, flower, triangular teeth or small orbit sign. Their bases or centres relate to a tile edge or seam.

Make one connected assembly do most of the graphic work. Keep most reading on one unbroken white or blue ground. A small number of isolated marks can bridge the gap between mosaic and type; a page-wide scatter of equal accents cannot.

### Recomposition by available space

Choose a construction according to the usable reading area, not solely the page orientation:

- **Wide message field:** build a low mosaic along the bottom, with one or both ends rising in unequal stepped stacks. The middle remains open above the low band. The base commonly occupies about one fifth to one quarter of the height, while an end stack can rise substantially higher. Put the headline in the continuous clear field; keep smaller identity information away from it.
- **Compact poster or upright face:** let a lower band and/or one side stack anchor the field. Reserve enough height above it for a stacked headline and, when required, a short text block. A white face can instead hang the mosaic from an upper corner and place a single warm flower below it. These are alternate organizations, not parts to accumulate.
- **Quiet information card:** give the text a generous plain field and reduce the mosaic to a narrow edge column. The decorative column need not become a bottom band as well. Keep its module large enough to recognize the flower and nested arcs.
- **Very narrow strip:** make a large, full-width motif panel at one end, often close to half the total length. Stack title, support and practical information in the remaining solid field. This large motif panel is a property of the strip construction; it is not a rule that every portrait begins with imagery.
- **Small tag:** simplify the assembly to a short transverse band or a small stepped base, retaining recognizable full cells. Stack the title and its language companion; do not shrink a wide composition wholesale.

The reference permits both white and blue versions of similar formats. Ground choice is independent of orientation. Pattern placement can switch ends or sides when the text and format need it; preserve the tile construction and the clear reading field. Do not turn one observed arrangement into a mandatory frame.

### Space and reading order

Let the title be the principal reading mass. Separate its language blocks from each other by a visible gap of roughly `0.25–0.45` of the CJK title's ink height. Leave at least half that ink height between title and a nearby graphic unless a deliberate seam defines their relationship. A flower may be isolated in a larger quiet interval; do not fill that interval with extra decoration.

Ordinary stacked title lockups are centred as compact units; longer explanatory blocks and practical information are flush left. Align related groups by their actual ink, not by unequal text-box bounds. On a narrow strip, a fine rule can separate the explanation from a compact bottom information group. It is functional furniture, not a line to repeat throughout the mosaic.

## Typography

### Display voice

The display is heavy, compact, square and visibly stepped. Latin display uses uppercase pixel-like lettering with constant chunky strokes, orthogonal notches and square counters. CJK display has dense dark strokes, emphatic right angles and small staircase details rather than rounded terminals. Keep letters upright: the energy comes from the construction around them, not from rotating the words.

The original faces are unidentified. Use these supplied licensed materials by filename; none is an identification of a font in the source:

| Role | Material and treatment |
|---|---|
| Latin display | `pixel.woff2`, the supplied authored surrogate, SIL OFL 1.1. Use its native outlines; it is a substitute rather than an original-reference font. |
| CJK display and reading | `cjk.woff2`, supplied study substitute, SIL OFL 1.1. Preserve readable character structure. The metadata does not establish a matching stepped display treatment. |
| Latin explanatory text | `sans-400.woff2`, supplied study substitute, SIL OFL 1.1. |
| Short labels and emphatic small text | `sans-700.woff2`, supplied study substitute, SIL OFL 1.1. |
| Compact practical figures | `condensed.woff2`, supplied study substitute, SIL OFL 1.1; retain upright, dense figures. |

For a closer CJK display match, use the supplied CJK glyphs as the semantic base for deliberate large-title lettering. On an approximately 12–16-unit square design grid, keep major strokes about two units thick, make counters rectilinear and introduce one-unit steps at selected turns. Keep distinguishing radicals and counters intact. Apply the treatment consistently across the title; do not pixelate body text. This is a production adaptation from the visible form, not a claim that the original lettering used this grid. If custom lettering is unavailable, identify the CJK face as a visible substitution rather than pretending it reproduces the source.

Optional title accents are a few tiny detached filled or open squares, about `0.08–0.13em`, near a line's upper or terminal edge. They read as pixel fragments of the title. Use them sparingly; they are not punctuation replacements or a background dot texture.

### Hierarchy and fitting

Size the principal CJK title to a clear span, normally about `55–80%` of the available reading width. Its compact line block should carry the greatest weight in that field. Use short phrase-based lines with leading around `1.05–1.12em` and near-normal character spacing. Rebreak a long title, enlarge its field or reduce surrounding ornament before reducing it to the size of supporting copy.

For a stacked bilingual lockup, the Latin display cap height is approximately `0.40–0.60` of the CJK character height. Keep the Latin block narrow and dense beneath it, often in two short lines. In a wide face with ample horizontal room, the two language blocks can sit side by side and the Latin can rise to about `0.65–0.75` of the CJK height. Separate these peer blocks with a thin upright blue rule; let each block align toward that divider. Do not force this two-column treatment into a narrow format. A single-language piece uses the relevant display voice without an empty companion block.

Set explanatory copy in small, ordinary sans or CJK sans with clear strokes, approximately `0.16–0.22` of title height and `1.45–1.65em` leading. Short slogan-like secondary groups may be slightly larger and bold, with a small regular translation directly beneath. Practical figures sit between body and title in scale, with a much smaller supporting line when content requires one. Keep no more than four distinct reading levels. Final minimum size follows the actual viewing distance; the source board does not establish production-size floors.

Preserve body text as live text. Keep display lettering accessible through an equivalent text layer or document structure when the medium permits it. Confirm glyph coverage from the actual supplied files before setting new languages; the metadata does not prove coverage or every available weight.

## Colors

Use white and a saturated medium blue as the two principal reading grounds. On white, use near-black for CJK headlines, body and organization marks; blue may carry the Latin display. On blue, reset all reading roles to white. These inversions are part of the system.

Inside the mosaic, oppose orange/red to blue and pale blue, then introduce yellow, blush and cream as distinct bright or soft fields. Yellow is a structural shape colour as well as the centre of the flower signs. Pale colours provide visible negative shapes within the tiles; they are not a substitute for the main white reading field. Reserve pink/lilac/cyan accents for the very narrow edge-stripe bundle. Keep black largely in reading text, flower dots and open-circle signs; do not outline every filled shape.

## Shapes

Normalize each recipe to its local square cell `m`. The internal counts below describe the signs; the number of signs or cells used on a piece remains free. Keep circles circular and preserve proportions when scaling. Quarter-turn rotations and mirrorings are available for tile motifs; type stays upright. Crop motifs at cell or trim boundaries deliberately, but retain a complete example of a complicated flower somewhere when it is a featured sign.

### 1. Opposed half-disc tile

Divide a square into two equal upright halves. Place a semicircle in each half, with its diameter on the square's outer left or right edge, so the two convex curves face inward. Each outer semicircle has radius `0.5m`; inset a second semicircle at the same centre to leave a broad band about `0.10–0.13m` thick. One side uses blue fill inside an orange band on cream; the other uses pale-blue fill inside a middle-blue band on blush. Keep the two colour families distinct. This paired, inward-facing construction is more specific than an isolated generic rainbow.

### 2. Nested corner arcs

Fill a square with orange, then draw three evenly spaced concentric quarter-circle stripes from one corner. Use cream stripes about `0.045–0.06m` thick, separated by orange gaps of similar or slightly greater width; the outside stripe approaches the opposite edges. The same construction can use blue stripes on orange at a smaller cell scale. Rotate the corner as needed, but keep one centre, regular spacing and a hard square crop. Do not replace it with a many-line fine rainbow or outline the square.

### 3. Quarter-round and semicircle blocks

Put a large solid quarter-disc, radius `m`, against two adjacent sides of a square, leaving a contrasting ground outside its curve. Orange/yellow against blue and pale blue/blush against blue are characteristic pairings. Half-disc crops supply taller convex edges. For the protruding orange end-cap, use a quarter-round top attached to a vertical rectangular stem: one top corner is fully round, the other side remains straight. It grows directly from the stack beneath it.

### 4. Red flower sign

Construct a compact six-lobed red flower, approximately circular overall. Arrange six equal rounded petal lobes at equal angles around a shared centre, overlapping into one continuous scalloped silhouette. Keep shallow valleys; do not make six detached circles. Put one black dot in each lobe on a ring about `0.32–0.35` of the flower's diameter from the centre. Each dot is about `0.12–0.14` of that diameter. Add a small scalloped golden-yellow centre, about one third of the overall diameter. This is a six-dot flower, not a face. Use a white or blush square behind it when embedded in the mosaic.

### 5. Warm four-lobed flower sign

Make a roughly square flower from four broad overlapping round lobes arranged two by two. The outline has rounded corners and slight inward pinches at the middles of its four sides. Use a golden-yellow outer silhouette. Nest a smaller amber version inside it, then layer four orange petal-like ovals pointing outward from the centre along the horizontal and vertical axes. Let the overlaps make compact darker-orange regions. At the centre put a tiny cream four-arm mark with an orange/red dot. Keep the centre much smaller than the petals, approximately one fifth of the flower's width for the cream mark and one tenth for the dot. This flower is warm throughout, with no black perimeter dots. It can stand alone in the plain field or sit on the mosaic's top edge.

### 6. Diagonally divided disc

Fit a disc about `0.9m` across into a square field. Split it into two solid halves along a 45-degree diameter: blue on one side, golden yellow on the other. Place a small orange disc at the shared centre, approximately `0.16–0.18m` in diameter. Keep the diagonal as one straight seam; there is no outline, shading or offset hub.

A second, smaller disc belongs to a vertical tile seam: centre a circle about `0.5m` across on that seam and split it vertically there. Use an orange half against a cool half, with the neighbouring square fields continuing behind it. The disc bridges two fields; it is not an arbitrary floating dot. A short group of tiny orange/blue rectangular blocks can sit beneath it on a shared horizontal line.

### 7. Leaves and triangular seams

Use small pointed oval leaves made from two circular arcs, roughly twice as long as wide. Arrange a pair around a shared tile corner or seam, with their pointed ends meeting; blue and pale-blue leaves can oppose a yellow or blue quarter-round field. Keep them as a compact joint, not a botanical branch.

Build the triangular rhythms from 45-degree right triangles rather than arbitrary jagged polygons. A sawtooth group uses repeated orange right triangles with equal base and height, a shared baseline and no gaps. A low faceted band alternates blue and pale-blue triangles with peaks half as high as their full bases. The resulting diamonds and mountains connect to cell seams. A small freestanding diamond is two triangles meeting along a horizontal middle seam, blue above and pale blue below.

### 8. Bars, rails and rounded slab

A short rectangular tile can carry equal-width alternating orange and yellow upright bars. Use a small number of broad bars: roughly six to ten across a square-width run, clipped to one common top and bottom. Preserve their pitch within an assembly rather than squeezing the repeat independently into each gap.

An outer vertical edge can carry a much finer bundle of roughly four or five parallel colour rails in blue, pale blue/cyan, pink and lilac. This is a narrow finishing edge, not an all-over stripe field.

For a large soft-ended red slab, use a rectangle about twice as wide as high with two diagonally opposite corners rounded to approximately the rectangle's full height; the other two corners remain square. Place it on blush or pink. Its two curved ends and long flat edges distinguish it from a generic fully rounded pill.

### 9. Open-circle cluster and star

The open-circle sign is six equal empty rings in two rows of three. Ring diameter is about `0.15–0.18m`, stroke about `0.02m`, with clear gaps about half a ring diameter. Use black on white or white on blue. Preserve the 2 × 3 relationship; keep this small cluster beside the mosaic or in an open interval, not as a full dot background.

A blue radial burst may appear as a single small counterpoint in a white field: a compact star of about twelve sharp points, alternating an outer radius with an inner radius around half as large. It has no outline or central hole. Its exact point count is less identity-bearing than the flowers and is not established as a repeat system.

## Forms and variations

For an announcement, make the title and its language companion a single clear lockup, then fit practical details into a separate quiet group. For a statement or sparse card, allow much more empty ground and reduce the graphic assembly to an edge. For a small object face, simplify the vocabulary while preserving the scale of its recognizable signs. For a series, change tile selection, ground and assembly silhouette together so the set has variation without new shape families.

Keep motif selection unequal: broad arcs and colour masses establish the mosaic, flowers are occasional distinctive events, and thin rails or rings are small punctuation. A motif can become the main image in a narrow strip; it does not have to become the principal image on every format. The reference does not establish dense documents, interaction, animation, charts or a data-colour system. Extending to those uses requires new decisions; retain readable solid fields rather than claiming decorative circles and bars encode information.

## Delivery check

- Can the piece be recognized from the stepped, joined field and the two flower constructions before its words are read?
- Are type and mosaic composed as related masses, with the reading field still continuous?
- Are the six-lobed dotted red flower and four-lobed layered warm flower visibly different signs?
- Do tile seams, circles, pattern pitch and the hard crops remain coherent after recomposition?
- Are title hierarchy and clear text grounds preserved in both white and blue versions?
- Has the new format changed the assembly instead of merely reducing an earlier layout?
- Are all words readable and complete, with font substitutions and unverified production details declared?

Avoid confetti, black outlines around every primitive, pastel-only recolouring, decorative pattern behind paragraphs, photographic filler, fake texture, shadows and compulsory identical bottom strips. Shared colours alone do not reproduce this identity.
