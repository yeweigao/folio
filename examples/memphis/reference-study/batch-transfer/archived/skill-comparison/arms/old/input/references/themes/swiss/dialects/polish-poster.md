# Polish Poster School — the authored metaphor

**Invocation:** 波兰海报学派 Polish Poster School — Make work as Tomaszewski's school made its posters: two things the subject joins fused into one hand-made image, the title drawn in the same hand, the facts demoted to a quiet block; never an image that merely shows the subject.

Warsaw, 1950s–70s: Tomaszewski, Trepkowski, Lenica, Cieślewicz, Świerzy, Starowieyski, Młodożeniec, for film, theatre, opera, circus and exhibitions; Tomaszewski taught the school at the Warsaw Academy of Fine Arts. The commissions left the image to the artist, so each poster became an author's reading of its subject, posted in the street. Spoken in Swiss, the Polish poster gives the image to the author and the facts to the grid.

- **The image is a metaphor, not a report.** Two things the subject joins become one form, as Trepkowski's bomb holds a ruined city. It states the author's reading and never poses as evidence.
- **One author's hand, shown.** Gouache, a swelling line, photomontage or cut paper, in one named hand with its marks visible; never a blend of hands.
- **Letters are drawn into the picture; the facts are set.** The title is written in the same hand and medium and takes part in the image; every fact also stands as live type.
- **The idea composes, not the grid.** The metaphor takes the field at the scale it needs, centred if it must; the grid holds only the facts.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Henryk Tomaszewski — the film posters (1947–) and *Henry Moore* (1959).** Find the one witty image the subject can be said in. Paint it in gouache on a muted ground with as few strokes as will hold it, and write the title in the same hand. Suits subjects sayable as one ironic image. Never: a second idea.
- **Tadeusz Trepkowski — *Nie!* (1952).** Reduce the subject to one silhouette that holds its opposite inside it, black on white, near-wordless. Suits protest, warning and grief. Never: an explanation beside the symbol.
- **Jan Lenica — *Wozzeck* (1964) and the opera posters.** Draw the figure in one continuous contour that swells into bands of flat colour, distorted until the feeling shows. Suits subjects with violence, absurdity or music inside them. Never: an outline of one weight.
- **Roman Cieślewicz — the photomontages (1960s–).** Cut real photographs, enlarge their screen and collide them over dense black, so the image argues by montage. Suits subjects with evidence to turn. Never: montage as decoration.

**Hand-made material.** Scan real hand work where you have it. Without it, generate the image with the hand and the medium named in the prompt, and declare it; with neither, a labelled slot of the exact crop holds the place. Code sets the facts, rules and type, never the hand; a brush font is not a hand.

**Swiss keeps:** the facts (date, place, credits) flush left on the tracks, in one grotesque, beneath or beside the image.

**Test:** The picture says something about this subject that a photograph of it could not, and says it once.

**Caricature:** A brush font, distressed paper, a surreal object unrelated to the subject; every Polish trick in one picture.
