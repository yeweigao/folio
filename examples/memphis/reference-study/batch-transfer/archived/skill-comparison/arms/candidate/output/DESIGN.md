# Art-activity identity · graphic Memphis tile construction

Use this system for a related family of cultural activity graphics, covers, small printed objects and information pieces. Build a bright geometric assembly against an uninterrupted reading field. The identity lies in the assembly's contours, its restricted motif vocabulary and its square, stepped display lettering, as well as its colours.

## Overview

Construct a kit of flat square and half-square cells, then join them into a connected border, tower, band or low landscape. Let a few complete shapes escape from that construction into the clear field. Compose the title and information as deliberate solid-ground groups; do not distribute geometric confetti through the text.

Keep the palette, motif anatomy, hard joins, display hierarchy and pattern-to-reading-field relationship throughout the family. Change the assembled silhouette, motif order, occupied edges, number of rows and title arrangement for each surface. The observed pieces are related compositions, not reductions of one master layout.

This is a contemporary graphic Memphis construction. The supplied board establishes no designer attribution. It does not establish a rule for Memphis in general.

## Materials and Images

Draw the ornament as clean vector geometry: opaque fills, true circles, circular arcs, straight cuts and a small number of pointed lenses. Use no photography, material texture, gradients, shadows, bevels or simulated transparency. Colour changes inside a motif are explicit flat shapes.

The ornamental assembly may meet and be cropped by trim. Keep the title, logos and functional information inside the safe area. An isolated flower, burst or diamond should remain complete; crop the modular field rather than accidentally cutting a detached emblem. Preserve circular proportions when adapting formats.

Treat a supplied wordmark as a small identity signature in black on white or white on blue. The reference's custom wordmark is not supplied as a reusable asset here; do not invent a replacement from its letter shapes. Use the new work's supplied identity.

## Layout

Use a **modular ornamental grid beside a hierarchical reading field**. The square module controls the image, not every line of type.

Let `m` be the principal tile side. Choose it from the intended assembly: an edge tower is usually one tile wide, a low landscape a few tiles high, and a narrow strip can hold one large tile across its width. Start by fitting a whole number of modules to the chosen occupied span. Use `m/2` for secondary cells and shallow bands; narrow stripes use a further consistent subdivision. Adjacent tiles share edges with no gutters or outlines. Merge several cells into larger unbroken colour shapes where needed. Do not expose the grid as a wireframe.

For a broad hero, a useful starting module is roughly one fifth of the height. Build an uneven base and unequal side rises around a generous centre. Some sections can be only a shallow triangular base; others climb several cells. Keep the skyline stepped and asymmetric rather than making a symmetrical frame. A quarter-circle cap or small shape can rise above a tower. The top edge need not carry ornament.

Recompose by use and proportion:

- **Broad display surface:** allow a connected low assembly across much of the bottom, with one or both side towers. Keep the title above its central low point. If two language blocks fit at comparable overall heights, place them beside one another, with a single thin vertical blue separator and clear ink-to-rule space. Preserve generous empty field above and around them.
- **Compact landscape information piece:** reserve most of the width for quiet text and let a narrow stack occupy one side; alternatively use the low border construction when the title is the principal content. A side stack is a composition choice, not mandatory on every landscape.
- **Ordinary portrait cover or carrier:** stack language blocks on the clear field. The image may become a substantial transverse band beneath the title, a lower stepped corner, or an upper edge assembly with a complete flower below it. Keep enough uninterrupted field for the reading task. Portrait does not automatically mean a miniature bottom strip.
- **Very narrow, tall ticket-like strip:** enlarge the geometry into the full-width upper region, approximately half the height. Use a separate solid lower reading region with a compact title group, a small paragraph, then operational information. A thin horizontal rule can separate description from the final facts. This upper-image/lower-text partition belongs to the strip format, not to all portraits.
- **Small badge or tag:** simplify the number of cells and text levels. A short header, one title field and one compact image assembly can occupy separate zones. Keep motif features large enough to survive printing; remove motifs before shrinking all of them.

On clear reading fields, start with an inset around `0.3–0.5m`; use smaller proportional insets only on narrow strips. Keep actual title ink at least about `0.25m` from the nearest nonbackground shape. Separate title and translation by roughly half a display line; separate the whole title group from body text by at least a full body line. These are working reconstruction measures, not recovered production specifications.

Align each information group consistently, but choose its alignment for its zone: centred compact titles and ticket information are supported, as are left-aligned prose blocks and side-by-side display blocks. Reserve unoccupied space as one continuous field. Do not fill it simply because a tile fits.

## Shapes

Use the following finite vocabulary. Dimensions below describe a construction that can be redrawn without the board; small optical adjustments are allowed. Rotate or reflect geometric cells in quarter turns. Do not distort them nonuniformly. Flowers remain upright and recognizably complete when detached. Do not require every motif on every piece.

1. **Opposed semicircle cell.** Split a square vertically into two halves. Put the centre of one circle at the midpoint of each outside vertical edge, radius `m/2`, and clip to the square: their inward tips meet at the centre seam. Give each half-disc a thick contrasting outer rim, about `0.1m`, and a solid inner half-disc. A characteristic pair is orange rim/blue interior on cream, opposite medium-blue rim/pale-blue interior on blush. A single rimmed half-disc can also occupy a half-width edge cell.

2. **Corner arc cell.** From one corner of a square, draw four evenly spaced concentric quarter-circle lines and clip at the cell edges. Use line thickness near `0.04m`, radial pitch near `0.17m`, with radii roughly `0.55m` through `1.06m`. The broad gaps matter: this is a few strong curves, not fine hatching. Use cream arcs on orange, orange arcs on blush, or blue arcs on orange in a smaller cell. Rotate the common centre to change direction.

3. **Large corner discs and leaf cuts.** Place a solid quarter-disc in a square with its centre at a corner; combine blue with orange, blush, pale blue or yellow. A secondary curved shape may occupy the remaining corner. A small contrasting pointed lens can sit inside a quarter-disc near the shared corner. Form a lens from two convex circular curves meeting at sharp tips, about twice as long as wide. A blue quarter-disc can become a larger upright sprout: put one orange lens inside it and a second pale-blue lens outside its lower corner, both aimed toward the same root. Keep the root and adjoining baseline exact; add no stem or outline.

4. **Red six-lobed flower.** Form an almost circular scalloped silhouette from six overlapping round lobes spaced equally around a centre, one at the top and one at the bottom. For an overall height `D`, use lobe radius about `0.21D` and lobe-centre radius about `0.29D`. Fill the silhouette vivid red. Put one black disc on each petal axis, diameter about `0.13D`, around a ring of radius `0.32D`. Place a yellow, softly scalloped round centre about `0.35D` across. Preserve six black dots and the compact, stemless silhouette; do not substitute a generic daisy.

5. **Yellow-orange four-lobed flower.** Build a rounded square clover from four overlapping yellow circles, radius about `0.28D`, centred near the four quarter positions. Its outer lobes occupy the corners, with shallow notches at the four cardinal directions. Nest four smaller orange circular lobes toward the centre; use deeper orange-red lens shapes where they meet around the horizontal and vertical axes. Leave a small cream equal-arm cross at the centre, about `0.25D` across, with a red circular dot about `0.1D` across. Keep the nested fourfold construction; this flower has neither the red flower's six dots nor its six-petal contour.

6. **Split circular medallion.** Fit a large disc nearly across a square. Divide it diagonally from lower left to upper right: blue on the upper-left side, yellow on the lower-right. Add a small orange-red hub at the centre, roughly one sixth of the disc diameter. It may sit above a half-height red band with opposite rounded corners on pink, then a plain yellow cell. This is an optional large strip motif, not a compulsory sequence.

7. **Triangles and faceted base.** Repeat three orange right triangles along one horizontal baseline, each about half a module high and wide, with vertical left sides. Separately, build a shallow continuous base from adjoining triangular polygons in blue, medium blue and pale blue, occasionally blush or cyan. Its peaks and shared vertices must meet; vary the triangle widths instead of drawing a row of identical mountains. These are flat colour facets, not shaded solids. A detached diamond consists of a blue upper triangle and pale-blue lower triangle meeting on one horizontal edge.

8. **Stripe inserts.** Use short rectangular bands of equal-width alternating yellow and orange bars, commonly half a module high. Keep the bar pitch consistent within an assembly. A much narrower full-height cap at an outside vertical edge can use parallel blue, pink and lilac strips. Reserve those candy colours for this edge detail rather than scattering them through the main field.

9. **Sparse satellites.** A blue burst has about twelve alternating long points and short valleys around one centre, without an outline or centre dot. A ring cluster has exactly six open circles in two rows of three; circle diameter is about `0.18m`, with a small even gap and a stroke around `0.025m`. Use black on white and white on blue. A small orange circle can straddle a blue cell edge; its portion over blue becomes a separately filled medium-blue segment. Small bar fragments may accompany it. These satellites punctuate the assembly; they do not become a uniform background pattern.

Let neighbouring cells make larger relationships: a shared arc can continue toward an edge; a stripe insert can terminate a curved block; triangle facets can support a flower or sprout. Avoid an evenly spaced catalogue of unrelated icons.

## Typography

Use supplied licensed files as study substitutes, not as identifications of the original fonts:

| Role | Material and treatment |
|---|---|
| Latin display | `pixel.woff2`, supplied authored surrogate, native cut. Use uppercase, heavy square strokes and visibly stepped diagonals. Keep letterspacing compact and word spaces clear. |
| CJK display | `cjk.woff2`, supplied CJK substitute at its actual provided cut. Seek a heavy, square silhouette, tightly grouped characters and restrained stair-step corners. The file's family identity and weight are not established by this inventory; do not claim it reproduces the reference lettering. |
| Body and small Latin | `sans-400.woff2` (Inter 400); use the supplied CJK font for Chinese. |
| Short secondary heading or identity line | `sans-700.woff2` (Inter 700), paired with the supplied CJK cut. |
| Prominent compact figures | `condensed.woff2` (Oswald family, supplied native cut); use only for functional numerical information. |

The display's defining feature is a dense rectangular letter mass with square pixel-like steps, rather than rounded techno lettering. For CJK display finishing, use one small square step unit consistently, around `1/16–1/24em`: stair-step selected diagonal or terminal contours without eroding counters or changing character recognition. Isolated tiny square accents can sit just outside the display block, like detached pixels. Do not apply this treatment to prose. A plain CJK substitute is an acknowledged fidelity limit; decorative squares alone do not reproduce the reference's letter construction.

Set the main display to the available clear span, maintaining its prominence before adding supporting text. Use compact lines, approximately `0.95–1.05` line-height, without horizontal compression. Break new copy by phrase. Two title lines are common evidence, not a fixed requirement. If more lines are needed, recompose the reading field and reduce the amount of ornament before reducing the title's rank.

For stacked bilingual titles, set the Latin capital height approximately `0.5–0.65` of the CJK character ink height. Keep the translation close enough to read as one group. On a wide side-by-side title, allow different line counts so the two blocks have comparable total heights. A single-language title takes the display role directly; do not preserve a blank translation slot.

Use body text around one quarter to one third of the principal display's ink height, with `1.4–1.6` line-height. Labels can be smaller but must remain readable at the actual output size. Keep long prose to a compact, quiet block; use ordinary sentence spacing. Short operational figures may be heavier and larger than body text while staying below the title. A barcode, if the new brief requires one, is functional data, not a decorative fill.

All listed fonts use SIL Open Font License 1.1. Carry their supplied licenses with redistributed files. The neutral inventory supplies versions for Inter, Oswald and the CJK cut; it does not identify the original reference faces. Do not use the available light sans merely to introduce another contrast.

## Colors

Use white and saturated medium blue as the two principal reading grounds. On white, use black for CJK display and prose; blue may carry the Latin display. On blue, reverse display, body, small identity and circle outlines to white. For a full blue field, reset every reading foreground accordingly.

Keep orange, red and yellow vigorous inside the geometry. Balance them with blush, pale blue and cream rather than lowering the saturation of the whole piece. Medium blue can separate a pale curve from the ground or supply a distinct segment in a circle. Black is mainly reading ink and the six flower dots, not a heavy outline around every tile. White may also be a cell ground. The grey surrounding the reference pieces is presentation-board ground, not an identity colour to reproduce.

Keep reading text at least 4.5:1 against its actual ground, or 3:1 for large display; never place reading text over the multicolour mosaic.

## Variations and Limits

Change the field from white to blue, enlarge one motif into a whole strip, isolate a complete flower, or move the assembly to a different combination of edges. Keep the same motif anatomy and type hierarchy through each change. The field may become very quiet for information-heavy work; retain a meaningful assembled edge rather than adding arbitrary small decoration to compensate.

Do not introduce wavy squiggles, random rotated shapes, checkerboards, universal black outlines, cartoon faces, or new flower species because they seem generically Memphis. Do not make the same thin border band serve every format. Preserve the contrast between an organized, tightly joined geometric region and the open reading field.

Before delivery, check that the title is the clear reading entry, the ornamental region has a deliberate connected silhouette, the two flower constructions remain distinct, and small stripes/rings have not collapsed at output size. Compare both in colour and greyscale: geometry and text grouping must still organize the piece.
