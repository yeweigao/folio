# Swiss Punk — the grid tested

**Invocation:** Wolfgang Weingart, Swiss Punk / New Wave typography — Make work as Weingart made the Kunsthalle posters: films layered and slipped on the light table, the baseline stepped, the screen enlarged until the process shows; never displacement that covers a fact.

Basel, 1968 to the 1980s: Weingart at the Basel School of Design, his students April Greiman and Dan Friedman, Willi Kunz, then *Emigre*. It is not a rejection of Swiss typography but a test of it from inside, by its own school. Spoken in Swiss, Swiss Punk is Swiss arguing with itself: the same grid and grotesque, displaced, stepped and layered until the system shows.

- **The grid is tested, not obeyed.** The order stays visible so its breaking reads; rotation, stepped baselines and displacement run through the piece as its grammar, never as one gesture.
- **Type is material, not only size.** Spacing, weight, width and direction change within a display line; registers collide inside one family.
- **Layers overprint off register; they do not sit apart.** Two or three inks of one form, slipped by a fraction of a stroke, read as one vibrating form. Never under reading text.
- **The process is the image, not a texture.** Film montage, an enlarged halftone that carries tone (built to its card in signature.md), early bitmap type.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Wolfgang Weingart — the Kunsthalle Basel posters and the *Typografische Monatsblätter* covers (1970s–).** Work in layers as on film. Build each layer separately (type, screen, rule), then offset, step and overprint them with declared values, and render to see the slip. Suits subjects in productive conflict with their own system. Never: displacement that covers a fact.
- **April Greiman — *Does it make sense?* (*Design Quarterly* 133, 1986).** Collage type, image and space on the computer at different depths, and keep the digital process visible. Suits technology and layered evidence. Never: depth faked with shadows.
- **Dan Friedman — the 1970s teaching exercises, later *Radical Modernism* (1994).** Keep the modernist grid and overload it, with legibility pushed to its edge and never past it. Suits manifestos and dense programmes. Never: quiet understatement.

**Tempering.** In documents of trust, a slip reads as a printing error. Temper in this order: the slip goes first, then rotation; the step and the float survive longest. State the amplitude in the skeleton.

**Swiss keeps:** the grotesque, the grid as the reference being tested, and body text, labels and data horizontal on it.

**Test:** The typographic relations themselves express the subject; remove one tilted word, and the dialect survives.

**Caricature:** Glitch filters, random distress, illegible stacks; a word echoed down the page; one rotated word on an otherwise Swiss page.
