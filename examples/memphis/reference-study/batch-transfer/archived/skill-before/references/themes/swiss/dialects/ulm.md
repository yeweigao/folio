# Ulm — the system decides

**Invocation:** 乌尔姆设计学院 HfG Ulm — Make work as Aicher made the Munich 1972 system: write the rules before drawing any case, colour only as a labelled code, pictograms on one modular grid that a stranger could extend; never a difference without a nameable reason.

The HfG Ulm, 1953–68: Max Bill, Otl Aicher, Tomás Maldonado, Hans Gugelot; then Braun, the Lufthansa identity (1962–63) and the Munich 1972 Olympics. Where Swiss designs the page, Ulm designs the method that makes every page: analysis before form, the system before the case. Spoken in Swiss, Ulm moves the decision from the page to the rules.

- **Rules before cases.** The design is the manual: grid, codes, type ramp and the treatment of each kind of message are written before the first unit, and every unit is one application another designer could extend.
- **Every difference has a reason.** One treatment per function; a change in size, colour or position names its reason, and taste is not one.
- **Colour and sign are codes, not accents.** A few hues, each mapped to one category and chosen for what the occasion should mean, decoded by labels or a legend and never the only cue; one family of solid pictograms on a modular grid at 0°, 45° and 90°.
- **Analysis before form; instruction as the voice.** Model the problem first (who uses it, for what task, at what decision), and let the layout follow the model; the words instruct in short, unambiguous lines.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **Otl Aicher — the Munich 1972 system.** Write the manual first: the colour code and its legend, the grid the pictograms are drawn on, the path the visitor walks. Then generate every case from it. The light Munich palette was chosen for the open games it had to signal. Suits events, places and instructions a stranger must use. Never: three kinds of arrow.
- **Max Bill — *Fifteen Variations on a Single Theme* (1935–38).** State one law, then produce every form as a systematic variation on it, one step per unit. Suits series and identities that must extend. Never: a shape whose rule cannot be stated.
- **Hans Gugelot — the M 125 system (1950s) and the Braun SK 4 (1956, with Dieter Rams).** Fix one module and make every part a whole multiple of it, so parts combine without fitting. Suits kits, catalogues and modular screens. Never: a dimension off the module.
- **Tomás Maldonado — the Ulm model.** Model the structure (users, tasks, decisions) before any surface, and let the layout follow the model. Suits complex information and data. Never: styling applied to an unstructured brief.

**Swiss keeps:** the grid, one neo-grotesque, flush-left setting, and the rule, now used as a code.

**Test:** A stranger knows what to do next, and a designer given only the rules could make a new case that fits.

**Caricature:** Sports pictograms and the Munich palette pasted onto an ordinary page; UI line icons; three kinds of arrow.
