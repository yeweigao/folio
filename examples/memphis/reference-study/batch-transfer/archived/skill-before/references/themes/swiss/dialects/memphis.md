# Memphis — the tiled field

**Invocation:** 孟菲斯 Memphis — Make work as Memphis graphics are made now, after Sottsass and Du Pasquier's laminates: a kit of flat primitives, some drawn from the subject's own forms, tiled whole across one square module in clashing hues, the type set as a block among the blocks; never a kit that would fit any subject, never pattern behind reading text.

Milan, 1981–88: Ettore Sottsass, Nathalie Du Pasquier, George Sowden, Peter Shire, with the Bacterio laminate and the Memphis textiles; then the graphic revival that tiles their colour and play onto Bauhaus and mid-century modular grids. Spoken in Swiss, Memphis keeps the module and refuses good taste.

- **Colour clashes on purpose, not one accent.** Four or five saturated hues with cream, white and ink, chosen as relations; a hue that names a category keeps it everywhere.
- **The field is tiled whole, not left white.** White and cream are tiles too; no cell is left over.
- **The kit is drawn for this subject, not borrowed.** Base primitives (quarter disc, half disc, disc, flower, star, cross, leaf, arch, checker, stripes) plus two to four forms abstracted from the subject onto the module, as Ikko Tanaka abstracted a motif. Pattern never carries information.
- **Type is a block among blocks.** Merged cells on one solid hue; one family at its extremes, a hairline line over black lines in one title.

**Hands in this school.** One governs; the others visit as single moves.
- **The tile system — Memphis graphics now (2010s–).** A synthesized register. Draw the kit first; lay one square module across the format; give each unit one field shape (an L, a band, a column, a corner) and tile it whole, one primitive per cell, never the same ground beside or above; merge cells for the type and the facts. Suits identities, series, decks, campaigns. Never: a kit that would fit any subject.
- **Ettore Sottsass and Nathalie Du Pasquier — Bacterio (1978), the Memphis textiles (1981–87).** Draw the pattern as a tileable laminate at full scale and clad a whole surface with it. Suits grounds and bands that must sing without depicting. Never: pattern at timid scale, or behind reading text.
- **Camille Walala — the painted façades and pattern environments (2010s–).** Divide a whole surface into large blocks, each a different pattern (stripes, checks, dots, zigzags) in primaries, pink, black and white, the words in one solid block. Suits environments, events, covers. Never: one pattern in two neighbouring blocks.
- **Peter Shire — the Bel Air chair (1982).** Build one volume from clashing parts and draw it in isometric projection, the type extruded on its faces. Suits launches, events, technology. Never: depth faked with shadow.

**Swiss keeps:** the square module, flush-left type, every fact live, reading text on solid ground, every contrast floor.

**Test:** Swap in another subject's content, and the kit no longer fits; remove the clash and the tiling, and the character goes.

**Caricature:** a generic flower-and-star kit on any subject; squiggle confetti; black outlines everywhere; an 80s pastel palette; Memphis as wallpaper behind a Swiss page.
