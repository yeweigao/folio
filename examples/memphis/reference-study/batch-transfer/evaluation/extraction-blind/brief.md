# Reference-to-DESIGN handoff

Inspect the supplied art-activity identity board and extract a standalone `DESIGN.md`. Treat it as a graphic Memphis reference without assuming that every graphic treatment shown is a universal Memphis rule.

The next maker receives only your file, new content and the licensed fonts you name. They do not receive the board, its individual pieces, your reasoning, visual components or reconstruction code. They must be able to make another work in the same visual world. Name an author only if the supplied material establishes one.

Write the shared construction, identity-bearing shapes and their internal relationships, type and colour roles, and the conditions under which the composition changes with format. Separate what belongs to the world from one piece's exact placement. Preserve necessary freedom; do not turn the observed arrangements into a fixed template. State the limits of the evidence.

Deliver one content-free `DESIGN.md`, with concrete enough rules and materials for that next maker. Do not make a poster. Do not copy the titles, dates, venue or instance coordinates into the system.

## Source material

The evaluation packet supplies the identity board as `input/reference.jpg` and a neutral inventory of available licensed fonts. All arms receive identical raw material and this brief. The world is the supplied set, not a generic library of style words.

## Comparison

Fresh writer contexts use no skill, the previous skill, or the candidate skill. Shuffle the outputs and judge them blind against the source and the brief: can another maker reconstruct the identity and recompose it without the image? Check executable relationships, conditional scope, source fidelity and unsupported assumptions. This compares extraction quality; it does not by itself prove rendered-output quality or general superiority.
