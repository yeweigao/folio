# Playful art identity · Memphis-derived modular graphics

Use this system for a connected family of cultural-event announcements, printed information, narrow admission pieces, bags and badges. It joins a stepped geometric border, a few distinctive flower and mechanical motifs, and compact pixel-built display lettering around an unusually clear reading field.

## Overview

Use the observed contemporary graphic tile method: construct a small reusable kit, join its parts into one connected edge composition, and give the words a solid, open field. Never replace that construction with scattered Memphis decoration.

No designer or original typeface is established by the source. “Memphis-derived” describes the graphic method, not verified authorship. The supplied board is the visual authority; a generic movement rule does not override its large white spaces, occasional centered type, or selective use of pattern.

Keep these relationships across the family:

- Cool blue anchors the identity; hot orange, yellow and red animate it; pink and pale blue provide lighter internal planes.
- Graphic parts touch, tile, overlap deliberately or share a baseline. The resulting assembly meets the trim and has a stepped silhouette facing the open field.
- Heavy, square-built display words contrast with small, ordinary reading text. English display uses capitals; the paired scripts feel like related blocks rather than the same font mechanically resized.
- The kit carries the identity even when only a short strip survives. The amount, location and orientation of that strip change with the object.

First select the content hierarchy and reading distance. Then choose a white or blue ground, reserve a clear title-and-information region, and build the graphic edge from the kit below. Choose one dominant flower, arc or geometric unit for the particular piece; supporting units remain smaller or partly cropped. Recompose existing modules before inventing a new motif. Content changes line breaks, density and the balance of solid field against mosaic; it does not require a literal illustration of each new subject.

## Images

Make the imagery as flat vector geometry with hard edges, circular curves and precise clipping. Use no photography, texture, lighting, soft shadow, bevel, dimensional extrusion or simulated print wear. Overlap is represented by a chosen flat color, not a general transparency effect.

The mosaic is usually trim-connected: a bottom band, side tower, corner assembly, or a dense end panel. Crop a circle or arc at its tile boundary; crop the whole assembly at the physical edge. Preserve the recognizable central structure of a flower when it serves as the lead motif. A small star, diamond or flower may detach into the open field as a single accent; do not distribute all motifs as confetti.

An existing maker mark may occupy a quiet margin in black on white or white on blue. Use a supplied mark rather than inventing a replacement. The board's presentation background and platform marks are not identity materials.

## Layout

**Grid system: modular graphics with merged reading zones.** Let the format be `W × H`, its short side be `S`, and the graphic square module be `u`. Derive all tile geometry from `u`; derive type from the width of its intended reading zone. These are working construction ranges inferred from the set, not measurements of an original specification.

For ordinary portrait, square and broad formats, start with `u ≈ S/6`, adjusting to obtain whole modules across the selected edge assembly. A practical range is five to seven modules per short side. Divide into half- and quarter-modules for strips, leaves, dots and connectors; merge into `2u` blocks for a large arc or disc. Do not apply this density unchanged to very narrow admission strips: use about two basic cells across their width and let a large arc span both.

Tiles have **no gutters, outlines or rounded tile containers**. Shared edges line up. Join at least several modules into a readable mass rather than placing identical isolated square stickers. The outer edge is flush with the trim; the inner edge can climb in whole or half-module steps. A curved cap may rise above the last square. Neighboring blocks may share blue when that makes a larger plane; alternate internal shapes and warm/cool relationships rather than enforcing a checkerboard of ground colors.

Reserve a solid reading region before drawing the border. Leave at least half a module of visible clearance between title ink and the mosaic, increasing it when the title is large. Within a paired title, separate the two scripts by roughly half a display cap height when stacked. Separate a title group from body copy or facts by at least one display cap height. Margins for small marks and essential facts begin around `0.25–0.4u`, increasing with reading distance and binding or handling needs. Measure these gaps between visible marks, not text boxes.

### Recompose by format

| Format or use | Construction |
|---|---|
| Broad announcement | Keep a large upper and central reading field. Spread a low mosaic across the lower edge and allow one or both ends to rise as unequal towers. The title may form two adjacent language blocks; a slim vertical blue rule can separate them. Keep the middle visually open even if the border reaches higher at the sides. |
| Square or normal portrait | Use a bottom band growing toward one side, often a taller right edge. The title occupies the upper field; supporting text sits in the remaining solid region. An isolated flower can stand just above the band. The full perimeter need not be framed. |
| Narrow admission strip | Let a two-column or large merged motif panel occupy roughly the upper two-fifths to half. Place the title, supporting text and practical information in a clear vertical panel below. Centering is appropriate here. A selective thin horizontal rule may separate reading copy from the facts and supplied machine-readable material. |
| Information-led horizontal card | Give most of the width to a small, aligned text column or several separated text groups. Compress the pattern into a narrow side stack; retain enough of an arc, flower and stripe to identify the family. |
| Bag or badge face | Respect handles, clips and seams. Either retain a clear upper title area over a dense lower band, or use a small top/side pattern and a larger isolated flower. Center the principal message when the object's vertical axis calls for it. Do not scale an entire poster onto the object. |

White and blue versions may use different arrangements. Do not simply invert a finished piece. Place long titles on more lines, enlarge the solid reading zone or reduce the mosaic's extent before reducing the title to the size of supporting copy.

## Typography

The display voice is a **heavy angular pixel construction**: broad square-ended strokes, right-angle or stepped outer corners, rectangular counters and occasional small square notches or detached punctuation. It has a tight, almost monospaced texture without becoming an unreadable pixel pattern. Keep Latin display capitals upright and tightly grouped; keep CJK display dense and substantial. Do not substitute a rounded toy face, thin geometric sans or widely tracked uppercase for this role.

Use the licensed `pixel.woff2` as the supplied authored display surrogate wherever its glyph coverage supports the content. Its identity is not the source's unidentified font. Use `cjk.woff2` for Chinese reading text and as the supplied CJK substitute where required. If a display glyph is absent from the surrogate, use the CJK substitute at matching apparent weight and disclose the substitution; do not claim the result reproduces the original lettering. Do not add random square particles to ordinary text to simulate a pixel font.

Use `sans-400.woff2` and `sans-700.woff2` for ordinary Latin copy, concise taglines and small labels. Use `condensed.woff2` for compact, prominent numeric facts when necessary. It is a substitute from the Oswald project, not an identification of the source's numerals. Do not use the available light face merely to create contrast: the reference's working contrast is chunky display against small, clear sans.

### Hierarchy and pairing

- **Primary title:** fit the shortest meaningful line grouping to approximately 65–90% of its reserved span. Start with apparent cap or ideographic height around `0.4–0.65u`; use a smaller end of the range on an information-led card. Keep line gaps compact, about 10–20% of the visible letter height. Use black or blue on white, white on blue.
- **English companion display:** use the same angular construction, usually two or three short centered lines beneath a CJK title. Start at 35–50% of the CJK title's apparent height. On a broad bilingual headline the languages may instead occupy adjacent blocks at comparable visual importance. Their actual language priority follows the brief; a translation is not required when none exists.
- **Tagline or secondary heading:** use bold ordinary sans, around `0.16–0.24u`, with its translation directly beneath at roughly 60–70% of that height. Keep it visually separate from the pixel title.
- **Reading copy:** start at `0.10–0.14u`, regular or medium sans, with leading 1.45–1.65. Give it a compact measure rather than stretching it across the field. Use a heavier cut only where physical reproduction needs it.
- **Practical facts:** make the key numeric or short factual line around `0.22–0.3u`, bold or condensed; subordinate details remain smaller ordinary sans. Keep real codes and supplied symbols accurate and unobscured.

These are relative starting sizes. Essential reading text must also meet the physical viewing-distance requirement; enlarge or reflow it when the object becomes small. Never shrink body text merely to retain a pattern block. Match CJK and Latin by visible density and baseline, not equal numerical font sizes. Keep reading text live; preserve actual font proportions. Do not stretch type to fill a rectangle.

## Shapes

Construct the following kit. Its identity depends on the **internal relationships**, not just the presence of circles, flowers and stripes.

| Unit | Construction and relationships |
|---|---|
| Cropped discs and ring halves | Place a large disc on a square edge so that a half or quarter remains visible. Nest a smaller differently colored disc to create a broad ring. Pair opposing blue/pale-blue and orange/blue halves across a shared vertical edge, with pink or cream occupying the exposed corners. Use approximately quarter-module ring thickness for the broad version. |
| Concentric quarter arcs | Use a single corner center outside or on the tile boundary. Draw three or four equally spaced quarter-circle tracks, with track width around `0.04–0.06u` and clear gaps of similar width. Use cream tracks on orange or orange tracks on pink. Keep the curves circular and concentric, not freehand rainbows. |
| Solid quarter-disc and curved cap | Fill a square with one quadrant against a contrasting flat ground. For the top of a side tower, let a tall orange rounded quadrant rise above the stack with one straight side continuing the tower's edge. |
| Four-lobed golden flower | Arrange four overlapping equal circular lobes in a compact two-by-two clover. The silhouette is softly squared with inward notches at the midpoints. Nest warmer orange lobes within the yellow outer lobes. Concentrate orange-red near the center, leaving a small cream cross-like opening with a red-orange center dot. Keep the flower stemless and bilaterally balanced; do not turn it into a daisy with radiating thin petals. |
| Red scalloped flower | Build a broad, roughly six-lobed red rosette with a yellow scalloped central disc. Place six black circular dots at equal angular intervals between the center and the lobe tips, one per lobe. The yellow center is about one-third of the overall diameter; black dots are much smaller, about one-eighth. The silhouette, center and dot ring share one center. Use no outline. |
| Diagonally split disc | Fit a large disc within a square light ground. Split it cleanly along one 45-degree diameter into blue and yellow, with a small orange disc centered on the division. Keep the small circle concentric with the large one. |
| Split boundary circle and short bar | Center a small disc on a vertical edge of a blue tile. Make its exposed half orange and its half over the blue plane middle blue. Nearby, use a short horizontal connector divided into two or three warm/cool rectangles; keep both attached to the same local grid. |
| Leaves and semicircular sprout | Join one large blue curved sector and two small pointed oval leaves at a common lower corner or baseline. One small leaf may be orange within the blue sector and the other blue on the light field. Use pointed ends and circular or elliptical sides, not a drawn stem. |
| Triangle chain | Join repeated right triangles and shallow isosceles triangles along a continuous lower baseline. Alternate blue, middle blue and pale blue to read as a low folded ridge. A short row of upright orange right triangles can rise above it. Use this as connective tissue, not a separate mountain illustration. |
| Stripes | Fill a shallow rectangular band with alternating vertical orange and yellow bars of equal width. At a side termination, a much narrower blue/middle-blue/pink stripe bundle can run vertically through several stacked units. Keep stripes flush with neighboring tiles. |
| Ring-dot accent | Set six small open circles as two rows of three, with equal diameters and gaps. Use black outlines on white or white outlines on blue. Keep the circles empty; this is a small ordered accent, not a dotted wallpaper. |
| Detached star or diamond | Use one compact blue many-pointed star with sharp triangular rays, or one small diamond split horizontally into a blue upper triangle and pale-blue lower triangle. Let one float near the graphic band when the field needs a single counterweight. |

Rotate or mirror the arc, disc and triangle units in right-angle increments. Change their colors within the established roles. Keep the flowers' central construction intact, even when their scale changes. Not every piece needs every motif. Prioritize a few legible relationships over a miniature catalogue of the kit.

## Forms

- **Announcement or cover:** lead with the pixel title, retain one clear language grouping, and use the mosaic as its edge counterweight; never bury the title in pattern.
- **Information card or reference:** lead with aligned reading groups on a solid ground and reduce the kit to an identifying strip; never give decoration the space required by facts.
- **Admission or credential:** establish a repeated vertical rhythm of motif, identity and essential information, with supplied codes kept clear; never use a decorative barcode in place of a real one.
- **Object face:** anchor the composition to the object's actual usable face and handling zones; never treat a handle, clip or seam as available reading area.
- **Other forms:** retain the typography, palette and edge grammar, but establish a new content structure appropriate to the task. The source supplies no evidence for a chart language, interactive behavior or multi-page editorial system.

## Variations

The principal variations are **white open field / blue open field**, **dense motif panel / low connecting band**, and **large lead motif / small cropped motif vocabulary**. Choose according to format and information density. These can coexist across a family; there is no fixed tile order or mandatory corner.

A large white piece may have very little pattern above its lower edge. A narrow piece may put pattern across its whole upper panel. Both belong if the same kit, hard joins, type texture and color relationships remain visible. Do not force the entire surface to be tiled, and do not add a thin decorative frame merely to imply completeness.

## Do's and Don'ts

Check the silhouette first at thumbnail size: read the title against its quiet field and see the mosaic as one connected shape. At reading size, check the flower anatomy, equal arc spacing, clean clipping, aligned stripe ends and clear gaps between visible text and geometry. Confirm all required facts are present and essential text is readable on its actual ground; small text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast and large text at least 3:1.

Avoid generic squiggles, random crosses, black outlines around every tile, all-over pastel wallpaper, gradients, pill-shaped text containers, floating cards and even sprinklings of the complete kit. Do not replace the angular display with an ordinary bold sans and assume the palette will preserve the identity. Do not mistake exact placement in one piece for a universal rule.
