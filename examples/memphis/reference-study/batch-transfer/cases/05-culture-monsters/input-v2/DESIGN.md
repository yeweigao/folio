# Culture Monsters · flat characters and coarse modules

A system for culture programs in which a flat creature/pattern collage threads through heavy titles and a red information field.

> Use alone: read Overview and Images, then construct Layout from W × H. S = 1000 is the reference short side; scale token values by the actual S ÷ 1000. Compute sizes derived from a module in that module. Recompose longer content before changing a reading size. One unit has one lead; use the Forms and final visual checks below.

## Overview
Memphis-inflected character collage, observed hand — Draw blunt, flat creatures from a small body/eye/dot vocabulary and interlock them with coarse solid patterns; never substitute expressive cartoon portraits or fine decorative texture. Authorship and original typefaces are unverified.
World: Keep the red field, paper title crossing, black heavy type, unequal modules and distinct character/pattern roles across cultural announcements and programs. Each piece supplies its own theme, facts, character poses and module joins. For unfamiliar content, distinguish its participants or actions through contrasting body silhouettes, without assigning invented meaning to a colour or face.
Method: Rank the text and reserve its reading rectangles. Draw a small family of visibly different silhouettes; choose one large single-eye body and smaller companion bodies rather than making equal portraits. Add coarse non-character pattern modules, then weave a reduced module run beside the lower information. Keep the broad title crossing and all required facts complete.
Amplitude: Use large, saturated solids and abrupt size jumps. Quiet turns keep fewer modules and fewer characters; they retain broad shapes and the lower continuation.
Anti-reference: Three equally sized cartoon mascots over a plain text footer.

## Images
Construct flat vector characters, solid patterns and live type. No gradient, rendered volume, highlight or photographic insert is part of this system. Character modules can bleed at trim or be clipped by their own module edge; preserve at least one complete eye and enough body contour to recognize the type. Pattern modules fill their tile without a little inset frame. Uniformly scale each drawing; change its crop or redraw a pose instead of distorting it. Credit any commissioned character work in the piece's brief.

## Layout
Grid system: Modular — start with u = S/4, then combine half-, single- and double-u spans into an unequal tessellation. Align adjoining edges without gutters or universal black borders. Use at least three module scales across the unit; a large body should outweigh the smallest pattern tile by roughly 2–4 times in width. Do not divide the collage into equal vertical character columns.
Reserve a broad, shallow paper title crossing within the red field, sized to the actual title lines. The collage must continue beyond this crossing: place a narrower run of at least one character and one pattern beside the lower reading field, reaching toward a trim edge. Either side, a stepped return or an offset inset is allowed; do not fix the run to the source's side or row coordinates. In landscape, extend the modules sideways; in portrait, stack more turns; in square, interlock both directions. Keep the title crossing and lower run legible as one field.
Give text a visible-ink inset of `inset` from module edges. Separate paired language lines by at least 0.15 of the larger line height, labels from their facts by half a label line height, and unrelated text groups by one body line height. Enlarge or split the red reading region before shrinking type. Do not let a module cut a title, date or other required string.

## Typography
Use Study Sans 900 with Study CJK 900 for `lead`, `secondary` and `facts`; use the same aliases at 700 for `label`, and 400 for `body`. The licensed Inter/Noto Sans SC study substitutions are recorded in [fonts.json](../../assets/fonts.json); original faces are unverified. Embed fresh glyph subsets for new content.
Heavy, ordinary-width text is a solid block in the collage. Give only one title the `lead` level; the other name/theme uses `secondary`, with both larger than the facts and explanation. Keep a translated title in a deliberate separate line, matching its role rather than its character count. Use the same family and weight for figures as for their text role. The `body` token is the smallest reading size. Break long titles semantically or allocate a wider crossing; never condense a normal-width face or shrink a bilingual line into a caption solely to retain white space.

## Colors
`red` is the principal information field and also a possible creature/pattern fill. `paper` cuts a clear title crossing through that field and supplies circular eye whites/dots. `ink` carries heavy reading type, pupils, dot mouths and occasional dark bodies. `blue` gives broad cool bodies or module grounds; `pink` and `yellow` supply contrasting bodies, lobes and coarse patterns. Use the bright colours in substantial solids, not as fine multicolour linework. Adjacent modules change hue without implying data categories.
Set reading text in ink on paper, red, pink or yellow; use paper on ink or blue when a short reading block is reversed. Only these pairs are allowed. Pattern and body adjacency is a drawing relation, never a substitute for readable text contrast.

## Shapes
Make body silhouettes from two or three swollen stacked lobes, a blunt round/octopus head with a few broad legs, or a soft scalloped block. Limbs are solid extensions about 0.08–0.18 of body width, with no fingers or joint detail. Keep these families distinct: a tall single-eye body, a squat paired-eye body and a scalloped or tentacled body cannot all become rounded disks.
For body width B, a single circular eye has diameter 0.15–0.50 B; paired circular eyes each have diameter 0.12–0.25 B. Use the larger single eye on the large lobed body and smaller eyes on compact companions. A black circular pupil is 0.45–0.65 of its white eye diameter. Mouths are absent, one small dot or one/two short rows of equal dots, each about 0.035–0.065 B wide; the row stays within about 0.60 B. No tilted oval eyes, glints, eyebrows, crescent smiles, enclosing open-mouth cavity or rows of large teeth.
Pattern modules use broad elliptical petals, a ring of 6–10 solid discs, 6–10 short blunt radial bars, repeated deep scallops, or 6–8 solid triangular cuts. Relative to tile width M, a disc diameter is roughly 0.12–0.20 M and a radial bar thickness roughly 0.05–0.09 M. Leave visible ground between members. A coarse burst may alternate long and short points, but never becomes a fan of hairlines. No fine hatching, dense stripes, isometric cubes, shaded edges or wireframes. Patterns have no eyes; a face belongs to a body silhouette.

## Forms
Cover: weave a large creature/pattern group, paper title crossing and lower fact field into one tessellation; no isolated top illustration panel.
Statement / Opener / Close: use one large flat body or coarse pattern against one heavy line, retaining a small lower return; no extra competing headline.
Exhibit: give one creature or subject a dominant module and its label a clean adjoining field; no competing character portraits.
Collection / Reference: use stable text alignment and peer labels within unequal visual modules; no invented ranking.
Argument / Comparison: put claims, shared scales and direct evidence labels on clean paper/red fields, with pattern confined to separate modules; no data encoded by decorative faces alone.
Sequence / Exercise: let a module change mark each action or task in reading order and reserve clear answer space; no decorative crossing through required content.

## Variations
Swap the location and span of the paper crossing, change character poses and crop, and change the coarse pattern selection. Keep unequal scale, the flat face proportions and a module run below the crossing. A dense turn may add small modules around one large body; a quiet turn removes modules without replacing them with detailed expression or texture.

## Do's and Don'ts
At thumbnail size, see unequal colour modules and heavy text, with a small module rhythm still visible beside the lower facts. In grayscale, the paper crossing and black type must separate from the creature/pattern field. At reading size, check circular eyes, small dot mouths, broad limbs and coarse pattern gaps. Required strings must survive module crops and language reflow.
Caricature: smiling mascots, a fine-line sunburst and a decorative cube above a continuous text slab. Preserve new poses and new module arrangements; reject changes to the flat character anatomy or pattern scale.
