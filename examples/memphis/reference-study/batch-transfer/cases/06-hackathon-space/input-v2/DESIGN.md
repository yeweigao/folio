# Technical Space · windows and projected objects

A system for technical gatherings that stages readable window echoes among clashing flat-colour solids on an electric-blue drawing field.

> Use alone: read Overview and Images, then construct Layout from W × H. S = 1000 is the reference short side; scale token values by the actual S ÷ 1000. Compute sizes derived from an object in that object. Recompose longer content before changing a reading size. One unit has one lead; use the Forms and final visual checks below.

## Overview
Memphis-inflected digital-space study, observed hand — Build one depth field from repeated window edges, projected solid faces and flat cut shapes; never reduce it to a software mockup of stacked rectangles. Authorship and original typefaces are unverified.
World: Keep the electric-blue/light-grid ground, blue outline/type, paper title face, narrow heavy display and fluorescent solids across technical programs. Each piece supplies its own title, locations, dates or other groupings, object arrangement and depth directions. Convert unfamiliar content into front-facing labels and labelled object faces in the same spatial construction, without inventing event facts.
Method: Group the information and draw its paper title window first. Echo its frame into depth, then interlock that stack with a projected block or cylinder and a cut disc, fold or burst. Assign smaller fact groups to complete faces/panes. Vary object directions and overlap to make a scene; resolve visibility before typesetting, then add the background grid.
Amplitude: Keep hard colour clashes and visible front/top/side differences. A quiet turn reduces the object count, retaining a window echo, a genuinely projected solid and a cut shape.
Anti-reference: A large coloured application window and two smaller application windows on a pale dashboard background.

## Images
Construct native vector planes, ellipses, outlines and live type. Use solid face colours without gradients, lighting or cast shadows. The grid meets trim; an object may bleed at trim, provided its distinguishing contour and every required label survive. Preserve circular/elliptical proportions under uniform scaling; a projected face is constructed with shared vertices, not made by stretching a finished rectangle. Text attached to a face follows that face's single affine projection. Keep front-pane text undistorted. Credit any separately supplied logo in the piece brief.

## Layout
Grid system: Hierarchical — use a square drawing grid with pitch S/18 and the `gridLine` stroke beneath one interlocked object field. Grid lines are secondary; they are neither reading columns nor a reason to distribute objects evenly. The principal title face spans about 0.55–0.75 S, medium solids about 0.20–0.40 S, and small labels/cut shapes about 0.08–0.20 S. Fit these scales to W × H by changing the field's arrangement, not by enlarging one empty window to fill the page.
Include both window echoes and projected geometry in every scene: at least one substantial closed block/cylinder with a visible top and side, and one cut disc/fold/burst, alongside the title stack. At least two overlaps must connect different object families. A small icon inside a window does not satisfy the projected-solid role. Keep a connected passage of blue grid visible around/between the objects so the ground remains a dark field supporting bright objects.
Echo a window in steps about S/100–S/45, using fine blue outlines and mostly paper copies; expose several readable frame edges. Stack echoes may retreat in different directions. A title stack may lead, or a larger connected solid may carry the scene, but both constructions remain present. In landscape, spread the interlock sideways; in portrait, extend its depth route vertically; in square, turn it around a common overlap. No object, city list or date pane has a prescribed source position.
Separate front-face visible text from edges by at least `inset`, paired language lines by at least 0.15 of the larger line height, and unrelated groups by one body line height. Do not put text on a face too narrow for these clearances. Enlarge/recompose the face or move a fact group to another pane before shrinking reading type. Rear copies may be hidden; front labels may not. Leave unused blue space as a deliberate depth passage, not a place for filler controls or invented slogans.

## Typography
Use Study Condensed 700 for `display` and `facts`, paired with Study CJK 700 for CJK content; use Study Sans 700 for `label` and 400 for `body`, also paired with Study CJK. The licensed Oswald/Inter/Noto Sans SC study aliases are recorded in [fonts.json](../../assets/fonts.json); original faces are unverified. Embed fresh glyph subsets for new content.
Set the title in tall, narrow, heavy upright lines on the paper front pane. Fact stacks are a clearly smaller level in the same narrow face; explanation and supplied maker marks stay subordinate in ordinary-width sans serif. Keep figure shapes and weight consistent with their role. The `body` token is the smallest reading size. Break long titles semantically and rebuild the face to fit; never compress glyphs. Only text on a projected face is transformed with its plane, with a minimum apparent reading height equal to the body role.

## Colors
`blue` is the full ground and the ink for outlines and text on paper, green and yellow. `grid` is a lighter blue-violet fine line over the blue ground, never a pale ground replacing it. `paper` is the principal title pane and most echo faces, carrying blue text; the largest reading window must not become a violet slab.
`green` gives broad connected planes or a disc; `yellow` gives contrasting top faces, folds or a smaller reading face. `pink` and `coral` give curved bodies or opposing planes. `violet` gives a receding solid face or a secondary label face with paper text. Use at least three fluorescent object colours in a full scene, but choose their assignment by adjacent-face separation, not random tinting of every window copy. Pink/coral faces are graphic surfaces, not reading grounds. Keep only the text/ground combinations listed in `pairs`.

## Shapes
Use crisp blue outlines at the `outline` width. A block has shared front/top/side vertices and depth about 0.15–0.35 of its front-face width; parallel edges share their direction within that object. A cylinder has a visible elliptical top about 0.20–0.40 of its diameter in height, with straight sides meeting the ellipse tangents. Build at least one substantial projected solid, not a frame pretending to be volume.
Counter the rectangles with a large flat disc/partial circle, a pointed burst or a folded right-angle connector. Give a burst roughly 8–12 unequal long/short points and a diameter about 0.15–0.30 S; a larger disc may sit partly behind a stack. Keep fold seams and cylinder rims continuous at overlaps. Different objects can turn in opposing oblique directions, while each individual object keeps coherent face joins. The clash comes from their orientations and fluorescent planes, not broken perspective within a block.
Window fronts have square corners, a thin header strip and, when useful, a few small neutral control marks. Controls are incidental, never the primary motif. Use repeated outlines for depth, not drop shadows, bevels, rounded cards or transparent glass. Draw rear planes and copies first, then front objects and labels.

## Forms
Cover: make the paper title stack the reading lead inside an interlocked solid/cut-shape scene; no window-only mockup.
Statement / Opener / Close: reduce to one short paper-face message and a compact echo/solid/cut-shape interlock; no additional headline competition.
Exhibit: enlarge one identifiable constructed object and attach a complete label face; no decorative miniatures around an unrelated image.
Collection / Reference: place peer facts on ordered complete faces or panes with stable type steps; no arbitrary depth used as ranking.
Argument / Comparison: keep evidence on front-facing paper panes with common scales and direct labels; reserve projection for surrounding structure, not distorted charts.
Sequence / Exercise: make each action or task a clearly ordered pane/face and keep answer space clear; no occluded steps.

## Variations
Window-led and solid-led scenes change which construction is largest, not whether the other exists. Change echo direction, face orientation, object locations and cut-shape choice for new content. Dense turns add medium objects; quiet turns remove extras while keeping the blue ground, paper title face, mixed geometry and readable front labels.

## Do's and Don'ts
At thumbnail size, see a dark blue field lifting several bright objects, with the paper title pane leading reading. In grayscale, distinguish the dark ground from the light title/echo faces and identify at least one solid's top/side joins. Check the scene for both repeated frame edges and a substantial non-rectangular/projected silhouette. At reading size, verify all front text, perspective joins and clearances; cropped back copies are intentional, cropped facts are not.
Caricature: neon-coloured rectangular windows with no cylinder, folded plane or solid face, especially on a pale grid. New spatial arrangements are welcome; replacing the shared depth construction with a flat interface is not.
