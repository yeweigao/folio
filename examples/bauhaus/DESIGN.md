# Bauhaus, Joost Schmidt · the constructed figure

A figure of the content built from point, line and plane (circle, line, square and their kin) on one module, in red, blue, yellow and black, the planes overlapping so the inks mix, with every line of type hung from the same construction.

> Use alone: read Overview and Images before any value; build the grid from your format by Layout. Tokens are at S = 1000 (S, the format's short side) and scale by S ÷ 1000, except a size the file derives from the format or a span, which is computed, never scaled. Content in one language takes that language's sizes. A unit carries one form, or a lead form with a quiet one beneath. Check the piece against Do's and Don'ts.

## Overview

Bauhaus, Joost Schmidt — the 1923 Bauhaus exhibition poster: draw the content's figure with compass and square from point, line and plane on one module, in red, blue, yellow and black, the planes overlapping so the inks mix, then set every line of type into the same construction; never shapes that build nothing.

World: any content, on any surface. Its leading parts (three at most) become shapes, its order or measure becomes the black thread they hang from, their weights set sizes on the doubling scale. Every piece keeps the module, the primaries, the thread, the faces and the construction; each brings its parts, its order and its figure.

Method:
1. Find the parts, their order, and the axis that orders them (time, a process, a rank, a quantity).
2. Choose the direction by the content, after Kandinsky: verticals and horizontals while it rests; one diagonal family when it moves, rises or clashes (a slope, a launch, a contest).
3. Lay the axis as one black thread; hang each part from it by a drop at its position.
4. Draw each part from the elements (circle, square, line, then triangle, bar, half and quarter circle), sized by weight on the doubling scale.
5. Overlap two planes only where two parts truly share something; the overlap shows their mixture as a transparency, and names what they share. Hang the type from the same drops.

A fourth term (time, sound, a sequence) takes the line, never a fourth hue. Kandinsky visits once: form and colour go to parts by place in the order, never by meaning. Malevich and Lissitzky visit with floating planes (Variations).

Amplitude: full: three primaries and black, flat, type at graphic scale.

Anti-reference: the triad recited as pattern; pastel or teal primaries; shapes off the module; the subject's literal pictogram.

## Images

Computed: the figure drawn from the module. Moholy-Nagy may visit once: one real photograph in black and white, cut to a rectangle or circle on the module, in tension with a bar. Never an illustration.

## Layout

Grid system: Modular — one square module; every edge on its lines.

S = min(W, H); margin S ÷ 20; twelve columns without gutters, sixteen where W > 1.4 H; module = column width; rows = whole modules that fit. The thread lies on a row line about a third down (at mid-height in landscape); the shapes hang from the row three below it; the masthead sits above, the ledger below, one row per fact. Sizes double (1, 2, 4, 8 modules); every edge, centre and drop lands on a module line. On a diagonal unit, the grid, the thread and the shapes turn together to one diagonal family; reading text stays horizontal and the title stays whole inside the trim. Rows nobody needs gather into one band of ink at the foot, never between the parts of the figure. A fact drawn true — a length, a count, a proportion the brief states — outranks the doubling scale: when the true size cannot fit at the scale a form prescribes, the scale yields to the fact, and the change is recorded; a fact is never redrawn to fit a scale.

## Typography

- **League Spartan** (OFL; 500, 700), whose round letters echo the circle; **Noto Sans SC** (500, 700) for Chinese.
- Display at a cap height of two modules, broken by meaning into lines that fit, never tracked or stretched; the Chinese display at the same ink height.
- Title 36 / 700, name 36 / 500, body and label 26; Chinese body 24. Nothing read smaller.
- Two weights, lining figures; no italic, outline or letterspacing. Latin may run lowercase, after Bayer.
- **Constructed title or numerals** (Albers): each glyph three cells wide and five tall on a sub-grid of half a module, strokes one cell, counters left open, one element per cell; colour by element (squares ink, quarter circles red, triangles blue, circles yellow, keyed), never by row, which reads as a flag. The same words also stand as live text.

## Colors

- **Ink `#111111` (primary).** Thread, drops, overlaps, rules, the foot band, everything read.
- **Muted `#4A4A46` (secondary).** Secondary text.
- **Ground `#F4F4F0` (neutral).** Paper white, over half the area.
- **Red `#D7281E`, blue `#1D3E9E`, yellow `#F3B900`.** First, second and last part, in that order; never type. Yellow carries an ink keyline where it meets the ground.
- **Line `#8A8A83`.** The grid, where it is shown.
- **Mixes: orange `#E8631A`, green `#2E8B57`, violet `#6A2C8E`.** An overlap shows the two colours' mixture as a transparency, made as Albers made it: the third colour chosen between the two, never computed by multiply, which only darkens a primary. Red and yellow give orange, yellow and blue green, red and blue violet; black stays black. A mix appears only where two parts share something, never as a flat fill, and no text sits on one.
- Primaries mark place in the order, never value or emphasis.

## Shapes

Flat, hard-edged, zero radius. The kit: square; circle (at least four modules across); bar (one module thick, four long); right triangle; half and quarter circle. Parts take square, circle and bar in order; a fourth restarts at the square, one doubling larger; triangles and part circles build the figure's details. Each shape's left edge sits on its part's column line. Thread 6 ink on the unit's one axis (0° and 90°, or the diagonal on a diagonal unit), each drop ending on a corner or the circle's leftmost point. Keyline 3 ink. Ledger rules 2 ink to open, 1 between rows.

## Forms

- **Cover:** masthead, the figure, the ledger.
- **Statement:** one sentence at title size hung from the thread, one shape as its counterweight.
- **Exhibit:** one shape at eight modules, or one photograph cut on the module, its label on a drop.
- **Data:** values as shapes of one kind along the thread; the doubling scale shows magnitude, the ledger the exact figure.
- **Sequence and comparison:** the thread in order, one drop per step; two figures on one thread at one scale.
- **Reference and close:** the ledger; the next action on the foot band.

## Variations

- **Vertical Chinese.** The Chinese display in a column two modules wide; once per series.
- **Typophoto** (Moholy-Nagy). One photograph in diagonal tension with one bar; once per deliverable.
- **Floating planes** (Malevich, Lissitzky). On a diagonal unit, the planes float along the diagonal on an open ground and one wedge may cut one circle; once per series.

## Do's and Don'ts

- **Look before delivering:** render it, see it full size and as a thumbnail. A shape off the module, a second diagonal family, type on a primary or a mix, a mix used as a flat fill, an overlap for prettiness, a diagonal on content at rest, a figure that would fit another subject: faults.
- **Floors:** facts verbatim and live; every pair in Tokens holds its contrast.
- **Test:** remove the type, and the shapes still build this content's figure, not any content's; every overlap names what two parts share; the direction matches whether the content rests or moves.
- **Caricature:** the Kandinsky triad on any subject; primaries floating on a ground, building nothing; every shape at its own angle; a borrowed "Bauhaus" display face; fake halftone or grain; gradients, shadows, rounded cards.
